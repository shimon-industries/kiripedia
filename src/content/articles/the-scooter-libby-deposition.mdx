**The Scooter Libby deposition** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of appearing as a witness in the prosecution arising from the exposure of [Valerie Plame](/wiki/valerie-plame), and of the moment the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s own lawyer cut him off. He tells it to establish how far outside normal practice a public presidential announcement of a [covert action programme](/wiki/covert-action-program) sits.

## The room

*"I was a witness in the Scooter Libby trial in 2006 or 2007, I guess it was, and I had to go before the special prosecutor — who ironically prosecuted me a couple of years later."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="42:16" />

The room was crowded with counsel. *"So I'm meeting with the special prosecutor, and the CIA general counsel's office is represented, and Scooter Libby's attorneys are there, and the Justice Department has its people there."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="42:16" />

## The question and the interruption

*"And he asked me a question: did I ever see these two senior CIA people talking to each other? And I said, well, they're not friends, if that's what you're getting at. I mean, the only conversation I ever saw them have was about a covert action program."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="42:49" />

The reaction was immediate. *"And the CIA general counsel jumps up and he's like, 'Stop.' And I said, 'I wasn't going to say anything about covert action.' He's like, 'You can't even say the words covert action. We don't even acknowledge that there is such a thing as covert action.' And I said, 'Okay, sorry.'"*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="42:49" /><Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="43:19" />

He repeated the constraint in a second telling a month earlier: *"when I was being called to testify in the Scooter Libby case, I was told that I wasn't even allowed to say the words. Covert action. Wasn't even allowed to say the words, let alone to talk about a program."*<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="19:44" />

## The comparison he draws

The anecdote exists in his telling only as a measuring stick. Against it he sets a sitting president announcing such a programme from a podium: *"now the president's going on national television to talk about the covert action program that he just approved at, you know, six levels above top secret."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="43:19" />

In the October version he made the same contrast in the language of career experience rather than protocol: *"here the president of the United States is publicly announcing a covert action program. I never saw anything like it in my life."*<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:15" />

The detail Kiriakou marks as ironic — that the prosecutor who took his testimony in the Libby matter would later prosecute him — he states once and does not develop.
