**Amir** is the pseudonym [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) uses for the colleague who worked beside him through the operation that captured [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) in 2002. Amir was Muslim and an ethnic Arab, a domestic officer on temporary duty to Pakistan who had never served overseas before — and who was, Kiriakou says, thrilled to be part of it and *"very much into the spirit of things."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="05:21" />

## The tea

That enthusiasm had costs. Halfway along the road between Lahore and [Faisalabad](/wiki/faisalabad), where the motorway turns into a dirt track, tea sellers pitch tents and travellers stop. Amir always got tea from them. Once he brought Kiriakou one with milk in it, and Kiriakou refused it — *"do you have any idea how long that milk has been sitting out?"* Amir drank his; Kiriakou threw his away. Amir had diarrhoea for the next six weeks, through the entire operation.<Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="05:21" /> Kiriakou's own view of the matter is that being ill in Pakistan is simply part of the job: on many days you are expected to work fourteen, sixteen, eighteen hours with a raging fever, and you should be prepared for it.<Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="05:57" />

## Driving into India

On their first drive to Faisalabad, Amir insisted on using one of the new handheld GPS units — devices the size of a large walkie-talkie. Kiriakou kept saying they were heading east; Amir insisted they were heading west. Then the unit announced: *approaching international border*. They had driven into India, and turned around under the gaze of Indian border guards coming out to see who was arriving down the dirt track — adding an hour to the trip.<Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="06:30" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="07:00" />

## Textile barons

Amir's most consequential improvisation came when the estate agent selling the [Lahore safe house](/wiki/lahore-safe-house) asked what the cash buyers did for a living. Exhausted and without a cover story, Amir said: *"we're textile barons."* Kiriakou agreed that yes, they were textile barons, and the agent — assuming they owned the factories outside town — blessed them for employing everyone in that part of the country.<Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="18:01" />

It was Amir who drove with Kiriakou through Lahore twice in the small hours before the raids, rehearsing the [route by landmark](/wiki/pakistan-without-maps) — shoe advertisement, traffic policeman, British school, McDonald's — because the city had no street signs.<Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="20:07" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="21:46" /> On the night itself the two of them watched from the roof of the Faisalabad safe house, and it was to Amir that Kiriakou gave his estimate beforehand: *"I think we're going to catch somebody. I don't know if we're going to catch him. I'm going to call it 50-50."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="31:50" />
