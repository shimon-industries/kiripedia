**Trump's Supreme Court appointees** are the subject of a judgement [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) interrupted himself to offer on 25 February 2026, in the middle of an argument about [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) publicly announcing he would ignore the Supreme Court.

*"You know, interestingly, I'm sorry to interrupt you. Interestingly, the Trump appointees to the Supreme Court have been pretty good."*<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="09:00" />

Pressed, he strengthened rather than softened it: *"They've been surprisingly good."*<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="09:00" />

## Where he puts the fault

The second half of the assessment redirects the blame away from the president he had spent the previous ten minutes criticising. *"It's these dinosaurs like Alito and Thomas appointed by the moderate presidents that are just disasters."*<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="09:00" />

[Ted Rall](/wiki/ted-rall) treated it as a genuine surprise, thanking him for the observation by name — which is what fixes the remark as Kiriakou's rather than his own.<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="09:00" /><Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="09:30" />

## The Kavanaugh qualification

Later in the same broadcast a viewer called Brett Kavanaugh a menace. Kiriakou did not defend him on the merits so much as on the ranking: *"I'm not saying that I would want the guy to be my, you know, housemate or anything. I'm just saying he's not Clarence Thomas or Sam Alito."*<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="33:50" />

The position is consistent across both remarks — that the court's worst members predate the current presidency, and that the appointments most people expected to be the problem have not been.
