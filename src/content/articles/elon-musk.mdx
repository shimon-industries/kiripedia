**Elon Musk** is the entrepreneur whom [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) recounts meeting by chance in early 2009 at the Hay-Adams Hotel bar in Washington, D.C., after an introduction from a mutual friend — at a time when Musk was worth about $1 billion following the sale of PayPal.<Cite s="2025-03-22-break-it-down-ukraine-foreign-policy-intelligence" t="23:30" /><Cite s="2026-02-09-epic-real-estate" t="47:08" /><Cite s="2024-09-03-george-peyrouton" t="52:52" /> Kiriakou's recurring account of the meeting has Musk, not yet famous or recognized by him, talking at length that night about selling PayPal for roughly a billion dollars and using the proceeds to buy Tesla, SpaceX and Starlink, with plans to colonize Mars — all before Kiriakou ever got to pitch his own TV show idea to the mutual friend they were there to meet.<Cite s="2026-02-09-epic-real-estate" t="47:08" /><Cite s="2024-09-03-george-peyrouton" t="52:52" /> Kiriakou now calls Musk arguably the most powerful man in the world, citing NASA needing his help to rescue stranded astronauts.<Cite s="2024-09-03-george-peyrouton" t="1:00:15" /> Kiriakou uses the 2009 memory to question how Musk's net worth grew from $1 billion in 2009 to $446 billion by 2024-2025, arguing the gain came "on the backs of the American people."<Cite s="2025-03-22-break-it-down-ukraine-foreign-policy-intelligence" t="26:06" /> He also cites a Wall Street Journal report that Musk lost $40 billion in wealth after [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) first announced tariffs, with Jeff Bezos separately losing about $20 billion amid the same stock market drop.<Cite s="2025-03-22-break-it-down-ukraine-foreign-policy-intelligence" t="28:14" />

## The Twitter Files database

Kiriakou says that after buying Twitter, Musk gave three journalists — Matt Taibbi, Michael Shellenberger, and a third — access to a standalone, searchable database containing every internal Twitter email exchanged from the company's founding until his purchase, rather than curated files.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="04:51" /> As of roughly two weeks before that interview, the journalists had gone through only about 10% of the documents in the database.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="05:55" />

## The algorithm and the forced follow

Kiriakou's objection to Musk's stewardship of the platform is that its ranking is not neutral. He describes the algorithms as manoeuvred *"to only allow a certain segment of people that he likes, and that say the things he wants, to get seen on X"*, and offers his own account as the illustration: he does not manage the account himself — a friend does — but when he looks at it he is *"bombarded with tweets from Elon Musk. I don't follow Elon Musk … and every time I try to unfollow him, I'm denied."*<Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="48:32" /> He cites the experience as part of his case for a subscription-funded, open-source alternative — see [Panquake](/wiki/panquake) — while applying the same free-speech consistency test to both: a platform that means what it says has to host people it dislikes.<Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="47:30" /><Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="49:04" />

## DOGE

Kiriakou has predicted Musk will "have to be reined in" over DOGE's publishing of alleged wasteful government spending.<Cite s="2025-05-05-austin-and-matt" t="13:42" />

## The government efficiency drive, assessed

Interviewed by [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) in 2026, the economist [Richard D. Wolff](/wiki/richard-wolff) rejected the premise of the department of government efficiency outright: *"I'm a professor of economics. I teach what efficiency is. This has nothing remotely to do with efficiency."*<Cite s="2026-07-07-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-prof-richard-d-wolff" t="8:25" /> His argument was that the federal civilian workforce is the smaller share of American government employment — roughly two and a half million against five million at state level and fifteen million at local level — so anyone genuinely pursuing efficiency would look elsewhere. The federal workforce was chosen, in his account, only because it is the one a president can fire, making the exercise *"a political hustle not an efficiency"* whose real function is to cut the deficit.<Cite s="2026-07-07-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-prof-richard-d-wolff" t="20:23" /><Cite s="2026-07-07-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-prof-richard-d-wolff" t="21:56" /><Cite s="2026-07-07-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-prof-richard-d-wolff" t="22:26" /> Wolff added that the federal civilian workforce is no larger today than it was in the 1960s, when the population was a hundred million smaller — which, he noted, is what economists actually call efficiency.<Cite s="2026-07-07-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-prof-richard-d-wolff" t="22:59" />
