The **Kent State shooting** was the 1970 killing of unarmed student protesters by the Ohio National Guard. It is one of the few historical events [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) claims a direct personal connection to, and he returns to it in two distinct registers: as the thing that made him a man of the left, and as the last comparable convulsion before the one he believes is coming.

## The family connection

A friend of Kiriakou's parents lost a daughter at Kent State — *"she was one of the five that was shot and killed,"* as he puts it.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:45:47" /><Cite s="2026-07-08-covert-strategies-revealed-covert-strategies-revealed" t="24:56" /> Asked whether losing a family friend's daughter was a large part of his identifying with the left, he agreed that it very much was.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:45:47" />

In his household the Ohio governor who ordered the National Guard to fire was, flatly, *"the devil."* Kiriakou's own framing of the order has stayed the same across tellings: open fire on unarmed students at a peaceful political protest — *"What is this? North Korea?"*<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:46:18" />

## The 1968–69 sequence

Kiriakou consistently sets Kent State at the end of a run of events he remembers from early childhood — *"I'm just old enough to remember 1968 and 1969. I was a kid."*<Cite s="2026-05-06-doug-bopst-doug-bopst" t="08:50" /><Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:44:45" />

The sequence begins with him sitting on his father's lap while the news showed buildings on fire. He asked why, and his father said a very bad man had killed Martin Luther King and black people were very upset — a sentence Kiriakou says he still remembers being phrased exactly that way.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:45:16" /><Cite s="2026-07-08-covert-strategies-revealed-covert-strategies-revealed" t="24:24" /> A couple of months later his mother cried all day because Robert Kennedy had been shot and killed.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:45:16" /><Cite s="2026-05-20-covert-operations-insight" t="42:55" /> In a Cyprus telling he dates the two killings precisely — King in April, Kennedy in June — and describes the gigantic demonstrations, in some cases riots, against the Vietnam War that followed, with Kent State coming a year later.<Cite s="2026-07-23-cyprus-diaspora-forum-john-kiriakou-fireside-chat" t="1:13:43" />

Compressed, the run he recites is: *"Martin Luther King's assassinated, then Bobby Kennedy's assassinated, then Kent State, and then the summer of love, and everybody's in the street, and the cops are shooting unarmed people."*<Cite s="2026-05-06-doug-bopst-doug-bopst" t="09:21" /><Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-cia-whistleblower-escape-system" t="1:14:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-strategies-revealed-covert-strategies-revealed" t="1:12:49" /> He calls 1968 *"nightmarish"* and says it got worse in 1969 with the shooting.<Cite s="2026-05-20-covert-operations-insight" t="43:25" /><Cite s="2026-05-28-john-kiriakou-podcast-john-kiriakou-podcast" t="43:11" />

## As a measure of the present

The reason Kiriakou raises Kent State is almost always comparative. Having lived through 1968 and 1969, he argues the country is now *"just as split"* as it was then — a division he says he has not otherwise seen in his adult lifetime.<Cite s="2026-05-06-doug-bopst-doug-bopst" t="08:50" /><Cite s="2026-05-06-doug-bopst-doug-bopst" t="09:21" />

His Cyprus account draws the arc out further and ends darker. After Kent State, he says, the country entered a period in which nothing so severe happened; now *"we're getting ready to explode again. Like it's coming. Everybody sees it that it's coming."* What makes it worse in his reading is that the division itself is disabling — *"nobody feels that they have the power to stop it"* — and he expects the consequence plainly: *"it's going to get worse before it gets any better. And worse is going to mean people are going to die."*<Cite s="2026-07-23-cyprus-diaspora-forum-john-kiriakou-fireside-chat" t="1:14:13" /> He also notes he is speaking freely while a pardon application is pending, saying he has *"literally nothing to lose."*<Cite s="2026-07-23-cyprus-diaspora-forum-john-kiriakou-fireside-chat" t="1:15:15" />

Alongside the same memories he traces the political narrowing he objects to: from what he calls the free thinking of 1968 and 1969 through Watergate in 1974, to a present in which the two parties *"pretend to have these significant political differences"* while *"rearranging the deck chairs on the Titanic."*<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:46:18" />
