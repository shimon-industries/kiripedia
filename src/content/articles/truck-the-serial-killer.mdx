**Truck** was the prison nickname of a convicted serial killer held at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto) during [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s sentence, and one of the men Kiriakou uses to illustrate how far he says he went during his time inside.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="35:02" />

## The crimes

In Kiriakou's account, Truck picked up prostitutes, had sex with them, and — not wanting to pay them — strangled them, then drove a couple of hundred miles with the bodies beside him before throwing them out at the roadside. The FBI knew a serial killer was operating during what Kiriakou calls the golden age of serial killers, roughly 1970 to 1990, but did not know where to look.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="26:10" />

He was caught when a 16-year-old he picked up at a truck stop and strangled survived. Because she was 16, Kiriakou notes, that technically made him a [chomo](/wiki/chomo) — a designation about which he says Truck was very, very sensitive.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="26:10" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="26:40" />

## The approach

Kiriakou describes Truck as an enormous man, at least six foot four and perhaps 350 pounds, with a handful of rotten blackened stumps for teeth and the worst breath Kiriakou had ever encountered.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="26:40" />

For reasons Kiriakou says were never clear to him, Truck constantly sought his approval. Their first exchange was almost belligerent — Truck demanding to know whether Kiriakou was the CIA man, and Kiriakou bracing for a fight. Truck then claimed to have done some work for the CIA in Vietnam, and afterwards to have run guns to the Angolan rebels in a shrimp boat.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="27:11" />

Kiriakou did not accept it. He told Truck a shrimp boat could not travel thirty miles, let alone the long way across the Atlantic to Angola, and to think of a better story — then walked away. One of [the Italians](/wiki/italians-at-loretto) warned him afterwards that Truck was one dangerous son of a bitch, and Kiriakou says he asked himself why he was challenging such a man.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="27:11" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="27:43" />

The rebuff had the opposite of the expected effect. Instead of angering Truck it made him more determined to win Kiriakou's approval — something Kiriakou says he still does not understand.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="27:43" /> Truck would save him a seat in the television room for Steelers games, which Kiriakou notes meant punching whoever's seat it was, and would pass on tips such as the frequency of a new classic rock station. Kiriakou says he worked hard not to anger him.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="28:14" />

Truck was also frequently in [solitary confinement](/wiki/solitary-confinement) for beating pedophile cellmates, usually over trivial provocations — one turned on a light while he was napping, another was snoring.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="28:14" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="28:44" />

## The Raviv beating

Kiriakou used Truck deliberately. He had refused to let [Larry Raviv](/wiki/larry-raviv), a murder-for-hire convict, move into his cell, which he says sent Raviv into a rage.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="30:19" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="30:50" />

On a day when Kiriakou had been called to the lieutenant's office to sign a waiver for a television interview with [Jake Tapper](/wiki/jake-tapper), Raviv — standing at the email computers, unaware Kiriakou was sitting two feet behind him watching football with Truck — told another prisoner that Kiriakou had gone down there to inform on them, and called him a rat.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="31:22" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="31:52" />

Kiriakou did not react. Truck asked whether he had heard it. Kiriakou leaned over and told Truck that an hour earlier he had heard Raviv call *Truck* a pedophile.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="31:52" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="32:24" />

Truck said nothing, got up, and beat Raviv unconscious. Kiriakou stayed in his seat and watched the game — and notes that when the alarm sounded he was the only man who had not scattered, with Raviv on the ground and Truck covered in blood.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="32:24" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="32:54" />

Called to the lieutenant's office and told he had been seen on four cameras, Kiriakou applied his standing rule to [admit nothing, deny everything, make counter accusations](/wiki/admit-nothing-deny-everything), suggesting the officers had staged the fight themselves. He was told to get out of the office.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="33:25" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="33:57" />

Truck was sent to solitary, formally charged with assault, and had five years added to his forty-year sentence. Raviv was airlifted to Pittsburgh with a skull fracture and returned six weeks later to apologise.<Cite s="2026-06-08-dead-drop-s2e4-with-friends-like-these" t="34:31" />
