**Bernie Sanders** is a United States senator from Vermont. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of him is not political but observational, drawn from the years he spent as a staffer for Senator [John Kerry](/wiki/john-kerry) on the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate-foreign-relations-committee), when the two men kept the same early hours in the same building.

## The cafeteria

Kiriakou's routine was a bagel from the Senate cafeteria every morning, and the detail he retained is that Sanders was always already there. *"Every single morning there was Bernie sitting at a table having breakfast and inviting any Joe who walked in off the street to sit and have breakfast with him — and the only thing he insisted on was that you call him Bernie."*<Cite s="2016-04-26-fort-collins-community-action-network-john-kiriakou-cia-whistle-blower-part-2" t="09:34" />

He sets that against the opposite manner in the same room, which he says he witnessed directly: another senator who would announce *"I'm a senator coming through, I'm going to the front of the line, I'm a senator"* — and who, on one occasion, *"shove[d] a pregnant woman out of his way so he could go to the front of the cashier's line."* Kiriakou's summary of the comparison is four words: *"Bernie's not like that."*<Cite s="2016-04-26-fort-collins-community-action-network-john-kiriakou-cia-whistle-blower-part-2" t="10:06" />

## The 2016 field

Asked in April 2016 whether any presidential candidate might address the torture question seriously, Kiriakou named two. The first was Sanders — *"who I just absolutely adore"* — and the second, *"as crazy as it sounds,"* was Rand Paul, whom he immediately discounted on electoral grounds: Paul *"doesn't mind polling at two percent,"* but *"he can't win a Republican primary."*<Cite s="2016-04-26-fort-collins-community-action-network-john-kiriakou-cia-whistle-blower-part-2" t="09:01" />

Sanders reappears in Kiriakou's argument about why American foreign-policy debate had narrowed to counterterrorism alone. His explanation is structural — that the country has *"transitioned to a permanent wartime economy,"* that large corporations *"can thrive only in this permanent wartime economy,"* and that this requires *"an enemy that we can build up against"* at all times. The evidence he offers for the grip of that arrangement is the silence of the field: *"almost none of the presidential candidates, with the exception of Bernie Sanders, are talking about actually downsizing the military."*<Cite s="2016-04-26-fort-collins-community-action-network-john-kiriakou-cia-whistle-blower-part-2" t="14:21" />

## February 2020: the vote and the expectation

Two weeks before his primary, Kiriakou said flatly that he loved Sanders and would *"proudly vote for Bernie Sanders,"* and thought he had *"enough momentum to put everybody else away."*<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="16:44" />

The expectation attached to it was pessimistic: *"that's not to say that the Democratic National Committee won't steal this thing from him at the Democratic convention again, exactly"* as in 2016.<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="17:16" /> The evidence he gave for *again* was two contests: *"Bernie Sanders won the West Virginia primary and the Colorado caucus, won handily, and didn't get a single delegate in either one of those states. [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/hillary-clinton) got all the delegates. That's not democracy."*<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="17:16" />

Asked whether the party would break up over it, his answer was two sentences long: he would not be surprised at all, *"and Trump's gonna win anyway."*<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="16:13" /><Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="17:49" />
