The **Rwanda deportations** are the British policy of removing asylum seekers to Rwanda for resettlement, which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) used in July 2023 as his evidence for including the United Kingdom among Europe's entrenched right-wing governments.

## The classification

Having listed Poland and Hungary, Kiriakou added Britain with a qualifier — *"in the UK, let's be honest"* — and then gave his reason. *"When they're scooping up refugees off the streets and then putting them on planes and sending them to Rwanda for resettlement, I mean that's a right-wing extremist policy and it's probably illegal."*<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="59:25" />

His interviewer agreed that it probably was, and Kiriakou did not soften the assessment.<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="59:25" />

## Where it sits in his argument

The remark closes a sequence in which Kiriakou had walked through the European elections of 2023 — Italy's prime minister, the polling of the French far right, and [the Greek neo-Nazi seats](/wiki/the-greek-neo-nazi-seats), which he treated as the most disturbing of the three.<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="58:51" />

His summary of the resulting political menu was that most of Western Europe now chooses between *"a right-wing government, a far right-wing government, or an extreme right-wing government."*<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="57:03" /> Britain's asylum policy was, in that frame, not an aberration but a data point.
