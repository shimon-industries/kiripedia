**James Bond** is the myth that shadows every real intelligence career, including [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s, from the Christmas presents he asked for as a boy to the language federal prosecutors used against him thirty-five years later.

## Wanting to be Bond

Asked whether he wanted to be James Bond as a kid, Kiriakou's answer is yes. At nine he told his parents he wanted to be a spy when he grew up; they thought it was cute and bought him walkie-talkies, a box with disappearing ink and a little code reader for Christmas.<Cite s="2019-08-08-david-gornoski-archives-david-gornoski-archives" t="02:07" /><Cite s="2025-10-08-dead-drop-s1e0-coming-very-soon" t="03:58" /> He kept logs of what the neighbor kids were doing.<Cite s="2019-08-08-david-gornoski-archives-david-gornoski-archives" t="02:07" />

He was still serious about it at sixteen, in a conversation he can still place on the road, driving down Old Plank Road past Frazier's Pond with a father who wanted him to take a science degree. *"I said, but dad, I'm not interested in science. I want to be a spy in the Middle East. And he said, why? After all these years, can't you just get this out of your system?"* The supply was television: Bond films and the spy comedy *Get Smart*. *"I really believed I was going to be James Bond. And in a lot of ways I was. We all were."*<Cite s="2025-10-08-dead-drop-s1e0-coming-very-soon" t="04:30" /><Cite s="2025-10-08-dead-drop-s1e0-coming-very-soon" t="05:02" />

He loved the movies, the subterfuge, the excitement<Cite s="2022-09-13-hr-news-channel-hr-news-channel" t="06:50" />, and remembers the recruitment-era thrill of working against Soviet communism as *"a James Bond kind of thing... of course that turned out to be ridiculous."*<Cite s="2023-03-28-art-and-documentary-art-and-documentary" t="04:44" /> The pull is institutional, not just personal: *"It's in operations where everybody wants to be James Bond, and in some cases they actually achieve that,"* aspiring to the handful of legendary senior officers with many recruitments.<Cite s="2025-04-27-the-dr-phil-podcast-i-exposed-the-cia-and-went-to-prison-john-kir" t="08:24" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-dr-phil-podcast-pardon-him-already" t="09:30" /> Asked directly whether the Bond aspect brings in a lot of recruits, his answer is *"oh, very much so"*, and the justification he offers is not glamour but itinerary: 65 countries in fourteen and a half years, several overseas tours totalling about five years abroad, and two attempts on his life, which he says is not unusual for an officer working counterterrorism.<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="27:28" /> Training delivered a taste: airplanes, mini-subs from the ocean depths, a shot-out windscreen that made him think *"this is awesome"*<Cite s="2015-07-25-splendour-in-the-grass" t="05:18" />, along with surveillance and surveillance detection, [counterterrorist driving](/wiki/counterterrorist-driving) and a multitude of weapons.<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="28:01" /> The way in, he notes, is no longer like that at all, since 1993 it has been *"far less sexy and clandestine"*, a matter of going to the CIA's website and clicking apply.<Cite s="2025-04-12-the-clear-signal-with-stev" t="1:13:56" />

## The reality audit

Rating Hollywood counterterrorism scenes, Kiriakou is blunt: *"Literally none of that is true to life, but that's what makes you love James Bond."* Almost no officers carry weapons; a post must be rated high or critical threat, and in his whole career he was armed only in Athens and Pakistan. Shooting one's way through a parliament is *"just not real life."*<Cite s="2024-12-17-insider-cia-officer-john-kiriakou-rates-9-counterterr" t="00:36" /><Cite s="2024-12-17-insider-cia-officer-john-kiriakou-rates-9-counterterr" t="01:06" /> But one Bond staple he certifies: James always runs into the CIA officer at the embassy party, *"that happens every day in real life. We always see each other and say hello."*<Cite s="2023-03-28-art-and-documentary-art-and-documentary" t="30:32" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="2:25:57" />

The franchise even reached the org chart: the CIA's venture capital arm named itself [In-Q-Tel](/wiki/in-q-tel), the Q inserted *"as kind of a wink-wink nod to Q from the James Bond movies"*, a piece of self-conscious cuteness Kiriakou reports with amusement in more than one telling.<Cite s="2025-02-19-katie-halper" t="1:02:10" /><Cite s="2025-10-16-seymizzle-podcast-ask-ex-cia-agent-and-whistleblower-john-kiria" t="46:57" />

## Who is James Bond?

The character has also been used as a diagnostic instrument, and Kiriakou reports the results on himself and his colleagues with visible discomfort. A New Mexico State University professor, Peter Jonason, published a paper titled *Who is James Bond?* finding that successful intelligence officers share a triumvirate of traits: *"the stratospheric self-esteem of narcissism, the fearlessness, ruthlessness, impulsivity and thrill-seeking of psychopathy, and the deceitfulness and exploitiveness of Machiavellianism."* His response is two words: *"that's a little harsh."*<Cite s="2025-10-30-full-audiobook-full-audiobook" t="05:46" />

He sets it against what a CIA psychiatrist told him, which points the other way: the agency does not want [sociopaths](/wiki/sociopathic-tendencies), who are too hard to control, but people comfortable working in ethical, moral and legal grey areas, *"I had no problem bending some rules if it was in the interest of national security."*<Cite s="2025-10-30-full-audiobook-full-audiobook" t="05:15" /> And an actual psychopath, on the definition he quotes, seeks reward at any cost and elbows risk aside, which is the opposite of the job: the officer *"tries to get the job done while not making waves or attracting undue attention."*<Cite s="2025-10-30-full-audiobook-full-audiobook" t="06:18" />

## What became of Bond

Kiriakou uses the figure once more as a measure of decline, in the case of the MI6 officer he says he ran an operation with in London in the 1990s, Christopher Steele, at the time *"the guy at MI6 that all the other MI6 guys wanted to be, that's how highly respected he was."* His theory is that Steele *"went from the James Bond kind of officer that every MI6 guy wanted to be to this caricature"* not through any change in the man but through the loss of the machinery around him: inside a service, an operations officer passes whatever he collects to analysts who sort the rubbish from the possible, and the golden-showers claim would have died there for want of corroboration. In private practice there were no analysts.<Cite s="2025-02-25-mk-ultra-usaid-overthrowing-governments" t="56:18" /><Cite s="2025-02-25-mk-ultra-usaid-overthrowing-governments" t="56:50" /><Cite s="2025-02-25-mk-ultra-usaid-overthrowing-governments" t="57:22" /><Cite s="2025-02-25-mk-ultra-usaid-overthrowing-governments" t="57:54" />

The myth was finally turned on him. Describing how prosecutors work to deny a defendant bail, Kiriakou says that when they came after him *"they talked about me like I was 007, you know, he's been to too many countries, he's worked for agencies, we can't…"* He got bail; it was set at $250,000.<Cite s="2025-04-19-the-big-mig-show-ex-cia-and-founder-of-panquake-john-kiriakou" t="12:34" />
