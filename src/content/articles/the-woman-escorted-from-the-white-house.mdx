Kiriakou's account of why he eventually spoke turns on a period of waiting. Having declined the enhanced interrogation training and having read the cable traffic coming back from the secret site, he kept waiting for somebody to go public and say that this was happening.<Cite s="2026-05-05-whistleblower-of-the-week-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposed-tortu" t="20:21" />

## Someone did

Somebody did, but on a different subject, and at a cost. Indeed, he says, someone blew the whistle on the secret prisons that the CIA had set up.<Cite s="2026-05-05-whistleblower-of-the-week-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposed-tortu" t="20:21" />

The person who paid for it was a woman at the White House whom Kiriakou says he respected deeply. She was escorted out of the building. Her badge was taken. She was told never to come back.<Cite s="2026-05-05-whistleblower-of-the-week-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposed-tortu" t="20:21" />

## The unresolved part

The detail Kiriakou dwells on is that the punishment may have landed on the wrong person. To this day, he says, she adamantly maintains that it was not her.<Cite s="2026-05-05-whistleblower-of-the-week-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposed-tortu" t="20:21" />

He does not attempt to settle it, and does not name her. His verdict is deliberately indifferent to the identification: *"whoever it was, they're an American hero."* And he adds that we will never know who it was.<Cite s="2026-05-05-whistleblower-of-the-week-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposed-tortu" t="20:21" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-whistleblower-of-the-week-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposed-tortu" t="20:52" />

## Where it sits in his story

The episode marks the end of Kiriakou's expectation that someone else would act. In the same passage he moves directly from the woman's removal to December 2007 and Brian Ross's telephone call, which set in motion his own decision to give the interview.<Cite s="2026-05-05-whistleblower-of-the-week-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposed-tortu" t="20:52" />

It also stands as an illustration of a claim he makes often about the machinery he later faced: that a leak investigation does not need to identify the leaker in order to produce a consequence, and that the consequence, once produced, is not reviewable by anyone. The woman he describes lost her position and her access without a charge, a hearing, or, on her own account, having done the thing.
