**Signature strikes** are the drone-targeting doctrine, exposed by [Daniel Hale](/wiki/daniel-hale)'s leak in [The Drone Papers](/wiki/the-drone-papers), under which the United States kills people based on patterns of behavior or association rather than confirmed identity.<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="12:37" /> [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says the *"signature of a terrorist"* could be as little as doing *"jumping jacks"* or carrying *"a bag of something over their shoulder."*<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="19:21" /> He says operators are explicit that *"any male over the age of 12 is a legitimate target"* on the theory that *"any male over the age of 12 can pick up an AK-47 and shoot an American soldier"* — *"not that they have the gun, but that they could pick up a gun."*<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="19:52" /><Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="20:25" /> Kiriakou compares the practice to *"the Gestapo rounding up all the people that they want to murder in the center of town."*<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="19:52" /> He adds that when officers radioed asking permission to launch, superiors told them it was *"just a formality — you're supposed to say launch."*<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="20:57" /><Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="21:29" />

Kiriakou has cited a similar Obama-era Defense Department standard elsewhere: any male over the age of 14 was considered an enemy combatant by default. He contrasts the scale of drone killing at the end of each administration — two people killed by drone strike in George W. Bush's last month as president, versus 424 people killed by drone strikes in Barack Obama's last month.<Cite s="2018-06-19-real-progress-in-action" t="16:16" /> Kiriakou says he uses a phone app called Metadata that tracks drone strikes, which shows him how frequently strikes occur — including civilian and child casualties — and how often they go unreported.<Cite s="2017-05-31-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="41:24" /> He says U.S. drone operators repeatedly struck wedding parties in Yemen, roughly half a dozen times, because a "tall Arab-looking guy" at the party was mistakenly thought to be Osama bin Laden.<Cite s="2017-05-31-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="41:54" /> He has described the same pattern elsewhere — bombing weddings because a tall man dressed in white was assumed to be bin Laden, and in some cases bombing the funeral afterward as well.<Cite s="2020-12-23-codepink" t="42:12" />

Kiriakou says that when he was stationed in Pakistan, he led raids that captured dozens of alleged al-Qaeda "fighters" — most of whom, he found, were actually teenage boys from isolated, illiterate, jobless villages who had been recruited with the promise of $500 a month, plus a martyrdom payment to their families if killed.<Cite s="2020-12-23-codepink" t="48:57" /> He says he has become increasingly vocal against drone strikes that kill American citizens without ever charging them with a crime, calling it the next major civil-liberties battleground.<Cite s="2017-05-17-strand-book-store" t="34:24" />
