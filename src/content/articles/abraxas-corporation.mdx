**Abraxas Corporation** is a private intelligence company founded in the 1990s by a group of retired senior CIA officers — men [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he used to work for — and set up, legitimately, as a limited-liability tax pass-through.<Cite s="2026-06-30-jack-neel-palantir-should-terrify-you" t="04:39" /><Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="38:12" />

## What it was for

Kiriakou is unsentimental about the original purpose: these vehicles existed so that retired officers *"could go smoke cigars and drink coffee and tell stories with their friends and hang out all day."* Abraxas was where the retired operations people went; its counterpart, [The Analysis Corporation](/wiki/analysis-corporation) of Arlington, Virginia, was where the Senior Intelligence Service analysts went.<Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="16:35" /><Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="17:06" />

## What 9/11 made of it

Then, in his phrase, *"lo and behold, they started making money after 9/11."* Abraxas was bought — Kiriakou believes by IBM — for well over a billion dollars, and its founders became not merely rich but *"Mega Millions Lotto kind of rich."*<Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="17:06" /> He contrasts that outcome with the fate of private *analysis*, which he says never succeeds, because anyone expert enough in a subject can do analysis and a professor will do it for five thousand dollars.<Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="17:37" />

## Where it sits in the argument

Abraxas recurs in Kiriakou's account of the revolving door as one name in a short list. It appears alongside [Palantir](/wiki/palantir), the company [In-Q-Tel](/wiki/in-q-tel) seeded with its first investment, when he asks where American intelligence is *not* present in technology and government — a question he says genuinely worries him.<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="39:13" /> It is among the contractors whose complicated agreements the CIA's Office of General Counsel negotiates, alongside Palantir, Musk's organisations and Amazon's cloud services.<Cite s="2026-03-17-deep-focus-irans-future-cia-background-checks-us-revolution" t="21:30" /> And it marks the lower rung of the post-agency career ladder: the senior people go on to something bigger and more lucrative, Kiriakou says, and let the lower-level officers end up at Booz Allen or Abraxas — though he adds that the word *former* in front of their titles means little, since they retain as much access to people as they ever had.<Cite s="2023-02-05-consortium-news-taibbi-kiriakou-hedges-twitter-files-short" t="02:34" />
