The **Epstein Files Transparency Act** was sponsored by [Thomas Massie](/wiki/thomas-massie), Republican of Kentucky, and Ro Khanna, a Democrat, and required the Justice Department by force of law to release the [Jeffrey Epstein](/wiki/jeffrey-epstein) files no later than 19 December.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="03:39" />

It passed the House 419 to 1 and the Senate 99 to nothing, and the president signed it. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of what happened next is that the president then said, in effect, "Tough luck. I'm not going to release anything. What are you going to do about it?" — and that the answer is nothing.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="02:35" /> About three million pages were released, leaving three to three and a half million withheld, more than seven months past the deadline.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="02:35" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="03:07" />

## The legislative route was never needed

Kiriakou, who holds a master's degree in legislative affairs, argues the whole congressional exercise was unnecessary. The files are executive branch documents housed at the Department of Justice: "All the president needs to do is say release the documents."<Cite s="2026-07-13-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-nick-bryant-on-the" t="18:48" /><Cite s="2026-07-13-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-nick-bryant-on-the" t="19:20" /> Congress reached for a discharge petition only because he would not, and Kiriakou reads the sequence of votes as a charade that delays release rather than compelling it — the president can encourage members to vote yes while a Senate leader kills the bill, leaving him able to say he tried.<Cite s="2026-07-13-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-nick-bryant-on-the" t="19:51" /><Cite s="2026-07-13-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-nick-bryant-on-the" t="22:30" /><Cite s="2026-07-13-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-nick-bryant-on-the" t="23:02" />

He also notes how rare the instrument was: discharge petitions are attempted often and almost never succeed — he cites one on withholding tax in 1986 — and predicts people will write books about this one.<Cite s="2026-07-13-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-nick-bryant-on-the" t="38:46" />

## The cost to its backers

Kiriakou records that the Republicans who supported release were punished: super PACs mostly affiliated with [AIPAC](/wiki/aipac) spent thirty-five million dollars against Massie, another member was forced to resign after her son received death threats and the White House declined to help, a sitting congresswoman came fifth in a governor's race after losing an endorsement, and a fourth was brought into the situation room and pressed to take her name off the discharge petition.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="04:11" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="04:41" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="05:12" />

## "I would just release everything"

Asked in February 2026 about [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump)'s position, Kiriakou declined to argue about guilt and argued about strategy instead: *"if I were Donald Trump and I were confident in my innocence, I would just release everything."*<Cite s="2026-02-08-how-epstein-was-used-by-mossad-cia-mi6" t="14:59" />

His objection is to the pace rather than to the policy: *"I just don't understand why it has taken this long, and it's not even done yet… he's shooting himself in the foot if he's truly, truly, truly innocent."*<Cite s="2026-02-08-how-epstein-was-used-by-mossad-cia-mi6" t="15:29" />
