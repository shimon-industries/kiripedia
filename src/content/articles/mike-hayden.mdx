**General Michael V. Hayden** was the Director of the National Security Agency on September 11, 2001, and subsequently became Director of the Central Intelligence Agency under President George W. Bush.<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="50:01" />

## *"Waiting for a 9/11"*

Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) — relaying NSA whistleblower [Tom Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake) — Hayden on the morning of September 11, 2001 told NSA personnel that the agency had been *"waiting for a 9/11 so that they could implement the bigger program and grab everybody's electronics."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="50:01" />

Kiriakou's framing: *"Not only is it illegal for NSA to intercept the communications of American citizens or U.S. persons, which is anybody in the country legally on a green card — it is a part of NSA's founding charter forbidding them from intercepting the communications of Americans or U.S. persons. And they just say, 'Tough. 9/11. National security. What are you going to do? You got to start from scratch.'"*<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="50:34" />

## Reputation at NSA

Kiriakou describes Hayden as an egomaniac who was disliked at the NSA, where employees actively sought transfers to the CIA: *"It was such an amateur hour under Michael Hayden over there at NSA. Nobody liked working for Hayden."*<Cite s="2020-05-25-scott-horton" t="41:22" />

## Personal falling-out and later decline

Kiriakou recounts a personal falling-out with Hayden after refusing to address him as "General" backstage at a Bill Maher taping — Kiriakou, a civilian, called him "Mike," prompting Hayden to walk out and skip the show.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="36:43" /> He says Hayden suffered a major stroke about a year before that interview and appeared frail, using two canes and needing his mouth wiped, at a National Press Club event.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="36:43" />

## On the "deep state" and CIA directors

Kiriakou has used Hayden's career, and the broader pattern of career intelligence officials, to illustrate what he considers a real "deep state": officials in senior positions for 25 to 42 years who outlast presidents and slow-roll directives they disagree with.<Cite s="2026-07-05-the-bad-news-program" t="1:04:58" /> He explains Chuck Schumer's January 2017 remark that intelligence agencies have "nine ways from Sunday to screw you" as meaning agencies slow-roll or ignore presidential directives they oppose — something he says happened to both Jimmy Carter and Trump.<Cite s="2026-07-05-the-bad-news-program" t="1:06:01" /> Kiriakou says he is philosophically opposed to any CIA director having been a general or admiral, arguing the last CIA director with true independence from the president was Stansfield Turner, who served from 1977 to 1981.<Cite s="2026-05-27-the-information-rights-pro" t="52:13" />
