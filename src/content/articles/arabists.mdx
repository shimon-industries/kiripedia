**Arabist** was the term used inside the U.S. foreign-policy establishment for officials who knew Arabic and the history of the region. Put to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) that the label worked as a dismissal — that respected State Department officers were written off as blindly prejudiced against Israel, or as antisemitic, simply for knowing the language and the history — his answer was: *"Yes. Absolutely true. Absolutely true."*<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="23:51" /><Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="24:27" />

The example he reaches for is Ambassador Chas Freeman, under whom he served in Saudi Arabia and whom he describes as *"one of the most brilliant minds I've ever encountered."*<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="24:27" />

The institutional context he sets around it is that sympathy for the Palestinians was, in his experience, the ordinary view among people who worked the region: *"there was a deep admiration just among the rank and file for the Palestinian people. Not for the Israelis, but for the Palestinians"* — the same at the State Department — and that it stopped counting once a decision reached the White House.<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="20:38" /><Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="23:17" />
