**Ali Abdullah Saleh** led [Yemen](/wiki/yemen) for decades. In [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account he was close to Saudi Arabia and close to the United States alike — until he split with Washington over Yemen's vote on the use-of-force resolution against [Saddam Hussein](/wiki/saddam-hussein).<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="26:24" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="26:57" />

A civil war began immediately, and Kiriakou stresses that it was never a simple two-sided fight: Al-Qaeda fought in it from the Hadramaut in the east — the name means dead vegetation, an area so dry that not even animals live there, and therefore a no man's land nobody else could enter, where [Osama bin Laden](/wiki/osama-bin-laden)'s family originated. Former communists attacked the central government in the south, and the Shia Muslims of the north-west attacked it too.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="26:57" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="27:28" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="27:59" />

Saleh fled to Saudi Arabia, which took him in reluctantly. Someone was able to get into the palace where he was living and plant a bomb, which, in Kiriakou's blunt phrasing, "quite literally blew his head off. So, Ali Abdullah Saleh no longer exists."<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="27:59" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="28:29" />

The movement that emerged from the north-western front of that war, the [Houthis](/wiki/houthis), eventually took control of the government he had led.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="29:30" />
