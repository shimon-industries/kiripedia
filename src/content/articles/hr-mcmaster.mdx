**H. R. McMaster** was National Security Adviser to [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) in May 2017, when reports that the president had disclosed sensitive intelligence to the Russian foreign minister and ambassador in the Oval Office produced a public defence from him: that the disclosure was wholly appropriate, and that *"the president wasn't even aware of where this information came from, he wasn't briefed on the source or method of the information either."*<Cite s="2017-05-17-democracy-now-blowing-the-whistle-why-trump-worries-him" t="00:00" />

## The fork

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), asked about that defence the following day, called the statement *"something very important"* and then turned it around. Neither branch of it leaves McMaster undamaged.<Cite s="2017-05-17-democracy-now-blowing-the-whistle-why-trump-worries-him" t="03:11" />

If the claim is true and the president genuinely did not know the provenance of what he was repeating, *"then shame on General McMaster, because that's his job, to make sure that the president is informed."*<Cite s="2017-05-17-democracy-now-blowing-the-whistle-why-trump-worries-him" t="03:11" />

If instead McMaster did brief the president on the information and either withheld the source or failed to convey what Kiriakou calls *"the clandestinity of this source,"* then *"maybe we should be talking today about General McMaster resigning."*<Cite s="2017-05-17-democracy-now-blowing-the-whistle-why-trump-worries-him" t="03:11" />

The underlying complaint is not about the president's authority to declassify, which Kiriakou concedes, but about the absence of the [procedure that exists for exactly this purpose](/wiki/declassifying-for-a-foreign-government).<Cite s="2017-05-17-democracy-now-blowing-the-whistle-why-trump-worries-him" t="02:08" />
