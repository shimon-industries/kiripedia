**Joe Weisberg** is a former CIA Counterterrorism Center officer whom [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes as a colleague, a friend and a brilliant writer — *"a real sweetheart"* who worked in the centre at the same time he did, and for whom he says he has *"deep respect."*<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="1:22:40" /><Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones.sponsors" t="32:41" /><Cite s="2026-06-28-deep-focus-global-power-struggles" t="41:42" />

## The resignation

Kiriakou tells the same story a dozen ways and it never changes in substance: Weisberg walked up to him at the office one day and announced he had resigned that morning. *"I was like, 'What? Why would you resign?' And he said, 'This job is just not for me.'"*<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="1:22:40" /><Cite s="2026-05-08-jay-dyer-real-cia-history" t="1:44:57" /><Cite s="2026-04-07-deep-focus-mideast-revenge-cia-territories" t="44:30" />

The reason is always the same organ. *"I don't have it in my gut to convince somebody to commit treason."*<Cite s="2026-04-29-jay-dyer-they-really-did-this" t="1:59:42" /> In the fullest telling: *"I just don't have it in my gut to get somebody to commit espionage or treason. I can't do it. I just don't think I'll be good at it."* Asked what he would do instead, Weisberg said he was single, had no children and nothing to tie him to Washington — *"I'm gonna go to Hollywood and find my fortune."*<Cite s="2025-12-29-dead-drop-s1e8-the-james-bond-academy.sponsors" t="23:45" /><Cite s="2026-06-28-deep-focus-global-power-struggles" t="41:42" /> Kiriakou's own gloss is that Weisberg was *"a terrible case officer"* who *"just wasn't cut out for it,"* a mismatch of personality rather than ability.<Cite s="2024-09-04-failure" t="04:12" />

## The novel, then the show

Between the agency and the blockbuster there was a book. Weisberg wrote a novel — *"quite a well regarded novel that didn't really sell very well"* — which the CIA's prepublication reviewers heavily redacted, and which was praised precisely for being true to life. After that one novel he created *The Americans*.<Cite s="2026-05-04-dead-drop-s1e26-did-john-le-carre-invent-the-modern-spy" t="16:32" /><Cite s="2026-04-29-jay-dyer-they-really-did-this" t="1:59:42" /> He became, in Kiriakou's description, a wealthy and highly sought-after A-list writer and showrunner.<Cite s="2026-06-28-deep-focus-global-power-struggles" t="41:42" />

Kiriakou's verdict on the result is unqualified: *"the best intelligence-related show ever."*<Cite s="2022-12-27-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l" t="1:18:31" /> He describes the premise in detail — two Soviet KGB intelligence officers posing as an American married couple in Falls Church, Virginia in the mid-1980s, their cover a travel agency at Dupont Circle, while they penetrate [the Farm](/wiki/the-farm), the Pentagon and defence contractors, recruit Americans and carry out hits. *"I loved it. It was 100% true to life. 100%."*<Cite s="2025-12-29-dead-drop-s1e8-the-james-bond-academy.sponsors" t="22:42" /><Cite s="2025-12-29-dead-drop-s1e8-the-james-bond-academy.sponsors" t="23:14" />

## Why he says it is true

The show's realism is, for Kiriakou, a documentary claim about the [Russian illegals programme](/wiki/russian-illegals-program) rather than a compliment about craft. The Russians send an illegal — a deep-cover officer — for twenty or twenty-five years, in place and waiting to be activated.<Cite s="2025-12-29-dead-drop-s1e8-the-james-bond-academy.sponsors" t="22:42" /> He points to a former neighbour of his own in Arlington, Virginia, an elementary school substitute teacher arrested as a Russian [sleeper agent](/wiki/sleeper-agent) for the SVR after years of living as an ordinary American.<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="1:23:12" /> And he reports the question he is asked most about the series — did the KGB really do something like that? — with the answer he always gives: *"oh yes. And not only did they do it, but they did it quite successfully."*<Cite s="2025-12-11-deep-focus-jack-barsky-kgb" t="05:00" />

## Every script redacted

Weisberg's other function in Kiriakou's argument is as the strongest available example of [CIA influence over Hollywood](/wiki/cia-and-hollywood) and of censorship by prepublication review. Weisberg submitted every single script of *The Americans* to the CIA's [Publications Review Board](/wiki/publications-review-board) for clearance, *"and every single script came back with redactions — and we're talking about fiction, fiction that was set in the 1970s, 1980s, and they're still redacting that stuff. It's mind-boggling."*<Cite s="2019-09-06-what-julian-assange-is-up-against" t="19:21" /><Cite s="2019-09-06-what-julian-assange-is-up-against" t="19:51" />
