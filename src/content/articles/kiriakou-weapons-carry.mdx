**How Kiriakou went armed in Athens** is a measure of how seriously he took [17 November](/wiki/revolutionary-organization-17-november) — and of the operational paranoia his training was designed to instil.

He describes the daily load precisely: a 9mm pistol in a tear-away waist pack with three magazines, and a Smith & Wesson snub-nosed .38 on his ankle carrying six rounds. All together, he reckons, about forty-seven rounds — as many as he could squeeze in.<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="09:31" /><Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="10:03" />

On his first day on the job his boss presented him with a Smith & Wesson brand buck knife, with the words *"just in case things go to shit."* It never left his back pocket. He still has it twenty-seven years later; it is in his nightstand.<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="10:03" /><Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="10:33" />

One habit qualified all of it. Kiriakou never kept a round in the chamber, being *"always afraid that God forbid one of my kids should find the gun"* — which is why, when a man came at him during the [attempted carjacking](/wiki/athens-carjacking), he had to pull the slide back before the weapon meant anything.<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="34:02" />

## Why

The armament went with a routine designed to defeat a pattern: leaving at a different time every day, taking a different route every day, never establishing a habit. His training, he says, was meant to make him paranoid — so that when the bad guys really were out to get him he would see them.<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="08:59" /><Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="09:31" />

His wife, he says, thought the threat exaggerated. She knew how well trained he was and how heavily armed, and she knew 17 November had no interest in her — a gap in perception he identifies as one of the things that corroded the marriage, since he could not tell her the whole truth and resented her obliviousness a little more each time.<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="10:03" /><Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="11:03" />

## What removes the right to carry

The entitlement is conditional and is lost easily. Kiriakou describes an officer barred from a [Beirut](/wiki/beirut) assignment because he had been accused of beating his wife: the accusation alone, without a conviction, removes an officer's authority to carry a gun, and a posting that cannot be worked unarmed therefore becomes closed to him.<Cite s="2026-01-19-dead-drop-s1e11-have-human-spies-become-obsolete" t="13:03" />

## Pakistan: the opposite decision

The Athens load was not how he worked everywhere. Arriving in Pakistan he declined a weapon outright, and gave two reasons for it. The first was his own state: he had *"just finished a tour in Athens, where I was the subject of an assassination attempt,"* and was *"still a little rattled, PTSD-wise,"* from it.<Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="02:33" /><Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="03:04" /> The second was what he thought he might do with a gun in that condition — *"I declined the gun when I first arrived in Pakistan because I was afraid that I would just kill somebody."*<Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="03:04" /> His reasoning was that everybody around him was armed anyway: *"just better to not have a gun, because if there's going to be a shootout, well, I'm going to just hope for the best and hope that my colleagues can take care of me."*<Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="03:04" />

He went armed for the raids, but it *"took me a couple of months to ease into that."*<Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="03:04" /> Body armour he skipped as well, and remembers being challenged on it by a colleague who tapped him on the chest on the way out: *"Hey, where's your bulletproof vest?"* His answer was that he hated the way it felt, that it was a hundred degrees outside, and that it would leave him sweating and dehydrated. The colleague's response was *"are you crazy?"* — to which Kiriakou pointed out that he did not have a gun on him either.<Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="03:35" /><Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="04:05" />

He attributes the whole disposition to age rather than courage. Asked by his mother after Pakistan whether he had been afraid, he said no — *"because I was so busy that it just never occurred to me to be afraid"* — and adds that in your twenties and thirties *"you think that you're indestructible. And looking back, I did think I was indestructible."*<Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="02:02" /><Cite s="2025-07-21-capital-and-coast-jailed-for-exposing-cia-secrets-john-kiriakou" t="02:33" />
