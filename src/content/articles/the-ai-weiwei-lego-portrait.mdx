**The Ai Weiwei Lego portrait** is John Kiriakou's account of his inclusion in an installation by the Chinese artist [Ai Weiwei](/wiki/ai-weiwei), given on the DeProgram programme in September 2025 as the coda to a longer complaint about the organisations that declined to support him during his prosecution.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:36" />

## The refusal that preceded it

Kiriakou first restates the grievance. [Amnesty International](/wiki/amnesty-international) *"refused to say a single word in support of me in my darkest days of 2012"*, a decision he attributes to its London headquarters.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:05" />

He separates the American office from that. Its head — *"a guy named Zeke, I think his last name was Johnson. Awesome guy"* — *"wrote me a letter when I was in prison apologising for his bosses in London"*, and *"bought me a subscription to The New Yorker to kind of dull the pain, I guess."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:05" />

## The commission

The organisation's position surfaced again in a context Kiriakou had nothing to do with. *"It was only later, when I was getting out of prison, that Ai Weiwei, the Chinese artist, wanted to do my portrait for this show at Alcatraz."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:36" />

The scale of the work he gives precisely: *"He made 178 portraits out of Legos of 178 political prisoners around the world."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:36" />

The artist's process included an external check: *"he wanted to clear the names with Amnesty International first."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:36" />

## The objection, and the reversal

Amnesty's answer was to deny the premise. *"And Amnesty said, 'Oh, we don't believe Kiriakou is a political prisoner.'"*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:36" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:06" />

Ai Weiwei, in Kiriakou's telling, insisted on personal knowledge: *"And he said, 'Oh, I know Kiriakou. I'm telling you, he is a political prisoner.'"*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:06" />

The organisation gave way, and the timing is the point of the story: *"And so they finally agreed. And it was the week that I got out of prison that Amnesty International finally said, 'Okay, okay, you were a political prisoner.'"*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:06" />

Kiriakou's summary of how the concession was extracted is unflattering: *"But they had to be, you know, pulled kicking and screaming to that point of view."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:06" />

## The wider pattern

He places the episode alongside other institutional refusals he encountered, and closes the subject with a remark about a prominent Washington attorney who reportedly still does not consider him a whistleblower: *"But isn't it wonderful to be at a point in my life where I don't give a fuck what he thinks about me?"*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="52:40" />
