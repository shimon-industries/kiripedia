**The Mayflower Madam analogy** is the comparison [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) draws between the Epstein files and the 1980s prostitution case whose defendant's address book, unlike the Epstein material, actually reached the public.

## The book that did become public

What Kiriakou finds instructive about the Mayflower Madam's *"very famous little black book"* is not the clients it named but the company they kept in it. *"Yeah, she had her johns in there and she had her girls in there, but she had her dry cleaner in there and she had her Chinese food in there. I mean, the point is that's what people do."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="46:47" />

An address book, in other words, records everyone a person deals with, and appearing in one establishes nothing on its own. The conclusion he draws for the Epstein material is blunt: anyone *"looking for a spreadsheet that says 'my child sex client XLS', it's not in there."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="47:19" />

## Why that is not the end of it

Kiriakou does not use the point to dismiss the question, and says so immediately: *"but that's not really the issue."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="47:19" /> The issue remains [the identity of the men who committed crimes against children](/wiki/the-maxwell-justice-department-interview), which a name-by-name reading of an address book will not settle either way.

His judgement on the government's handling is deliberately non-partisan. The administration is *"playing it too cute"*, and he adds, *"like all administrations"* do.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="47:19" />
