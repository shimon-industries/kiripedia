**Advice to an aspiring intelligence analyst** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s answer to a question from a 21-year-old student pursuing a career in intelligence analysis, who asked what degrees were most applicable and what he should be reading.

Kiriakou called it a great question and answered it in three parts. The first is geographic specialisation: *"I would focus on your geographical area of interest. For me it was the Middle East. Some people devoted their whole lives to Latin America, Eurasia, China, whatever."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:28" />

The second is depth within it — *"become the go-to person on that issue"* — and the third is volume: *"honestly consume everything, everything."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:28" /><Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="22:01" /> He drew one boundary around that instruction, distinguishing it from indiscriminate consumption of conspiracy media: *"I don't mean go to InfoWars and watch people for 2 hours every day. But in your field, in your area, be sure to get everything."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="22:01" />

## Reading what you don't enjoy

The example he gives of the discipline involved is Foreign Affairs, which he describes as *"sometimes so dense,"* with articles *"usually written by people who have never actually created or implemented foreign policy, they've just studied it."* He forces himself through it anyway, on the principle that *"you have to know what everybody is saying."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="22:01" />

The reason is practical and, in his account, applies to his own working life. *"Oftentimes — and I mean this literally — there are issues that I know I'm going to have to comment on that I just simply haven't formed an opinion on. And so I need to see what other people whom I respect are saying, so that I can form my own opinion."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="22:31" />

He agreed with the related suggestion that the value of such publications lies partly in reading the establishment's mind — Foreign Policy being *"what the Beltway is thinking"* — and gave as the example the moment the magazine began conceding that Ukraine had lost the war, which he took as the establishment accepting a conclusion he and his co-host had reached years earlier.<Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="22:31" /><Cite s="2026-03-20-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="23:02" />
