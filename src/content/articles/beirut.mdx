**Beirut** is the city [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) confirms was a genuine espionage playground, though he places its peak well in the past.

## Why it was a spy centre

His answer to whether the reputation is deserved is unqualified — *"Beirut has always been an espionage playground"* — but the era matters: *"Not so much in recent years as in the 1950s, '60s and the first half of the 1970s."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="03:44" /><Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="04:16" />

The reason was the city's standing: *"Beirut was the Paris of the Middle East. Beirut was the banking center of the Middle East."* Everyone came — *"the CIA was there, the KGB was there, the Israelis were there."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="04:16" />

The detail he adds is the one not usually given: *"but more secretly the French ran the place."* His comparison set is European — *"it was very much like, you know, Vienna or Geneva or London, in that it was a spy center."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="04:16" />

## What ended it

The Lebanese civil war, beginning in the mid-1970s, changed the city's function. Practical access remains part of the problem: *"it's hard to get in and out of Beirut. The airport is unreliable. MEA, Middle East Airlines, is unreliable."* He notes occasions when the landing lights failed and all flights were cancelled.<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="04:48" />

Kiriakou notes he had never been, and was going in three weeks — *"I want to walk the Corniche. I want to try the excellent restaurants."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="05:19" />

## The enormous embassy

Asked whether the huge new American embassy in a small country is a regional CIA headquarters, Kiriakou says no — *"not by a long shot anymore. It could have been once upon a time, but those times have changed."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="05:19" />

The size is a function of fortification, and the history behind it is specific. The US embassy in Beirut *"was blown into rubble in 1983,"* within weeks of the Marine barracks bombing; hundreds of Americans died, there have been multiple embassy bombings, and *"lots of CIA people died."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="05:50" />

Hence the footprint: *"if you really want to seriously fortify a building, you have to do it like big, like an entire city block kind of big. That's how you protect what is deeper inside."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="06:21" />

Asked whether people should be worried, his answer is time-stamped: *"No, I don't think so. Not anymore. In 1975 or 1982 or 1993, I would have given you a different answer — but I think not anymore."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="06:21" />

## Who can be sent there

Beirut is a posting the agency treats as armed by definition, and the rule Kiriakou describes follows from that. Asked by a manager in the [Counterterrorism Center](/wiki/counterterrorism-center) whether he could go, he suggested sending the officer who actually covered Beirut, and was told that the man had beaten his wife and could not go — because an officer who is *accused*, not even convicted, of beating his wife is not allowed to carry a gun, and *"you can't work in Beirut without carrying a gun."*<Cite s="2026-01-19-dead-drop-s1e11-have-human-spies-become-obsolete" t="13:03" /> In another telling of an almost identical exchange the officer who could not be sent had instead been [psychiatrically evacuated](/wiki/psychiatric-evacuation) and was barred from travel.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="18:00" />
