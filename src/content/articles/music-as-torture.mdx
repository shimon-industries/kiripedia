**Music as torture** is the playing of recorded music on indefinite loop as a coercive technique, which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes as long-standing [CIA](/wiki/cia) practice rather than a post-September 11 innovation. Asked whether the agency had ever considered using music on prisoners, his answer was that it had already been done for decades: *"they did that all through the Vietnam War."*<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="20:10" />

The two recordings he names from the later program are the Bee Gees and the theme song from *SpongeBob SquarePants*, both played on loop.<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="20:40" /> His account of why the technique works does not depend on the music being unpleasant. Told that the *SpongeBob* theme is a good song, Kiriakou agreed that he liked it too, and located the damage in the count rather than the material — *"but after you hear it 10,000 times, you start to…"*<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="20:40" />

Music does not appear among the named techniques of the formal [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program, and Kiriakou discusses it in the same register as the [cold cell](/wiki/cold-cell) — practices applied in the field without the Justice Department cover that [waterboarding](/wiki/waterboarding) received.<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="19:05" /><Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="20:40" />
