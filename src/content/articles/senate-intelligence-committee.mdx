The **Senate Intelligence Committee** — formally the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI) — is the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s designated congressional overseer, and in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s telling both the author of the most important document of the torture era and a case study in oversight failure.

## The torture report's method

Kiriakou defends the [Senate torture report](/wiki/senate-torture-report)'s conclusions on the strength of its sourcing: the committee used six million primary-source documents — live, real-time cables that CIA officers wrote from the field to headquarters as the interrogations happened. *"We know firsthand that the torture did not work."*<Cite s="2015-03-02-voices-of-liberty-cia-torture-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-part" t="01:33" /><Cite s="2015-08-18-politics-done-right-politics-done-right" t="15:47" /> Those documents also recorded officers who, ordered to begin torturing, said *"wait a minute, I didn't sign on for this"* — in at least a couple of cases insisting on coming home rather than remain in the field and torture prisoners.<Cite s="2015-11-14-robin-hensel" t="49:27" />

He gives [Dianne Feinstein](/wiki/dianne-feinstein) her due on this one: long known as an unusually friendly chairwoman to the agency, which made the ferocity of her torture-report fight the more striking.<Cite s="2015-05-09-awakeradio1-awakeradio1" t="27:50" /> The eventual McCain-Feinstein legislation formally, officially, and permanently banning torture is the achievement Kiriakou likes to think he played some role in.<Cite s="2015-07-25-splendour-in-the-grass" t="27:45" />

## "Cheerleaders for the CIA"

Kiriakou's structural judgment is harsher: since the middle 1980s, Congress has proven little more than cheerleaders for the CIA. Feinstein, as chairman, *"did nothing to stop CIA malfeasance"* in all her years heading the committee, and the same has held under Republican leadership — members of both the SSCI and the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence see their job as supporting CIA operations. *"We don't need any outside bodies to be supporting CIA operations... we need an organization that can find the truth."*<Cite s="2017-03-09-berlin-gegen-krieg-berlin-gegen-krieg" t="07:56" /><Cite s="2017-03-09-berlin-gegen-krieg-berlin-gegen-krieg" t="08:27" />

The committee also anchors his critique of the official whistleblower channel: boss, Inspector General, General Counsel, then the oversight committees. Thanks to the [Tom Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake) case at NSA and the [Jeffrey Sterling](/wiki/jeffrey-sterling) case at CIA, he says, *"we know that simply does not work."*<Cite s="2015-07-25-splendour-in-the-grass" t="50:12" /><Cite s="2015-10-21-chris-petracco-interview-partial-with-former-cia-case-office" t="10:20" /> He notes the committee credentials of others when it suits the argument — if Bob Graham, a highly respected SSCI member, believes there was official Saudi involvement in 9/11, that is to be taken seriously.<Cite s="2015-10-21-chris-petracco-interview-partial-with-former-cia-case-office" t="05:38" />
