**Iranian suicide drones** are the weapon [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) told viewers to watch on the first day of the 2026 war, breaking into the news reads to say so: *"Another thing we should be we should be looking for, Ted, um is uh is the continuing use of drones."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="32:42" />

## Why they matter

The advantage he identifies is industrial rather than technical. *"One thing that the Iranians are very good at is they build their own suicide drones right there in Iran using Iranian parts, and they can build lots of them."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="32:42" />

Domestic production from domestic components is what makes them immune to interdiction — a supply that cannot be blockaded, in a war where Kiriakou expected everything arriving from Russia or China to face exactly that problem. Stated at its bluntest: *"The Iranians manufacture drones faster than the United States does. That's the future of warfare."*<Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="1:28:30" />

He grounds the claim in the previous autumn: *"Remember all these drones that they sent against against Israel back in October, these were all homemade drones."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="32:42" />

## What "suicide drone" means

Kiriakou defines the term rather than assuming it. *"When I call them suicide drones, it means that they have explosives strapped to them, and they are remotely flown directly into a target where they explode. They're not meant to launch a missile and then return to their base. So, they're they they commit suicide, um so to speak."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="32:42" /><Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="33:12" />

The category is not Iran's invention. Sixteen countries now build drones, he says, and they build suicide drones — the drone itself is the weapon and it is simply flown into the target. Those drones are cheap, small and devastating; it is *"kind of just as good"* as owning a Reaper or a Predator.<Cite s="2025-06-01-austin-and-matt-11-inside-the-cia-whistleblowers-war-crimes-and-blind" t="1:05:02" />

## The price

The economics are the heart of Kiriakou's argument. A suicide drone costs between five and ten thousand dollars to make: *"You just strap explosives onto them, and you fly them into a target."*<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="17:18" /><Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="1:28:30" />

The comparison he draws is with the American equivalent, which he prices at around a hundred and fifty thousand dollars, because it fires missiles and carries cameras and *"all this stuff."* The Iranian ones *"just have GPS and they just go."*<Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="1:29:33" />

## Why air defence fails

The defence problem, on his account, is arithmetic rather than engineering. A system like the Israeli Iron Dome works: it can shoot things out of the air, it can shoot down incoming missiles. *"But you can't shoot down 5,000 incoming drones. You don't have 5,000 missiles."*<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="17:18" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="17:49" />

## Against a ship

The same logic of quantity governs the naval case. A single drone sent against an American warship, he said, will be shot down; the defence fails on volume, because *"a ship has only so many defensive missiles on it."* At five hundred, *"you can't shoot all of them down."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="58:07" /><Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="58:40" />

By July 2026 he had made this the centrepiece of a broader claim that the United States cannot win the war at all, because the nature of warfare has changed. Two aircraft carrier strike groups had been sent, each carrier accompanied by eleven or twelve ships — against an adversary fighting with ten-thousand-dollar drones. *"How in the world are you going to protect an aircraft carrier against suicide drones that are just going to fly into its side?"*<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="16:48" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="17:49" />

## The limits of the weapon

Kiriakou does not present them as unanswerable. Their range is short: they can fly from Iran to Israel, but they cannot reach Diego Garcia.<Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="1:29:02" />

He reads a pair of Iranian missile launches as an experiment in closing that gap. Two missiles were fired and intercepted; they did not need to be, because the Iranians had stripped them of everything but their engines, and the weight saved would have carried them all the way to Diego Garcia. They are experimenting, he says, and a day will come when they can reach Western Europe — *"we're not there yet, but it's coming."*<Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="1:29:02" />

## The consequence for the Gulf

The weapon's real strategic effect, in Kiriakou's assessment, falls on the countries hosting American forces rather than on Iran or the United States. The Gulf Arab states are *"beside themselves"* over it, and having recently visited Kuwait, Abu Dhabi and Dubai — and claiming close contacts in five of the six royal families — he reports them all asking the same question: why are the American bases here?<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="17:49" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="18:19" />

The inventory he lists is the reason the question stings: army bases in Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and Oman; the headquarters of the [Fifth Fleet](/wiki/fifth-fleet) in Bahrain; the largest US Air Force base in the world in Qatar; ground troops, air units and sailors in the United Arab Emirates — and the United States unable to stop Iran attacking any of them. His conclusion is that these governments are beginning to think they should make their own peace with Tehran, and that in their position he would be seriously reassessing the relationship with Washington.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="18:49" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="19:22" />

The October attack on Israel is, on his reading, the same lesson learned in the other direction: [six hundred drones sent, seven through](/wiki/iron-dome), and the seven were the point.
