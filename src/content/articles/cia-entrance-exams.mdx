**The CIA entrance examinations** John Kiriakou sat at the end of the 1980s came in two rounds, and he has described both in unusual detail.<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="03:48" />

## Round one

The first round had three parts. The first was a long multiple-choice test on current events, which he found so easy that *"you don't even have to read the newspaper every day — if you just look at the headline or look at the front page every day without actually reading anything you're still going to do well on this test."* His example: a question asking whether the prime minister of Greece was Andreas Papandreou, Leonid Brezhnev, Mao Zedong or U Thant — *"even if you didn't know you could assume which one was the Greek name and which was the Asian name."*<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="03:48" /><Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="04:20" />

The second was a large fold-out map of the world with every country left blank, to be filled in from memory.<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="04:51" />

The third was the psychological inventory — several thousand items, phrased as statements to be agreed or disagreed with rather than as questions. The one he remembers is *"I like boxing."* He did not care about boxing either way, reasoned that he would probably stop if Mike Tyson were on, and said yes — and then met the same statement again 285 questions later, and again some 415 questions after that. *"So I saw what they were trying to do. It was a psychological exam and they just wanted to see if you were consistent and if you were trying to tell them what you thought they wanted to hear."*<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="05:26" /><Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="05:59" />

## Round two

A call from personnel a couple of weeks later told him he had *"blown the doors off"* the exams. Round two was held in a nondescript building in Virginia, where he faced a psychologist, a psychiatrist and an anthropologist seated together on the other side of a table. The questions were personal — *"can you describe your relationship with your mother"*, *"was your father the disciplinarian of the family"* — and one stayed with him: *"have you ever betrayed a friendship?"*<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="06:31" /><Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="07:06" />

He had no idea what the right answer was. *"I said, oh my gosh, no, I don't think so. I said, let me think about it for a minute. And they said, no, no, that's the answer we were looking for."*<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="07:50" /> The panel then took hair, blood and urine and sent him away; another call weeks later said he had aced those too, and he was sent to headquarters for interviews.<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="07:06" />

His own reading of why he passed is candour plus temperament: he tested *"very highly on the extrovert chart"*, and that, with his professor's recommendation, was what persuaded them.<Cite s="2016-01-12-the-sharin-hour-the-sharin-hour-1-11-16-john-kiriakou-ex-c-i" t="55:02" />
