SecureDrop is a tool pioneered by WikiLeaks for protected document submission. It is an example of the organization's work in source protection, encryption, and privacy. <Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="22:42:00" /> Elsewhere in the same panel discussion, Kiriakou discusses [Meredith Whittaker](/wiki/meredith-whittaker)'s work developing a new business model for the encrypted-messaging app Signal.<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="01:21:44" />

## Confirmed, and the limit of his knowledge

Asked in 2020 to confirm how the system works — a whistleblower deposits material, and the platform delivers it to a newsroom without any link between the person and the material — Kiriakou said that was right, and agreed that it appears on the websites of a number of American outlets as a route for submissions.<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="22:39" /><Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="23:09" /> He also accepted the account that [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange) was among those who built it, along with Aaron Swartz.<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="23:09" />

He declined the follow-up. Asked to weigh SecureDrop against GlobaLeaks and the Dutch platform Publeaks built on it, he said he had never heard of Publeaks, and referred the comparison to [Bill Binney](/wiki/bill-binney) and [Thomas Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake) — *"most definitely Bill Binney and Drake"* would give a detailed answer.<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="23:41" /><Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="24:14" />
