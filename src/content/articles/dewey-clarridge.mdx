**Dewey Clarridge** was a senior CIA operations officer, and [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s assessment of him separates stature from conduct.

*"Dewey Clarridge is a legendary figure in CIA operations. He was a giant from the 1980s. A giant who was prosecuted, actually, for his involvement in the [Iran-Contra](/wiki/iran-contra) affair."*<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="19:22" />

## On the Saudi bomb

Shown an old interview in which Clarridge said flatly that the Saudis already had nuclear weapons, *"several"*, Kiriakou treated the claim as credible on the strength of the man: *"whether you like Dewey Clarridge or not, whether you like Dewey Clarridge's politics or not, he was a serious operator at the CIA. And if he says that the Saudis have a nuclear bomb, I would believe that the Saudis have a nuclear bomb."*<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="20:55" />

His own reasoning runs in parallel: *"one of the first things I learned when I joined the CIA in January of 1990 was that the Saudis had long been seeking a nuclear bomb,"* and *"I always assumed, as Dewey Clarridge did, that the Pakistanis shared those nuclear bombs with the Saudis. I believe that was the quid pro quo."* The detail is set out in [the Saudi nuclear programme](/wiki/saudi-nuclear-program).<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="19:22" /><Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="20:25" />

## On the cocaine

Asked whether Clarridge had been involved, in his official capacity, in the importation of cocaine into the United States, Kiriakou's answer was short: *"I believe that he was. You know, I've researched the daylights out of this."* What he thinks the agency did is set out in [CIA drug facilitation](/wiki/cia-drug-facilitation).<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="20:55" />

## The Saudi bomb claim

Explaining what the parties to [the Mecca Agreement](/wiki/mecca-agreement) bring to it, Kiriakou reaches for Clarridge's last public claim. Noting that Saudi Arabia has *"actively sought nuclear weapons since the 1980s"*, he adds that Clarridge, *"a former deputy director for operations at the CIA, who has since passed away"*, gave an interview on MSNBC shortly before his death *"in which he said that he believed the Saudis already had a nuclear bomb, that they had purchased it from the Pakistanis."*<Cite s="2026-08-11-john-kiriakou-a-shocking-new-alliance-emerges-in-the-middle" t="25:05" /><Cite s="2026-08-11-john-kiriakou-a-shocking-new-alliance-emerges-in-the-middle" t="25:36" />

Kiriakou reports the claim as Clarridge's belief rather than endorsing it, and uses it to size the pact: Pakistan is a declared nuclear power, and if Clarridge was right, the agreement binds two.<Cite s="2026-08-11-john-kiriakou-a-shocking-new-alliance-emerges-in-the-middle" t="25:05" /><Cite s="2026-08-11-john-kiriakou-a-shocking-new-alliance-emerges-in-the-middle" t="25:36" />
