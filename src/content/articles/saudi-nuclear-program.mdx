The **Saudi nuclear program** predates [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s career: when he arrived at the [CIA](/wiki/cia) in 1990, Saudi Arabia's pursuit of the ability to enrich uranium was already old news, the kingdom having sought nuclear power since the 1980s.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="54:36" />

Kiriakou treats the stated rationale as implausible on its own terms. Saudi Arabia is the sunniest country in the world and has little solar generation; it is windy in the desert and there is little wind power; it has large bodies of water on two sides and little tidal power. "Why nuclear?"<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="55:07" /> The conventional wisdom, he says, has always been that the Saudis want a nuclear weapon — and senior former CIA officers have claimed over the years that they already have one, purchased from the Pakistanis.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="55:38" />

He raised the subject in July 2026 on reporting that the United States and Saudi Arabia had agreed a nuclear power deal, describing it as a story breaking very slowly that most mainstream outlets were not really examining.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="54:36" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="55:38" />

## The quid pro quo, and the missing half

Kiriakou dates his own knowledge of Saudi intent to his first days at the agency: *"one of the first things I learned when I joined the CIA in January of 1990 was that the Saudis had long been seeking a nuclear bomb."*<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="19:22" />

The funding relationship he describes is exceptional in scale: *"the Saudis are more generous with Pakistan than with any other country on the planet — to the point where the city of Faisalabad, a city of some 7 or 8 million people in east-central Pakistan, was named after Saudi King Faisal because of his generosity. The Saudis gave the Pakistanis as much money as they needed to develop a nuclear bomb, as a counter to the Indians."*<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="19:54" />

What he infers from it: *"I always assumed, as [Dewey Clarridge](/wiki/dewey-clarridge) did, that the Pakistanis shared those nuclear bombs with the Saudis. I believe that was the quid pro quo."*<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="20:25" />

He then names the part he cannot answer, and does not pretend to: *"the question then is, do they have a delivery system? Do they have a missile capable of carrying a nuclear device? I think the answer is probably no — but impossible to say."*<Cite s="2026-02-09-judge-napolitano-judging-john-kiriakou-how-rogue-is-us-intelligence" t="20:25" />
