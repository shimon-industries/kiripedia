**Dubai as Iran's window** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s answer to a viewer who asked whether hitting Dubai hard would benefit Iran, given how much American interest was concentrated there during the 2026 war.

Kiriakou's answer was negative and immediate. *"I think no and I'll tell you why because that's where Iran does its business."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="03:06" />

## The function of the city

The reason he gives is economic dependence rather than sentiment. *"Iran I mean Dubai is chock-full of Iranian businessmen and that's Iran's outlet to the western world. That's how Iran evades sanctions by having businesses in Dubai. So I think no. Hitting Dubai is not going to help Iran at all."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="03:06" />

On this account Dubai is not a target that Iran is restrained from hitting by fear of retaliation, but an asset Iran holds inside the opposing coalition — the place through which a sanctioned economy reaches the markets that sanction it.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="03:06" />

## What was hit anyway

Kiriakou's own inventory of the damage Iran managed to inflict includes Dubai twice over. Iranian forces *"fired rockets at the Jebel Ali the the the free port in Dubai,"* damaged the airports at Manama and Kuwait, fired a rocket at the Saudi oil fields that was intercepted, and damaged a couple of hotels in Dubai.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="02:34" />

His general assessment of that campaign is that it was slight. Asked whether Iran was causing damage anywhere, his answer was *"Not that I've seen"* — and *"there's some damage but absolutely nothing like what's being inflicted on Iran."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="02:34" />

The distinction he draws is between the hotels, struck to frighten tourists and empty the Gulf economies, and the city's commercial machinery, which Iran needs working. The strikes on [the Gulf hotels](/wiki/iranian-strikes-on-gulf-hotels) are, in his reading, calibrated to damage a neighbour's economy without severing Iran's own lifeline.<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="04:45" />

## The wider judgement

The restraint fits Kiriakou's overall reading of Iranian strategy in the war. Iran is not trying to win: *"all they have to do is survive."* An economy that has to outlast a bombing campaign cannot afford to destroy the one port through which it still trades.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="02:34" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="11:30" />
