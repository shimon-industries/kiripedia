**Blackmail in agent recruitment** is a subject [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) addresses from the training room rather than from operations: what [CIA](/wiki/cia) career trainees were explicitly told, and where he thinks the teaching stops being descriptive.

## What the instructors said

*"In my training, I was told the same thing. When you're trying to recruit an asset, you want that recruitment to be over the long term. And in order to have a successful long-term recruitment, you need to be able to trust each other. Even if you don't like each other, you have to at least be able to trust each other."*<Cite s="2025-11-20-american-exception-cia-veterans-deep-state-w-barry-eisler" t="05:46" />

The rule followed from that: *"my instructors were very clear in saying that we cannot get long-term working relationships going on the right track if we start with blackmail. It's just not something that we would do."*<Cite s="2025-11-20-american-exception-cia-veterans-deep-state-w-barry-eisler" t="06:19" /> The justification offered was operational — that blackmail is an unreliable and high-risk tool for managing assets — rather than ethical.<Cite s="2025-11-20-american-exception-cia-veterans-deep-state-w-barry-eisler" t="05:46" />

## The exceptions he volunteers

Kiriakou does not present the teaching as a complete account of agency behaviour. He concedes the historical case immediately: *"certainly they did it in the past, pre-[Church Committee](/wiki/church-committee)."*<Cite s="2025-11-20-american-exception-cia-veterans-deep-state-w-barry-eisler" t="06:19" />

And he separates recruitment from suppression. Where the objective is not cooperation but inaction — where you simply need a target not to do something — *"yeah, sure. Blackmail threats."* He adds the further step: *"maybe even you outsource it to another intelligence service, another friendly intelligence service, uh, who might not be as friendly as you are."*<Cite s="2025-11-20-american-exception-cia-veterans-deep-state-w-barry-eisler" t="06:49" />

His summary of the doctrine as day-to-day policy is unchanged, and carefully bounded: *"as a matter of day-to-day policy, we were told that blackmail wasn't going to be done."*<Cite s="2025-11-20-american-exception-cia-veterans-deep-state-w-barry-eisler" t="06:49" />
