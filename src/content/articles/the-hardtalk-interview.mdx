**The HardTalk interview** was the first [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) gave after his release from [prison](/wiki/fci-loretto). He describes the BBC programme as *"a kind of a hard-hitting mean-spirited talk show"* whose host *"kept trying to provoke me."*<Cite s="2023-09-22-ex-cia-officer-exposes-the-torture-program" t="25:39" />

The provocation that worked was an accusation of unrepentance: *"Well, you're not showing any remorse for the crime that you committed."* The answer has since become his standing formulation of the case against his own conviction.<Cite s="2023-09-22-ex-cia-officer-exposes-the-torture-program" t="26:12" />

> *"No, because I have no remorse. It's not a crime to reveal a crime. The crime was committed by the CIA. The crime was committed by the torturers. I'll never apologize for what I did."*

He followed it immediately with an appeal to authority rather than to sentiment: *"John McCain got up on the floor of the United States Senate to thank me for providing a public service to the American people. I'm glad that I did what I did."* The force of the citation, in his telling, is that [McCain](/wiki/john-mccain) had himself been tortured.<Cite s="2023-09-22-ex-cia-officer-exposes-the-torture-program" t="26:12" /><Cite s="2023-09-22-ex-cia-officer-exposes-the-torture-program" t="26:43" />

The same exchange produces his flattest statement of what the [Espionage Act](/wiki/espionage-act) charge amounted to: *"talking to the New York Times and the ABC News is not espionage. Sorry, it's just not."*<Cite s="2023-09-22-ex-cia-officer-exposes-the-torture-program" t="26:43" />
