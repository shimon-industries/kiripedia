**The 2026 World Cup final** was won by Spain, and [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) discussed it the following day — both the match and the diplomacy around the trophy.

## The match

Spain won *"in something of a dirty game,"* and Kiriakou is careful about where he places the blame: not on the Spaniards but on the Argentines whom they defeated. There was even a red card, which he notes is a big deal.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="30:43" />

## The presentation

[Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) — a football fan, and by Kiriakou's account someone who had exerted a lot of influence over FIFA — was present to hand the trophy to the winners.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="31:15" />

Kiriakou calls the moment a little bit awkward. The United States was the host, and the presentation came after weeks of Trump arguing publicly with Spain's leadership. Spain had some fun with it: the Spanish media cropped him out of all of the celebration photographs, which Kiriakou found funny.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="31:55" />

## The reading of Trump

Asked afterwards whether there was tension between him and Spain, Trump said he had spoken to Spain, congratulated them on a great team, and had no tension with anybody.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="32:27" />

Kiriakou, who says he met Trump once briefly and has watched him for many years, takes the answer at face value in a specific sense. So much of what Trump does in public is show — showmanship — and when he says there is no tension with Spain, Kiriakou thinks he genuinely believes it. The Spanish may disagree; Trump treats it all as part of the game of politics, with no hard feelings.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="32:27" />
