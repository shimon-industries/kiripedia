**Seven thousand letters** is the figure [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) gives for the correspondence he conducted during his 23 months at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto): about 7,000 letters to 675 different people.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:38" /><Cite s="2026-06-15-dead-drop-s2e5-the-abyss" t="20:08" />

## The postcard

Kiriakou dates the decision precisely. He had been in prison about four hours when mail call came, and a woman from Ringgold, Georgia sent him a postcard of a rose with a very nice note on the back.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:04" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:38" />

His reaction was that he did not even know the person and that it was a thoughtful gesture, and he made a decision at that moment that he would respond to every piece of mail he received — with no idea, he says, that this would come to 7,000 letters to 675 people over the next 23 months.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:38" />

## What it produced

Kiriakou says the correspondents became friends and have remained so ever since 2013, in constant touch. His summary of the experience is simply that it was wonderful.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:38" />

It also solved the central problem of the sentence. Where most prisoners passed the time by working out and watching television — the prison, he says, is Groundhog Day, every day just like every other — Kiriakou was busy answering mail, and it kept him very busy.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="13:56" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="16:13" />

He places it alongside reading and writing as his defence against [depression in prison](/wiki/prison-depression), and says he read well over a hundred books in the same 23 months.<Cite s="2026-06-15-dead-drop-s2e5-the-abyss" t="20:08" />

## The book

The same habit produced *[Doing Time Like a Spy](/wiki/doing-time-like-a-spy)*. Kiriakou says the book started off as a joke and that he did not even intend it to be a book — he simply thought he would write down some of the more ridiculous and more absurd things that happened.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="16:13" /> He wrote it in longhand on legal pads, one word at a time, and it won two literary awards.<Cite s="2026-06-15-dead-drop-s2e5-the-abyss" t="20:38" />

Separately from the personal correspondence, Kiriakou's public newsletter from inside became [the Letters from Loretto](/wiki/letter-from-loretto), which he sent out through his attorney [Jesselyn Radack](/wiki/jesselyn-radack) as [legal mail](/wiki/legal-mail).<Cite s="2026-06-22-dead-drop-s2e6-use-your-words" t="20:15" />
