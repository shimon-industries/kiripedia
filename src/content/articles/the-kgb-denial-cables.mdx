**The KGB denial cables** are two messages [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites as proof of an unwritten rule governing how the American and Soviet services treat each other's officers.

## The agreement

*"We've always had a gentleman's agreement with the Russians that we don't harm or interfere with their people, and they won't harm and interfere with our people."*<Cite s="2025-12-15-afshin-rattansi-s-going-un-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-reveals-all-o" t="25:00" />

## Athens, 1975

Kiriakou read the traffic himself. Recalling the reports from the murder of the [CIA](/wiki/cia) station chief in Athens, [Dick Welch](/wiki/richard-welch), on 23 December 1975: *"the very first cable that we received was from Moscow, and it was the KGB saying, we promise it wasn't us."*<Cite s="2025-12-15-afshin-rattansi-s-going-un-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-reveals-all-o" t="25:00" />

## Dallas, 1963

He gives the same account of the assassination of John F. Kennedy on 22 November 1963: *"the very first message that the CIA received was from the KGB saying, we promise it wasn't us. There's this gentleman's agreement that has always been respected."*<Cite s="2025-12-15-afshin-rattansi-s-going-un-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-reveals-all-o" t="25:33" />

## Why it still matters

Kiriakou raised the agreement to answer a practical question about the war in Ukraine: what happens when Russian troops reach the [CIA](/wiki/cia) bases reported to be sited along the Russian border. His expectation is limited and specific — *"sure, they might overrun CIA bases and they'll likely expel those CIA officers, but I wouldn't expect much more."*<Cite s="2025-12-15-afshin-rattansi-s-going-un-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-reveals-all-o" t="25:33" />

The interviewer's response, that he was not sure the Russians were gentlemen, went unanswered as the segment ran out of time.<Cite s="2025-12-15-afshin-rattansi-s-going-un-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-reveals-all-o" t="25:33" />
