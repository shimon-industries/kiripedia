**Muammar Gaddafi** ruled Libya for four decades — and his end supplies [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) with one of his most-repeated lessons about American promises.

## The Libya model

In February 2003, Kiriakou recounts, [Steve Kappes](/wiki/steve-kappes) — then the CIA's associate deputy director for operations — made a secret trip to Libya and told Gaddafi the United States was about to attack Iraq and overthrow [Saddam Hussein](/wiki/saddam-hussein); if Gaddafi didn't want to meet the same fate, he would have to turn over all his weapons of mass destruction. *"And that's exactly what he did."*<Cite s="2025-05-10-neutrality-studies-this-is-what-they-don-t-tell-you-about-iran-h" t="06:42" /><Cite s="2025-05-10-neutrality-studies-this-is-what-they-don-t-tell-you-about-iran-h" t="07:12" />

Then came 2011: Gaddafi — disarmed — was overthrown and *"lynched by his own people at the insistence of the United States."* The Iranians watched, Kiriakou says, and drew the only rational conclusion: develop a homegrown weapons industry and never trust the deal. The gap between what the United States says and what it does is the whole story.<Cite s="2025-05-10-neutrality-studies-this-is-what-they-don-t-tell-you-about-iran-h" t="07:44" />

## The serial prediction

Kiriakou has since folded Libya into a second argument, in which Gaddafi's removal is not an isolated error but the middle term of a pattern he attributes to [Benjamin Netanyahu](/wiki/benjamin-netanyahu). Prefacing it as *"maybe be a little controversial"*, he points to video of Netanyahu testifying before Congress in 2002 that [Saddam Hussein](/wiki/saddam-hussein) was an existential threat to Israel and to the United States, and that overthrowing him would bring peace to the Middle East. *"And then there wasn't."*<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="09:52" />

The same testimony followed, he says, in 2008 or 2009, with the name changed: *"If we overthrow Muammar Gaddafi and the Libyan government, they're an existential threat to the United States and to Israel. We'll have peace in the Middle East."* Kiriakou's verdict on the outcome is four words long — *"And we did that, and it just made everything worse."* A year before he spoke, the same argument was made a third time about the Iranian government.<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="10:24" />

He states the pattern flatly when he sets out the three claims in sequence — Saddam, Gaddafi, then Iran, each removal promised to bring peace to the Middle East: *"Nothing that he tells us is true. Nothing."*<Cite s="2026-06-08-the-sharpe-exchange-reveals-the-dark-reality-of-intel-ops" t="27:56" /> His objection is to the price rather than the prediction: *"We do all of Israel's dirty work for it. We kill thousands, tens of thousands of people, and what do we get out of it? Nothing."*<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="10:54" />

The argument closes back on the Libya model. Having laid out the three overthrows, Kiriakou asks what a leader of Iran can rationally do to protect himself, and answers that the only thing available is to build a nuclear weapon — noting that nobody is having a conversation about North Korea.<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="10:54" /> Gaddafi disarmed and was killed; the lesson he draws is the one he says Tehran drew.

## "A terrible policy error"

Kiriakou is unsentimental about the man — *"a very bad guy"* — but the policy verdict is severe: Washington had a healthy working relationship with Gaddafi, who hated al-Qaeda as much as America did. *"Flipping sides at the last minute... to overthrow Gaddafi was a terrible policy error. Look at the place now — it's literally anarchy."*<Cite s="2024-09-18-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-reluctant-spy" t="26:34" /><Cite s="2024-09-18-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-reluctant-spy" t="27:06" /> Libya's aftermath — shifting desert borders, tribal society, gun-running from the first days of the uprising — became a no-man's-land.<Cite s="2024-09-18-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-reluctant-spy" t="35:25" /> He cited the Libya disaster in real time as the reason not to repeat the play against Assad,<Cite s="2015-12-14-arn-menconi-my-interview-w-whistleblower-john-kiriakou" t="22:41" /> and notes the speculation that Gaddafi's gold-dinar project — a threat to the dollar — was part of why Libya was destroyed.<Cite s="2019-09-30-the-jimmy-dore-show-real-reason-trump-sent-troops-to-saudi-oil" t="12:34" />

Gaddafi also features in Kiriakou's standing complaint about the "crazy" reflex: Saddam was crazy, Gaddafi was crazy, bin Laden was crazy, Arafat was crazy — *"everybody that we don't like is crazy. They're not crazy."*<Cite s="2022-07-24-scheerpost-scheer-intelligence-kiriakou-on-biden-s-grove" t="44:46" /><Cite s="2022-07-23-the-darkened-hour" t="07:18" />
