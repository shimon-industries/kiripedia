**Will Hurd** is a former CIA case officer, U.S. congressman and OpenAI board member. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he mentored Hurd — then a junior case officer with *"a gift"* for convincing people to commit espionage — before Hurd resigned to run for Congress in a court-mandated majority-Hispanic district, which he won three times.<Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="52:43" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="53:15" /> Hurd briefly ran for president in 2024, gave up his seat, and joined the board of OpenAI; Kiriakou offers him as an example of the intelligence and technology worlds coming together, keeping *"one foot in the game and one foot in business."*<Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="53:46" /> In a separate interview Kiriakou gave a fuller account of the same mentorship, calling Hurd *"an awesome case officer, as honest as the day is long"* despite their differing politics, and recalling Hurd announcing his resignation to run for Congress; Kiriakou notes Hurd — who is half Black, half white — won three times in the same Supreme Court-mandated majority-Hispanic district before his 2024 presidential run and subsequent seat on the OpenAI board.<Cite s="2026-04-29-jay-dyer-they-really-did-this" t="1:39:57" /><Cite s="2026-04-29-jay-dyer-they-really-did-this" t="1:40:28" /><Cite s="2026-04-29-jay-dyer-they-really-did-this" t="1:40:58" /> Kiriakou has repeated the mentorship claim in at least two further interviews, in each case calling Hurd an *"awesome"* or *"gifted"* officer with *"a gift for recruiting people,"* who left the CIA after about six years, was elected to Congress three times from the same majority-Hispanic Texas district, ran briefly for president, and now sits on the board of OpenAI and chairs the board of another AI company.<Cite s="2026-01-26-tin-foil-hat-w-sam-tripoli" t="15:04" /><Cite s="2026-07-05-the-bad-news-program" t="1:08:35" />

## An intelligence placement?

Asked whether Hurd's political and tech-industry trajectory could be an intelligence-community placement rather than an independent career, Kiriakou says he doubts it, though he does not rule it out entirely: *"Is that the just embedding an operative there? ... It's possible, but I would say no."* His reasoning is that Hurd went to work first for a former CIA boss who had already left the agency to make money in the private sector — ordinary career succession rather than a run operation.<Cite s="2026-01-26-tin-foil-hat-w-sam-tripoli" t="16:38" />
