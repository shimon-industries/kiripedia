**Clive Stafford Smith** is the British human rights lawyer who represents [Aafia Siddiqui](/wiki/aafia-siddiqui), and whom [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) introduced to his audience in September 2025 after Stafford Smith had spent that morning at Kiriakou's house recording an episode of his Deep Focus podcast on the case.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="45:20" />

## What Kiriakou says about him

*"Listen to what an awesome guy he is,"* Kiriakou said before listing the reasons. Stafford Smith is *"a member of the Order of the British Empire"*, an honour given, in Kiriakou's account, *"because of the work that he's done on human rights."*<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:15" />

The two facts Kiriakou dwells on are financial and statistical. *"He has never charged a client a single dollar in all of his life. He just lives on donations."* And: *"He's represented 66 [Guantanamo](/wiki/guantanamo-bay) detainees and he's gotten every one of them released. All 66."*<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:15" />

## The Siddiqui case

It is Stafford Smith who supplied Kiriakou with most of what he now says about [Siddiqui](/wiki/aafia-siddiqui): the sentence being the longest ever handed down in the United States for an attempted murder in which no weapon was discharged; the photograph showing the bullet holes in the tent canvas weeks before she was caught; and [Hamid Karzai](/wiki/hamid-karzai)'s admission about what became of her children.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="47:59" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="48:29" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:03" />

It was Stafford Smith who travelled to Afghanistan, befriended the Taliban government, and had them instruct the deposed Karzai to receive him; who found the two older children and reunited them with Siddiqui's sister; and who registered the sister's DNA with the commercial ancestry databases and had the missing infant's photograph aged by artificial intelligence, in the hope that an eighteen-year-old somewhere will recognise himself.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="49:33" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:03" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:06" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:37" />

He is suing [the CIA](/wiki/cia) and the FBI knowing he will lose both, on the theory that the publicity is the remedy.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:37" />

## The letters

Stafford Smith told Kiriakou that Siddiqui, held [in solitary confinement](/wiki/solitary-confinement) since 2008, still responds to letters, and that he will deliver them to her. Kiriakou passed on the address on air.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="52:41" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:15" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:45" />

His assessment of her prospects is the bleak one Kiriakou repeats without softening: *"her only hope is that President Trump commutes her sentence and lets her go home to Pakistan."*<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:45" />
