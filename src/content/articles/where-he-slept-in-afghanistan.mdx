**Where he slept in Afghanistan** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of his own lodging across many trips to the country, given on 22 October 2025 in answer to a viewer who asked, after hearing about the scorpions of Baja, whether Afghanistan had been more comfortable.

## The premise of the question

His first answer was about fauna rather than beds. *"I've been to Afghanistan a bunch of times. The last few times were, you know, bite-free."* There were, he noted, no tarantulas; he saw one snake; and he saw *"hundreds of foxes, desert foxes. They come out of their dens when the sun goes down. You can see their eyes shining off in the distance."* Marmots were plentiful in both countries.<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="09:25" /><Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="09:55" />

## The progression

The accommodation improved as the American presence hardened, and Kiriakou lays it out as three stages.

*"The first couple of times I went to Afghanistan, I just slept in a tent with everybody else."*<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="09:55" />

The first improvement was marginal and he mocks it. *"I mean, there was once where I actually had my own tent, so I was, you know, living high on the hog."*<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="09:55" />

The second came with infrastructure. *"And then, once we got a serious foothold there, I'd stay in the officers' quarters at Bagram."*<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="09:55" /><Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="10:27" />

## The civilian trip

The last category is different in kind, because he was no longer working for the government. *"And then, when I went as a private citizen to get those little kids out who had starred in [The Kite Runner](/wiki/kite-runner-rescue-mission), I stayed at one of the big Afghan hotels."*<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="10:27" />

His assessment of it is deliberately unremarkable: *"and it was like any three-star hotel that I stayed in anywhere else."*<Cite s="2025-10-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="10:27" />

## Why the inventory is useful

The sequence is a compressed history of the American war told through where one man was allowed to put his bed. The shared tent belongs to the improvised early period; the officers' quarters at Bagram to the years of a large, permanent, walled American installation; the ordinary hotel to a country in which a foreign civilian could check in and sleep in a room like any other.

It is also a rare instance of Kiriakou answering a comfort question about a war zone without irony. He was more troubled, on his own account, by a month at [a resort in Baja](/wiki/the-resort-with-no-air-conditioning) than by any of it, a comparison he makes without apparent embarrassment.
