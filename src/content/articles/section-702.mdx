**Section 702** is the section of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act under which the U.S. government conducts warrantless electronic surveillance. Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) it is *"a section that directly takes our constitutional freedoms away from us in the name of national security."* It is the authority that NSA whistleblower [Tom Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake) and Edward Snowden first made publicly known in the summer of 2013.<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="40:02" /><Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="40:32" />

## Scale of misuse

Per Kiriakou, citing the Brennan Center for Justice: the FBI has made *"over 200,000"* illegal queries against the Section 702 database, including a recent two-year window in which there were approximately 78,000 misuse queries.<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="40:32" /><Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="41:04" />

The [Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court](/wiki/fisa-court) is, in Kiriakou's framing, a rubber stamp: of ~34,000 FISA warrant applications, estimates range from 11 to 120 denied, *"hundredths of one percent."* Kiriakou's go-to example of the cost: *"Ask Carter Page how that worked out for him."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="41:37" />

## The biennial renewal pattern

Section 702 comes up for reauthorization every two years. Each cycle, per Kiriakou, *"a couple more people will go over to the no side, but not in any numbers to actually mean something."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="47:55" />

## The Trump reversal

Donald Trump campaigned against Section 702 in 2016. After taking office, per Kiriakou, Trump said *"he talked to three dozen generals and admirals and every single one of them said we have to have 702 to keep America safe. So I support the reauthorization of Section 702."* Kiriakou: *"Okay, that's exactly the opposite of what you ran on."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="46:52" /><Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="47:24" />

## Why it persists

Kiriakou's diagnosis: there are no replacement systems being demanded, because there is no political will. *"How 702 keeps us safe when we have other electronic means to keep us just as safe without spying on American citizens unconstitutionally, nobody's ever explained."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="47:24" />

## "No expectation of privacy": the mechanics of interception

Discussing [NSA surveillance](/wiki/nsa-surveillance), Kiriakou has described Section 702's practical effect using shorthand he attributes to the Patriot Act: if any party on a phone call, text thread, or email is a foreign national, every party to that communication loses any expectation of privacy, and the government can intercept the entire exchange without a warrant. *"If somebody else on that thread... was a foreign national, you have no expectation of privacy and the government can intercept everything."*<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="10:00" /> He adds that the NSA then passes such intercepts to the FBI directly, with no court in the loop: *"NSA not just takes it and holds it, they offer it up to the FBI... there are no courts involved in any of this."*<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="11:02" />

## Metadata purchasing as a parallel mechanism

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has described the commercial purchase of metadata as a surveillance mechanism that operates in parallel to, and effectively bypasses, the warrant-based framework that Section 702 nominally requires for targeting American persons. Where Section 702 demands a court-approved targeting determination, government agencies can instead simply purchase bulk metadata from social media companies, which have already acquired ownership of that data through user terms of service.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="31:14" />

Kiriakou described this as a legislative gap rather than a legal violation: *"There's no legal prohibition on purchasing it."* He called for Congress to address the practice, noting it has developed over the past twenty years without any statutory response.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="33:20" />

## The price of confirmation

Kiriakou has used the section as a test of what the confirmation process extracts from reformers. In February 2025 he identified it as the point on which [Tulsi Gabbard](/wiki/tulsi-gabbard) was *"really getting tripped up"*: she agreed, in his account, that warrantless surveillance *"is wrong. It should be illegal. It should be unconstitutional"*, and yet had to tell Republican senators in private meetings that she supported renewal, which he says she does not.<Cite s="2025-02-06-covertaction-magazine-former-cia-counter-terrorism-specialist-john-fYN9zL" t="00:06" /><Cite s="2025-02-06-covertaction-magazine-former-cia-counter-terrorism-specialist-john-fYN9zL" t="00:37" />

His hope was that the concession would prove tactical and be reversed from inside the office, *"a very Washington thing to do."*<Cite s="2025-02-06-covertaction-magazine-former-cia-counter-terrorism-specialist-john-fYN9zL" t="01:07" />
