**The Egyptian berm** is the earth barrier Egypt constructed along its frontier with the Gaza Strip, which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites as evidence that Cairo anticipated an Israeli attempt to force the Palestinian population of Gaza into Sinai.

## The pressure and the refusal

As [the famine](/wiki/gaza-famine) deepened in July 2025, Egypt came under what Kiriakou called *"real pressure today to open the border"*. Its answer was categorical: *"They have said that they're not opening the border under any circumstances."* The reason he gives is not indifference but complicity, *"if they open the border, they will be complicit with the ethnic cleansing."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:05" />

He states the resulting dilemma without resolving it: *"Do you just let people starve, or do you jump in on the side of the Israelis in cleansing Gaza?"*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:36" />

## What the berm is for

Kiriakou's reading of the barrier reverses the obvious assumption about which direction it faces. *"The Egyptians aren't stupid. That's why they built that berm over a year ago,"* he says, because *"they're worried that the Israelis will throw open the border and then, you know, they won't be able to resist."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:36" />

The constraint he identifies is on the Egyptian army rather than on Egyptian policy. Confronted with a mass movement of civilians pushed toward the crossing, Egyptian troops *"aren't going to want to and will not shoot two million people"*, so the barrier does the work the soldiers would not do.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:36" />
