**The Arlington house** is the property [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) uses as his standing illustration of American housing costs — a small house bought with hazard pay, sold at a profit he found startling at the time, and now worth a figure he treats as evidence that a generation has been shut out.

## Bought with hazard pay

The purchase followed his return from [Pakistan](/wiki/pakistan-station-2002). *"I remember when I got back from Pakistan, I had so much money from overtime and danger pay and language differential and post differential and per diem. I bought a house. I bought a house, a little one."*<Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="44:58" />

He is precise about how little. *"It was 1,200 square feet on three floors. That's how small it was. And I paid — I still remember — I paid 3.47 for it. 347,000. I sold it two years later for 721, and now it's a million bucks. A 1,200-square-foot house. A million dollars."*<Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="45:28" />

The benchmark he and his then-wife used at the time was a newspaper story. *"My ex and I at the time marveled over this Washington Post article — and this was like, I say, 20 years ago — that Arlington had sold its first million dollar house. And I said, can you imagine that? A million dollars for a house. It's just insane."*<Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="46:02" />

## The teardown

A second telling, given on the same programme in January 2026, describes a different property and a different stage of life. *"Twenty years ago I was middle middle class, my new wife was middle middle class, and we bought a house in a middle middle class area of Arlington, Virginia. We tore it down and we built a nice house in which to raise our family."*<Cite s="2026-01-26-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="39:37" />

He prices the same street twice, and not identically. In November 2025 he gave the Arlington average as *"2 million"* and his own neighbourhood as *"3.2 million"*, describing the area as *"no nicer than any other neighborhood — just a normal, middle class, middle neighborhood."*<Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="46:02" /><Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="46:33" /> In January 2026 he gave the teardown figure as *"$2.4 million"* and the built-house figure again as *"3.2 million."*<Cite s="2026-01-26-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="40:07" />

## What it costs him now

The conclusion he draws is personal before it is political. *"I can't afford to live here. I have two housemates and we live in a townhouse. Otherwise, I could never hope to live here."*<Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="46:33" />

And it is generational. He returns repeatedly to a conversation with his third son: *"he knows that he will never again live in Arlington, Virginia. It's not possible."*<Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="44:58" /> The January version is blunter still — *"my children will never be able to live in the neighborhood in which they grew up. It's not possible. And it's not going to turn around."*<Cite s="2026-01-26-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="40:07" />

He dates the inflection to a single morning: *"as soon as the planes hit those towers and the Pentagon, everybody started getting rich in the Washington area."*<Cite s="2026-01-26-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="40:38" /> Absent a change, he says, the arithmetic ends badly — *"we're headed for an economic catastrophe where an entire generation is cut out"* — and he adds that he thinks the country is already there.<Cite s="2025-11-05-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-1mebPn" t="46:33" />
