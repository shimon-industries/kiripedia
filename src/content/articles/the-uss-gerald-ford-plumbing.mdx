**The USS Gerald Ford plumbing failure** came to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) twice, first as a news item, then as a viewer's allegation, two days before the United States attacked Iran.

The viewer's version was specific: that sailors were putting their clothes into the toilets to back them up, so that the ship would have to leave station rather than wait for war with Iran.<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="37:57" />

Kiriakou had already seen the underlying report. *"There was a piece on Fox, I think it was, day before yesterday about the USS Gerald Ford having such a significant plumbing problem where there's human [waste] like up to your ankles, literally. That the only thing they can do to take care of it is to turn around and go back to Norfolk."*<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="37:57" /><Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="38:27" />

## The suspicion

What he added was that the viewer's reading had occurred to him independently. *"And I wondered when when this came out 2 days ago if it was sabotage."*<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="38:27" />

Neither host produced evidence, and both left it where it stood, *"Sabotage. It sounds like it. Sure seems like it."*<Cite s="2026-02-25-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="38:27" /> The corpus records the suspicion as a suspicion; no later confirmation appears in the source material.

The episode matters mainly for what it says about Kiriakou's reading of the pre-war mood: he treated deliberate crew action to avoid an Iran deployment as plausible enough to raise unprompted, in the same broadcast in which he judged that [the American military's own preference was to keep waiting](/wiki/the-white-house-tip) until more assets were in place.
