The **Bahrain Intifada** was a Shia uprising against Bahrain's Sunni [Al Khalifa](/wiki/isa-bin-salman-al-khalifa) monarchy that began roughly two months into [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s 1994 [Bahrain posting](/wiki/bahrain-1994-1996-posting), when a bombing at Bahrain's Ministry of Labor set off what became known as the first Intifada, Arabic for "uprising."<Cite s="2025-11-24-dead-drop-s1e5-singin-in-bahrain" t="08:33" />

## Warning and trigger

Kiriakou learned of brewing unrest from Indian journalist Indira Chand of the *Gulf Daily News* (later a World Bank spokesperson), who told him, off the phone lines both of them assumed were bugged, that *"there is a civil uprising, bubbling just beneath the surface and it's ready to explode."*<Cite s="2025-11-24-dead-drop-s1e5-singin-in-bahrain" t="08:33" /> Kiriakou attributes the unrest to Shia unemployment combined with the government importing Sunni foreign workers, including Indians, for jobs, a policy of demographic dilution that had already been fueling resentment. The government responded to unrest with executions, which Kiriakou says had previously been very rare in Bahrain.<Cite s="2025-11-24-dead-drop-s1e5-singin-in-bahrain" t="09:05" />

## Abdul Amir Al-Jamri

Kiriakou identifies Abdul Amir Al-Jamri as the uprising's leader. The Bahraini government first sent police into Shia villages, who found themselves outgunned, and then the military with armored personnel carriers and tanks.<Cite s="2025-11-24-dead-drop-s1e5-singin-in-bahrain" t="11:09" /> Kiriakou had first met Al-Jamri in 1981, on an official CIA trip, when a junior political officer, who later became U.S. Ambassador to Kuwait, brought him along to interview Al-Jamri for a human rights report; Bahrain's prime minister then had that officer expelled from the country for it, telling the ambassador he wanted him out "like today."<Cite s="2025-11-24-dead-drop-s1e5-singin-in-bahrain" t="11:42" />

By the mid-1990s uprising, Kiriakou says he was literally the only person at the U.S. embassy in Bahrain who had personally met Al-Jamri, per Ambassador David Ransom, *"nobody knew as much about Bahrain"* as Kiriakou did.<Cite s="2025-11-24-dead-drop-s1e5-singin-in-bahrain" t="12:47" />

## Government crackdown and Saudi anxiety

Kiriakou, serving as the U.S. embassy's human rights officer during the uprising, says the Bahraini government responded with extrajudicial killings, simply disappearing people.<Cite s="2017-06-06-disruption-network-lab" t="12:54" /> He says Saudi Arabia has long feared that unrest among Bahrain's Shia population would spread across the causeway to Saudi Arabia's own Shia-majority Eastern Province, precisely where the kingdom's oil is, driving Riyadh's preemptive instinct to arrest or kill Shia activists there before they could "get ideas about democracy and human rights."<Cite s="2024-12-21-not-a-grayman" t="1:18:01" />
