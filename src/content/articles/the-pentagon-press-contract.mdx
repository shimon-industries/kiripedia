**The Pentagon press contract** is an undertaking [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says was put to every news outlet covering the Department of Defense a few weeks before late July 2026, and which he describes himself as appalled by.

## The terms

Every outlet covering the Pentagon had to sign a contract saying that they would not publish any information that had not been officially given to them by the Pentagon.<Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="10:55" />

Kiriakou's summary of the effect is that the only thing an outlet could publish would be propaganda personally vetted by the office of the Secretary of Defense — and that this makes investigative journalism illegal.<Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="10:55" />

## The legal theory behind it

The mechanism he identifies is the [Espionage Act](/wiki/espionage-act). The idea, as he puts it, is that if you publish something technically classified that would be espionage and you would be charged. His objection is constitutional: this is protected free press.<Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="11:26" />

## Who signed

Almost nobody. By Kiriakou's count the only organisations that said yes and actually signed were Newsmax, One America News, a Turkish newspaper he had never heard of, a Hungarian newspaper he had never heard of, and a couple of bloggers.<Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="11:26" />

The consequence he reports is a building without reporters: *"otherwise, there's no news coverage of the Pentagon right now, at least not at the Pentagon in the press office."*<Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="11:56" />
