**Clyde Hall** is the name John Kiriakou gave, with a viewer's help, for an imprisoned Ohio congressman whose remark about prison friendship he came to accept only after serving a sentence himself. He told the story on the DeProgram programme in July 2025.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="52:32" />

## Identifying him

Kiriakou introduced him without a name: *"there was a crooked congressman from Ohio who went to prison a couple of years before I did. He was in my prison."* When the host guessed Jim Traficant, Kiriakou corrected him, the man represented *"the district immediately south of Jim Traficant"*, and then admitted the gap: *"I forget his name now."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="52:32" />

The name came from the audience. A viewer supplied it in the chat, and Kiriakou confirmed it on air with evident surprise: *"Yes, it was Clyde Hall. How in the world did you pull that up? ... I have not thought of that name in a decade."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="52:32" /><Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:03" /> The identification is therefore a viewer's, adopted by Kiriakou rather than produced by him.

The sentence he gives is *"like two years or two and a half years,"* on *"a corruption charge."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:03" />

## The remark

What Kiriakou remembered was what the man said after his release. Against the standard formula, *"they say that you don't go to prison to make friends"*, the congressman said the opposite: *"I came out of prison having made some of the best friends of my life."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:03" /><Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:33" />

Kiriakou heard this *"before I was ever in trouble"*, and his judgement at the time was blunt: *"thinking that he was nuts."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:33" />

## The reassessment

His own imprisonment reversed it. *"But he's not nuts. And I understand it now."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:33" />

Offered trauma bonding as the explanation, Kiriakou half-accepted and then rejected it on a specific ground: *"in part it could be, but the people that you trauma bond with, you tend to walk away from."* The friendships had outlasted the prison, which he takes as evidence they were something else, *"these guys were genuinely good guys."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:33" />

He makes no attempt to reconcile that with what the men had done, immediately noting that *"some of them did some very bad things"* and going on to tell [the story of the body in the barrel](/wiki/the-body-in-the-barrel).<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="53:33" />
