**Closing the Gulf embassies** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s assessment of a decision that was announced while he was on the air on 3 March 2026, days into the American and Israeli war against Iran.

He broke off from a question to report it. *"Hey, before I answer, uh, the United States just announced that it is closing its embassies in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait. This is dramatic."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="21:22" />

## Why he read it as dramatic

The measure of the decision, for Kiriakou, is the war it is not being compared to. *"All through the the first Gulf War, we never closed any embassies."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="21:22" />

The single exception he allows proves the threshold rather than lowering it: *"obviously, we had to close Kuwait because it was it was attacked and invaded and our our diplomats were held as hostages, human shields in Iraq."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="21:22" /><Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="21:54" /> An embassy closed because the country it stood in had ceased to exist as an independent state is not a precedent for closing two that had not been invaded.

His conclusion is about what Washington expects rather than what has happened. *"To close two American embassies means that the White House and the State Department have decided that this is going to get a whole all hell of a lot worse than what it is right now."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="21:54" />

## The evacuation that could not happen

Asked what obligations American missions carry toward United States nationals in a war zone, Kiriakou said the usual answer was that Washington gets them out — *"Usually, the US does that. Usually"* — and then explained why the usual answer had failed. The difference was speed: *"we're we're so deep into a hot war so quickly, like there was months in the run-up to the Gulf War. There's no time to do it."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="22:26" />

What the State Department had issued instead was an instruction without a means of complying with it: an announcement *"urging all Americans in the Persian Gulf to get out. But, there's no flights. There are no flights."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="22:26" />

The gap was being filled, in one case, by a host government rather than by Washington. *"Today, the the UAE government said that any person, any Westerner, stuck in a hotel and unable to evacuate will have all of his hotel bills paid. So, just stay, relax, protect yourself, and we'll figure things out later."* Kiriakou attributed it to national character rather than policy: *"That's the Emiratis. That's how they are. They're very generous."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-regime-derange-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and-jo" t="22:58" />
