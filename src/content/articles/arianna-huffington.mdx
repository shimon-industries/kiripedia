**Arianna Huffington**, founder of the Huffington Post, is the person who — largely by accident — gave [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s prison writing its audience.

## The accidental pipeline

Kiriakou's first [Letter from Loretto](/wiki/letter-from-loretto) was never meant for the world: he sent it to his attorney, who passed it to the proprietor of the website Firedoglake, Jane Hamsher. What Kiriakou didn't know was that Hamsher was good friends with Arianna Huffington — who put it on the Huffington Post under a banner headline. It went, in his words, completely crazy: more than a million hits in the week it published, followed by CNN and 60 Minutes coming to the prison and a press wave that took on a life of its own.<Cite s="2017-05-17-strand-book-store" t="21:24" /><Cite s="2017-03-17-the-zero-hour-rj-eskow-the-zero-hour-rj-eskow" t="05:19" /><Cite s="2017-06-29-rob-kall-bottom-up-show" t="49:49" /><Cite s="2017-08-21-internet-party-internet-party" t="33:23" /> *"I thought, my God, I have a soapbox here. I can reach people."*<Cite s="2017-06-29-rob-kall-bottom-up-show" t="49:49" />

In his fullest account of the chain, the letter went from Kiriakou to his attorney, from the attorney to Jane Hamsher of firedoglake.com — an alternative news site slightly left of centre, whose writers he rates highly, Kevin Gosztola in particular — and from Hamsher, who published it there, on to Huffington, who gave it the banner headline.<Cite s="2026-06-22-dead-drop-s2e6-use-your-words" t="06:57" /> The newsletter itself was modelled, *"very arrogantly,"* on Martin Luther King's letter from Birmingham Jail, and in describing daily life it inadvertently exposed two crimes committed against him by guards, one of them a felony.<Cite s="2026-06-22-dead-drop-s2e6-use-your-words" t="06:26" />

Within 24 hours of the Huffington Post publication it had gone, in his word, crazy; within another 24 the prison — not Kiriakou — was fielding interview requests from CNN, Fox, ABC News, NPR, *Time*, *The Week*, *The Atlantic Monthly*, *The New Yorker*, *The Economist* and *Playboy*.<Cite s="2026-06-22-dead-drop-s2e6-use-your-words" t="07:31" /> His conclusion at the time was the same one he draws now: *"this is going to be my superpower."*<Cite s="2026-06-22-dead-drop-s2e6-use-your-words" t="08:02" /> The traffic figure varies between tellings, from more than a million hits to over two million.<Cite s="2017-08-21-internet-party-internet-party" t="33:53" />

The distribution was deliberately redundant and entirely unglamorous. Each letter went out in four copies — to Hamsher, to his lawyer, to his wife, and to his publisher, because he intended to turn the letters into a book. The prison could not work out how he was getting them out, and he let the staff believe he was running a sophisticated smuggling operation: *"in fact I was just put an envelope and put a stamp on it and sent it."*<Cite s="2021-10-17-la-progressive" t="30:42" />

The prison's response was an investigation — of Kiriakou, and how the blog got out.<Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="1:05:06" />

## The platform

Huffington's site became the launch pad for the letters that followed. His open letter to [Edward Snowden](/wiki/edward-snowden) — written expecting a few hundred readers — started at the Huffington Post and was republished everywhere.<Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="49:58" /> His exposé of guard corruption led to a Department of Justice inspector general investigation.<Cite s="2018-06-04-suzi-3d-unity4j-vigil" t="24:02" /> And when the prison moved to silence him — stripping him to pen and paper, floating diesel therapy — he wrote about that immediately, *"and she immediately published it... it went viral."* Oliver Stone and John Cusack tweeted *"hands off John Kiriakou,"* and [Code Pink](/wiki/code-pink) mobilized its members to call the [Bureau of Prisons](/wiki/bureau-of-prisons).<Cite s="2018-06-26-rdap-dan-rdap-dan" t="03:49" />

Huffington also came to Loretto in person, bringing two young journalists with her.<Cite s="2023-02-18-savage-joy-marie-mann-savage-joy-and-john-kiriakou" t="36:49" /> Kiriakou had blogged for the Huffington Post before prison,<Cite s="2015-05-09-awakeradio1-awakeradio1" t="01:07" /> and he credits her outlet — alongside The Intercept — with running stories the New York Times, Wall Street Journal, and Washington Post wouldn't touch.<Cite s="2015-11-17-telesur-english-interviews-from-washington-dc-whistleblower-d" t="22:06" />
