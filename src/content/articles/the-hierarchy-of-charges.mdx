**The hierarchy of charges** is John Kiriakou's account of how prisoners at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto) ranked each other by what they had been convicted of, given on the DeProgram programme in July 2025 in answer to a viewer asking whether some crimes carried more standing than others.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:26" />

## The top

Asked which charge was most respected, Kiriakou answered in one word, twice: *"RICO. RICO."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:26" />

His explanation is that the statute functions as a proxy for something else. *"RICO usually had murder attached to it. Usually it was conspiracy to commit murder, because a guy will murder somebody and then 20 people will get charged for it because they're all part of the conspiracy."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:56" />

The respect, in other words, attached less to the racketeering statute than to the killing it implied — and to the organisational membership that produced the conspiracy count in the first place, a status separately recorded in [the -47 suffix](/wiki/the-forty-seven-suffix) on a prisoner's number.

## The bottom of the free-standing scale

Below the ranked offences sat a set that nobody bothered to rank. *"The only guys where nobody gave a shit was like arson,"* Kiriakou says. *"I knew three arsonists and people were like, 'Eh, arson.' Did you kill anybody? No. Okay, nobody cares."* He puts insurance fraud and mortgage fraud in the same indifferent category.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:03:27" />

One case in that bracket he had never encountered before: a man convicted of *"property fraud"*, who *"just forged papers transferring ownership of a golf course to himself and then he tried to charge people rent."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:03:27" /><Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:03:58" />

## Contempt for the rich

The active dislike was reserved for a different group. *"I'll tell you who they didn't like. They didn't like these big rich white-collar criminals,"* Kiriakou says.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:56" />

Asked whether the objection was that such men were soft, he agreed — *"yeah, soft"* — and gave the sentiment in the voice of the men who held it: *"Oh, you thought you were going to get away with it. Poor you. You got caught and now it's like, 'Oh, I'm in prison. Oh my god.'"*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:56" /><Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:03:27" />

Offered the summary that this was a class judgement, Kiriakou accepted it without qualification: *"It's a class thing."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:03:27" />

The ranking sits alongside the one he gives elsewhere in the same conversation for the [sex offenders and informants](/wiki/chomo) at the bottom of the prison's social order, and the [Italians](/wiki/italians-at-loretto) at the top of it.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:00:22" />
