**Typhoid fever in Bangkok** is an episode [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) adds to a discussion of [CIA](/wiki/cia) inoculation practice, as the case that showed him what the injections did and did not buy.

## The diagnosis

Kiriakou introduces it as an addendum to the shots story and rates it against everything else. *"I caught typhoid fever in Bangkok in 2002. And it's the sickest I've ever been in my life."*<Cite s="2026-02-11-deprogram-ted-rall-bad-bunny-epstein-files-and-more" t="08:48" />

His first reaction to the diagnosis was to reject it on the strength of his own paperwork: *"I said, 'But I said it can't be typhoid fever because I got the I got the shots. I got the typhoid shots.'"*<Cite s="2026-02-11-deprogram-ted-rall-bad-bunny-epstein-files-and-more" t="08:48" />

## The correction

The doctor's answer is the part Kiriakou repeats. *"The typhoid shots don't prevent you from getting typhoid fever. The typhoid shots make the typhoid fever slightly less virulent when you catch it."*<Cite s="2026-02-11-deprogram-ted-rall-bad-bunny-epstein-files-and-more" t="09:18" />

The severity even so was considerable. *"It was like the worst case of the flu I've ever had in my my I had to go to the hospital twice."*<Cite s="2026-02-11-deprogram-ted-rall-bad-bunny-epstein-files-and-more" t="09:18" />

The episode sits directly against [the gamma globulin shot](/wiki/the-gamma-globulin-shot), which Kiriakou describes as the routine that produced a lifetime of injections before overseas travel — and which, on this evidence, produced attenuation rather than immunity.
