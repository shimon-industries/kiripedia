The **Office of the Director of National Intelligence** is the coordinating layer placed above the American intelligence agencies in 2004. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s position is that it should not exist.

## Why it was created

He gives the origin as a reaction to a single man's failures: it was created *"because [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet), who is the DCI, director of central intelligence, essentially the director of the entire intelligence community, fell on his face with 9/11 and the Iraq war and they needed to have an adult in the room. And so they just created this ODNI thinking if we put one more layer of of oversight, it's going to make everything better. And all it did was make everything more confusing and fatter."*<Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="32:13" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="32:46" />

## The growth

Kiriakou says he agrees with one thing [Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) has said about it — that it is bloated, inefficient and full of fat — and gives the numbers: *"When when I was still in the [CIA](/wiki/cia) and they created ODNI, they had 16 employees. 16 employees. Now they have about 24,000 employees. Why? What are they doing?"* He allows for the transfer of the President's Daily Brief from the CIA — *"okay, there's there's two dozen people right there"* — but not for the rest, and asks in particular why CIA operations against Iran, Iraq, Russia or China are run from the ODNI when *"it's CIA case officers collecting the information, CIA analysts analyzing the information, CIA budgets that are financing the information."* His answer: *"It it was just a power grab."*<Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="30:09" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="30:41" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="31:13" />

## Authority it does not have

The structural objection is that the director of national intelligence does not direct national intelligence. Almost all of the agencies technically under the ODNI *"are actually DoD agencies"* — the NSA and the DIA belong to the Defense Department, as do army, navy, air force, marine and coast guard intelligence, all of them nominally ODNI components while falling under the Pentagon. *"So what does the DNI do with all of these other organizations? It controls only the budget. Nothing but the budget. So why bother why bother having it in the first place?"*<Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="31:13" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="31:43" /><Cite s="2026-06-08-ddgeopolitics-prison-for-truth" t="32:13" />

## Two directors, one job

Kiriakou describes the relationship between the two offices as formally settled and practically not. *"The CIA director technically falls underneath the DNI, but in reality, on a day-to-day basis, that''s just not true."* Cabinet rank is discretionary and rotates by administration: *"presidents can choose, for example, among CIA director, DNI, and ambassador to the United Nations, who gets a cabinet position."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="30:25" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="30:56" />

The office''s founding purpose was coordination of all eighteen intelligence agencies, which *"for all practical purposes"* meant control of the budget. Kiriakou identifies the structural limit on that: *"don''t forget that fully half of those intelligence agencies are actually parts of the Pentagon. And so in the end the DNI doesn''t have authority over those — the Secretary of Defense has authority over those."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="30:56" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="31:26" />

Within the space that remains, he says, the office has grown steadily. *"Over the last 20 years more and more authority has been absorbed by the DNI. The [President''s Daily Brief](/wiki/presidents-daily-brief) used to be a CIA-only publication; now it''s a DNI publication. Major operations — Iran, Iraq, Afghanistan, Russia, China — used to be solely the domain of the CIA; now they fall under the DNI. So it''s almost as though we have two CIA directors who are competing with each other for primacy."* His summary of the boundary: *"there''s no clear division between DNI and CIA. It''s just something that is fought out on an ad hoc basis."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="31:58" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="32:31" />

## What the DNI actually controls

Describing the office in December 2024, Kiriakou set the [DNI](/wiki/office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence) against the [CIA](/wiki/cia) director and judged the comparison awkward: the CIA job is *"a more important position, but it's almost like comparing apples and oranges."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="47:44" /> On this telling the office began with *"a staff of 15"* and had grown to *"more like 8,000 — it's grown rather quickly and unnecessarily."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="47:44" /> The figures differ from the sixteen-to-twenty-four-thousand range he gives elsewhere; the direction of travel, and the verdict on it, do not.

He identified two concrete transfers out of the CIA. The first is the [President's Daily Brief](/wiki/presidents-daily-brief), which became a DNI publication — a change he approves of, on the grounds that the CIA, [DIA](/wiki/defense-intelligence-agency), [NSA](/wiki/nsa) and the State Department's Bureau of Intelligence and Research *"ought to have the ability to write for it. It shouldn't be a CIA exclusive publication."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="47:44" /><Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="48:16" /> The second is the [National Intelligence Council](/wiki/national-intelligence-council): the National Intelligence Officers — for the Middle East, Russia, China, Latin America, Africa, warning, proliferation, terrorism, Europe and Southeast Asia — *"have all been transferred to the ODNI."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="50:22" /> The sense-of-the-community memoranda and National Intelligence Estimates they produce were *"written exclusively by CIA or INR,"* and *"now anybody in the community can write them, because they're all going to be coordinated by the DNI anyway."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="50:53" /> Kiriakou reads that as a corrective to a standing CIA weakness — *"very smart people who went to the best schools in the country and then they engage in this group think."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="51:23" />

On budget his account is blunt about where the leverage is not: *"the CIA's budget is 98%, everybody else is 2%,"* so the office's budget authority over the rest of the community is thin.<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="48:16" /> What he treats as the office's real instrument is the [operating directive](/wiki/operating-directive), which he calls *"really really really important."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="48:16" /> The work is a year-round sifting — *"going through hundreds of thousands, millions of cables, liaising with cabinet members and sub-cabinet members to try to get to what every stakeholder has on his or her plate that the intelligence community can collect on."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="49:20" /><Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="49:51" /> His summary of the role: *"it's more of a coordination position than anything else."*<Cite s="2024-12-12-jackman-radio-episode-188-former-cia-officer-john-kiriakou" t="49:51" />
