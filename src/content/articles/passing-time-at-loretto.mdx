**Passing time at Loretto** is the practical problem [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) identifies as the real hardship of a sentence. In his account the worst part of prison is that it is Groundhog Day: every single day is just like every other day.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="13:56" />

For most of the men at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto), he says, the answer was working out and watching television.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="13:56" />

## The televisions

The television arrangement was factional. Kiriakou says the prison had eight sets, and that the Hispanic prisoners controlled four and the Black prisoners the other four.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="13:56" />

He describes the programming with an explicit caveat that he hates to stereotype but that this is simply how it was. The four Black-controlled sets showed MTV, VH1, BET and a sports channel. The Hispanic-controlled sets showed Telemundo, another Spanish-language channel he could not recall, ESPN Deportes, and for reasons he attributes to some long-departed prisoner's taste in soap operas, CBS.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="14:26" />

The white prisoners did not get to choose.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="14:26" />

Kiriakou says this did not much affect him, because he was not a big television watcher at the time: he did not watch *Love and Hip Hop Atlanta*, and he did not watch a Peru–Venezuela soccer match. So he read.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="14:26" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:04" />

The arrangement had consequences beyond boredom. Kiriakou notes that violence at Loretto was relatively rare, and that most of what there was broke out over what to watch on television.<Cite s="2026-05-18-dead-drop-s2e1-welcome-to-loretto" t="08:16" />

## Working out

For many prisoners the answer was exercise every day. Kiriakou describes men who were nothing but rock-hard, knotted muscle, because there was nothing else to do but work out.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:04" /> Among the Hispanic prisoners it was not even optional: an unwritten rule of [the Pisces prison gang](/wiki/pisces-prison-gang) required every member to work out daily.<Cite s="2026-05-18-dead-drop-s2e1-welcome-to-loretto" t="14:17" />

## Kiriakou's own solution

Kiriakou says he was fortunate. Four hours into his first day a stranger's postcard arrived, and he decided on the spot to answer every piece of mail he received, a decision that produced [7,000 letters to 675 people](/wiki/seven-thousand-letters) over 23 months and kept him very, very busy.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="15:38" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="16:13" />

He was also writing. *[Doing Time Like a Spy](/wiki/doing-time-like-a-spy)* started as a joke, he did not intend it as a book, but thought he would write down some of the more ridiculous and absurd things that happened.<Cite s="2026-06-01-dead-drop-s2e3-circle-of-friends" t="16:13" />

He places reading in the same category, as a defence against [depression in prison](/wiki/prison-depression): a voracious reader anyway, he says he read well over a hundred books in 23 months.<Cite s="2026-06-15-dead-drop-s2e5-the-abyss" t="20:08" />
