**Felix Rodriguez** is a Cuban-American former [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) officer involved in the Bay of Pigs invasion and the Vietnam War, reported to have been present at the 1967 killing of Che Guevara in Bolivia. He is a distinct figure from [Jose Rodriguez](/wiki/jose-rodriguez), the post-9/11 CIA official who ordered the destruction of the agency's waterboarding tapes.

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) recounts that podcast host Danny Jones and his co-host Steven interviewed Rodriguez in Miami; Kiriakou notes that the trip followed an appearance by Danny Sheehan, who told Jones he had deposed Rodriguez and alleged Rodriguez had been "on the team that killed the president" — an allegation Kiriakou attributes to Sheehan rather than endorsing himself.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:05:27" /><Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:06:29" />

In a separate account of the same trip, Kiriakou recalls that the Bay of Pigs chief he initially had in mind when discussing his post-9/11 CIA branch — staffed largely by retired senior officers who volunteered to return as contractors — was not Rodriguez, but a different, older officer; Rodriguez, he clarifies, was a distinct figure who had "worked for him."<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="23:02" /> He and his co-host Steven traveled to a museum in South Florida in Miami — one of the only interviews they ever conducted requiring travel — to interview Rodriguez in person.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="42:45" />

## Che Guevara and denial of souvenir-taking

Asked directly whether he had kept Che Guevara's Rolex as a souvenir, Rodriguez denied it: *"No, no, no, no. I never kept his ro[lex]."*<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:07:00" /> Kiriakou notes that, separately from the Rolex denial, rumors persist that Rodriguez kept Che's fingers as a souvenir.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="44:49" />

## The Havana CIA museum

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he visited the CIA museum in Havana, which displays items confiscated from Rodriguez and other Bay of Pigs fighters; the museum is housed in a mansion across the street from the Tunisian embassy and holds CIA identification, weapons, and related material from the era.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:09:04" />

## Billy Waugh

Kiriakou compares Rodriguez to [[billy-waugh]], the legendary Special Forces and CIA paramilitary officer, saying the two men were "very similar" and that the wars they fought shaped them in the same way.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="37:04" /> Kiriakou says he met Waugh on a joint operation in the Middle East lasting several months, returning to the U.S. on September 9, 2001 — two days before the 9/11 attacks.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="37:35" /><Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="38:38" /> Waugh, he recalls, drove with a vanity license plate reading "17 hits" beside a Purple Heart symbol, insisting to Kiriakou it was genuine and joking that "some sorry ass son of a bitch in North Carolina has 18."<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="38:07" /> Weeks after 9/11, Waugh told Kiriakou he had been in Afghanistan "killing people," while Kiriakou — one of only two fluent Arabic speakers then in the CIA's Counterterrorism Center — was kept behind because the mission, focused on killing rather than interrogating, did not need a translator.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="39:09" />

Kiriakou recounts several of Waugh's war stories, some corroborated in Annie Jacobsen's book *Surprise Kill Vanish*: that his first kill, in Vietnam, was cutting a woman's throat with a serrated dagger; that he was shot down behind enemy lines in Vietnam, his pilot killed, and walked roughly 25 miles south on a bullet-shredded leg to reach friendly lines, earning a Silver Star and his first Purple Heart; and an embellished account of cutting a cow's artery in a rice paddy to drink its blood for nourishment during the trek, which Kiriakou likened to a scene from *Apocalypse Now*.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="40:12" /><Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="41:14" /><Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="41:44" /> Kiriakou says Waugh, who never had children, treated the military as his entire life — after being declared disabled following his first combat injury, he hitchhiked across the country trying to convince the CIA to send him back into the field.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="42:45" />
