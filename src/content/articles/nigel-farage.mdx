**Nigel Farage** is a British politician on whom [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) gave a considered assessment in September 2025, when a viewer asked what he thought of the prospect of Farage in Congress.

## The rehabilitation

Kiriakou dates the change precisely. *"As recently as five years ago, Nigel Farage was a joke. He was like a kind of a neo-Nazi fascist weirdo fringe weirdo from the British right wing."*<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="13:35" />

What produced the shift, he says, was the collapse of the alternative: *"just over the last 5 years, with the electoral humiliation that the Conservative Party underwent, Farage has come out as like a real voice on the right."* The man himself, in Kiriakou's view, did not move, *"he's no less fascistic than he was five years ago. He just has been accepted by the mainstream as a legitimate"* voice. He finds this *"nuts."*<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="13:35" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:05" />

## The prediction

On a visit to London in March, Kiriakou read a piece in the Times, *"a conservative paper, and it's owned by Rupert Murdoch"*, saying Farage would likely be the next leader of the Conservative Party.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:05" />

His forecast if that happens is unequivocal: *"I could see the end of the Conservative Party as a viable political force."* The mechanism is defection. Conservatives *"who aren't that conservative, they're called wets"*, would move to the Liberal Party, *"which is not liberal, it's actually quite conservative, especially on foreign policy"*, and the Farage conservatives who remained *"would just be sort of a rump party."*<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:05" /><Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="14:35" />

## The historical parallel

What Farage reminds Kiriakou of is not a person but a period: *"what I've read happened in the UK in conservative politics in the run-up to the Second World War. There were a lot of Brits who thought Hitler was a-okay. And a lot of those Brits, you know, it took years for them to change"*, the Mosley types.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="15:05" />

The detail he and his host settle on is the respectability of it at the time: *"people forget that the British Fascist Party was a real thing"*, and *"there were members of the royal family that were members of it."*<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="15:36" />

## Boris Johnson

Asked in the same run of questions about Boris Johnson, *"two peas in a pod"*, as the questioner put it, Kiriakou gave a shorter answer. *"There was nobody in the UK more shocked when Boris Johnson became prime minister than Boris Johnson. He was a joke."* Not merely a joke, he added, but a type: *"a provocateur journalist who made a career by pissing people off on purpose"*, one of the original I-have-an-opinion right-wing journalists.<Cite s="2025-09-06-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="18:49" />
