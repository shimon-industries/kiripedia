**The MI5 model** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s name for a proposal he says he first heard discussed inside the agency in the early 1990s, and which he expected the domestic-terrorism legislation drafted after 6 January 2021 to deliver at last. Pressed in February 2021 on a bill that created new domestic terrorism units inside the Department of Homeland Security and the FBI rather than any new offence, his answer was that the idea was not new: *"that's been discussed since the early 1990s."*<Cite s="2021-02-12-scott-horton-john-kiriakou-on-the-governments-dangerous-re" t="24:19" />

His own introduction to it came abroad.<Cite s="2021-02-12-scott-horton-john-kiriakou-on-the-governments-dangerous-re" t="24:19" />

> *"On my very first trip to London as a CIA officer, I'd been to London many times, of course, but on my first trip as a CIA officer, we talked with MI5 about their duties as a domestic intelligence service. [MI6](/wiki/mi6) is famously the foreign intelligence service. MI5 does only domestic intelligence. They don't have the power to arrest, but they don't need to arrest anybody. That's what Scotland Yard is for."*

The American application was on the table in the same conversations. *"The discussions way back then, I mean, this must have been 1992 or 1993, we were talking about setting a similar thing up in the United States, either parallel to the FBI or as a part of the FBI, that would just spy on American citizens and then provide that intelligence to the FBI to effect arrests."*<Cite s="2021-02-12-scott-horton-john-kiriakou-on-the-governments-dangerous-re" t="24:50" />

What he draws from the gap is that the design outlasted every justification offered for it: *"here we are all these years later, 30 years later, and it looks like it's gonna come to pass."*<Cite s="2021-02-12-scott-horton-john-kiriakou-on-the-governments-dangerous-re" t="24:50" /><Cite s="2021-02-12-scott-horton-john-kiriakou-on-the-governments-dangerous-re" t="25:23" />
