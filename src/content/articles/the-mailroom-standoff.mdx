Asked about Marty Gottesfeld being cut off from other journalists inside a communications management unit, Kiriakou said the same thing had been tried on him and described how he beat it.<Cite s="2019-07-22-free-martyg-john-kiriakou-dana-gottesfeld-conversation" t="39:30" />

Staff at FCI Loretto tried to stop him communicating with other journalists. He told them they could not do that, because he had a First Amendment right. They told him he did not. He answered that he did, and that it was in the Bureau of Prisons regulations — then went to the law library, printed the regulations out, and laid them on the counter in the mailroom.<Cite s="2019-07-22-free-martyg-john-kiriakou-dana-gottesfeld-conversation" t="39:30" />

## "It's brand spanking new"

Two of the staff then argued that the printout was old. Kiriakou told them it had been published a month earlier. At that they looked at each other, told him to go, and sent the letter.<Cite s="2019-07-22-free-martyg-john-kiriakou-dana-gottesfeld-conversation" t="40:01" />

Kiriakou draws two lessons from the exchange, and states them together. The first is that the staff do not know their own regulations. The second is that they know the prisoner is at such an incredible disadvantage that they can do almost anything they want to him and he has no recourse — which is why, he says, the pressure has to be kept up from the outside, by people whose hands are not tied.<Cite s="2019-07-22-free-martyg-john-kiriakou-dana-gottesfeld-conversation" t="40:01" />
