**The sixty-five-cent arrangement** is the informal deal [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) made with the chaplain at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto) that freed him to write his prison memoir.

## The job

Kiriakou had offered on his first full day to teach — he had a bachelor's degree in Middle Eastern studies, a master's in policy analysis and completed doctoral coursework at Virginia — and was screamed at for the suggestion. He was made a janitor in the chapel instead, at about sixty-five cents an hour.<Cite s="2023-02-06-in-limine-podcast-in-limine-john-kiriakou" t="16:42" /><Cite s="2023-02-06-in-limine-podcast-in-limine-john-kiriakou" t="17:50" />

The [chapel job](/wiki/prison-chapel-job) had one incidental advantage: the chaplain liked him, because he was the only person working there who was not a sex offender. But the chaplain's budget was tight and the prison was badly overcrowded — built for around 680 people and holding 1,425, with men in bunks in the hallways.<Cite s="2023-02-06-in-limine-podcast-in-limine-john-kiriakou" t="17:50" />

## The deal

Kiriakou proposed a trade that cost the chaplain nothing and helped somebody else. He did not want the work and did not need the sixty-five cents an hour; if the chaplain would keep him on the books and pass the money to the next man in line, he would simply stop coming in.<Cite s="2023-02-06-in-limine-podcast-in-limine-john-kiriakou" t="17:50" />

The chaplain agreed, and Kiriakou used the recovered hours to write. He is clear that he had not gone in intending to write anything: *"they made it so easy, like they gave me so much material that I couldn't not write the book."*<Cite s="2023-02-06-in-limine-podcast-in-limine-john-kiriakou" t="18:20" /> The result was [Doing Time Like a Spy](/wiki/doing-time-like-a-spy), written longhand because he had no access to a computer.
