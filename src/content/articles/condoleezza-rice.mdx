**Condoleezza Rice** was the U.S. National Security Advisor at the time of the September 11 attacks, and she appears in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s accounts three times: as the meeting he never made that morning, as the official who told the CIA director to absorb a lie, and as his own preferred explanation for why the warnings went nowhere.

## The meeting that never happened

Kiriakou recounts that he and [Cofer Black](/wiki/cofer-black) had a meeting scheduled with Rice on the morning of 9/11 over a mundane matter: an obscure Government Printing Office report, *Foreign Policy of the United States, Greece, Turkey, Cyprus, 1949 to 1967*<Cite s="2026-07-03-comedy-overload-comedy-overload" t="2:33" />, was about to be published, and its declassified cables would out three recruited assets — still alive in their 90s — whom U.S. law would then require the government to protect with citizenship, a house, and money. They wanted Rice simply to pull those cables.<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="38:40" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="39:10" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="39:44" /> The meeting never happened; as the driver arrived, Kiriakou saw the first tower burning on a secretary's small TV.<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="40:15" /> In a separate retelling he again describes the scheduled meeting over the same obscure book — nobody, he says, was ever going to read its 1,200 declassified pages — noting it also happened to contain the names of three still-living CIA sources, whom an obscure U.S. law would require the government to offer citizenship if the agency ever outed them.<Cite s="2026-07-06-the-jason-jones-show" t="54:09" /> He remembers that on the morning itself people around him were calling her Condi rather than Condoleezza.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mad-that-podcast-cia-spy-9-11" t="1:13:30" />

Kiriakou recalls that just as he remarked, elsewhere that morning, on how clear the day was compared to a 1930s accidental bombing of the Empire State Building, the second plane struck the second tower.<Cite s="2025-04-12-the-clear-signal-with-stev" t="1:22:18" />

## 'Take one for the team'

Kiriakou confirms the account of a briefing in which [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) told George W. Bush that [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) was not who the Agency had said he was, and Bush replied that Tenet was not going to make him lose face over it, having called Zubaydah important repeatedly.<Cite s="2021-10-11-scott-horton-ep-5610-the-torture-of-abu-zubaydah" t="12:03" /><Cite s="2021-10-11-scott-horton-ep-5610-the-torture-of-abu-zubaydah" t="13:06" />

Rice was in that meeting. When Tenet objected that they owed it to the public to tell the truth — that Zubaydah was not the man they had thought — Rice, in Kiriakou's account, chimed in: *"George, you're just going to have to take one for the team."*<Cite s="2021-10-11-scott-horton-ep-5610-the-torture-of-abu-zubaydah" t="13:06" />

Kiriakou's own memory is of the effect on Tenet rather than the substance: *"I don't think I have ever seen George Tenet as angry as he was that day"* at having been spoken down to that way. *"He was livid."*<Cite s="2021-10-11-scott-horton-ep-5610-the-torture-of-abu-zubaydah" t="13:06" /><Cite s="2021-10-11-scott-horton-ep-5610-the-torture-of-abu-zubaydah" t="13:36" /> The stake was not private: Abu Zubaydah's supposed importance was the main public justification for the torture programme.<Cite s="2021-10-11-scott-horton-ep-5610-the-torture-of-abu-zubaydah" t="12:33" />

## In the room for Iran

Rice also appears in Kiriakou's account of a principals committee meeting he attended as Tenet's note-taker, sitting directly behind him. It was chaired not by the president but by [Dick Cheney](/wiki/dick-cheney), with Rice and her note-taker on the next screen, [Colin Powell](/wiki/colin-powell) and his, the chairman and vice chairman of the Joint Chiefs and theirs, and General Tommy Franks of CENTCOM with his.<Cite s="2026-03-15-consortium-news-world-this-week-war-on-iran" t="19:01" /><Cite s="2025-09-25-joe-mkhitaryan" t="54:53" /><Cite s="2025-12-04-dave-smith-part-of-the-problem-1335" t="39:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight" t="02:39" />

The meeting is the one at which Franks closed an order-of-battle briefing by saying that if all went as planned they could be in Tehran by August — prompting Tenet to cut his microphone and ask Kiriakou whether Franks had said Baghdad or Tehran. Kiriakou told him: Tehran. Back at the office the deputy director's response to the news was *"are they still talking about that? We're not going to attack Iran."*<Cite s="2026-03-15-consortium-news-world-this-week-war-on-iran" t="19:32" /><Cite s="2026-03-15-consortium-news-world-this-week-war-on-iran" t="20:02" />

## Her failure to listen, and what he makes of it

Pressed on how the warnings could have gone unheeded — the CIA saying something massive was coming, and Rice not acting — Kiriakou declines the conclusion the question invites. He does not believe an American president would allow an attack like that, and does not believe any one official had the power: the bureaucracy is too vast, and Cheney, though powerful, was not powerful enough to single-handedly stop the wheels of the intelligence community.<Cite s="2023-04-25-real-news-reality-asserts-itself-real-news-reality-asserts-itself" t="03:05" />

His alternative is unflattering rather than conspiratorial: *"Maybe Condoleezza Rice was an incompetent boob. I mean, there's that school of thought too."*<Cite s="2023-04-25-real-news-reality-asserts-itself-real-news-reality-asserts-itself" t="03:35" /> He grants the underlying complaint — that the warnings were enough to justify telling immigration to be stricter, or taking a closer look at Saudi immigration — while pointing out that policy changes in a big lumbering bureaucracy can take months or years.<Cite s="2023-04-25-real-news-reality-asserts-itself-real-news-reality-asserts-itself" t="02:34" />
