**Ibrahim Traoré** is the military ruler of Burkina Faso, assessed by John Kiriakou on the DeProgram programme in September 2025 after a viewer raised his name. Kiriakou recognised it at once — *"Oh, I know him"* — where his co-host did not.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:01:03" />

## Taking power

Kiriakou's summary is that Traoré is *"a young military officer in Burkina Faso that took power I don't know, two, three, four years ago"*, by overthrowing the government.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:01:03" />

His judgement of the trajectory since is negative and recent: *"He's become, in my view, increasingly irrational over the last couple of months."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:01:03" />

## The undelivered transition

The first charge is the broken promise. *"It's been at least three years now and there has been no transition back to democracy, which he had promised at the outset. Didn't happen. He's the dictator. The military dictator."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:01:03" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:01:34" />

Kiriakou treats the promise itself as a recognised genre rather than a personal failing, and supplies the standard wording: *"Well, we will have an election as soon as things settle down."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:01:34" />

## The recent measures

He cites three, in the order he recalled them.

The first is criminal. *"He outlawed homosexuality. So if you're gay, you risk 10 years in prison for being gay."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:01:34" />

The second is institutional, and Kiriakou explains the body before describing its abolition — Burkina Faso maintains a permanent electoral commission whose job is to plan each election. *"He scrapped it. Fired everybody. Got rid of everybody. He took personal control."* The consequence he draws is direct: *"and there aren't going to be any elections."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:04" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:36" />

The third he sources vaguely, saying only that he does not remember where he read it: a programme *"to expel all of the foreigners from Burkina Faso."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:36" />

## His verdict

Kiriakou notes his own surprise at knowing as much as he does about the country — *"I don't even know why I know this much about Burkina Faso"* — and does not romanticise what preceded the coup: *"Burkina Faso was never a garden spot."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:04" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:36" />

The conclusion is one sentence: *"but man, it's getting worse and worse and worse."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:02:36" />
