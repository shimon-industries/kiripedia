**Gail Slater** was the official put in charge of the antitrust unit within the Department of Justice, coming from JD Vance's office and seen, per Sean Davis — a co-panelist alongside [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) on the same Megyn Kelly episode — as a "mega populist" who would translate that agenda into scrutiny of big tech and other large companies.<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="07:44" />

Davis says the Bondi Department of Justice was "pretty actually bad" on antitrust, and that Slater "had all her power taken away" — firms that had reached settlements with the DOJ were able to sidestep her office and avoid the normal scrutiny that should accompany a settlement.<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="08:15" /> As an example, Davis cites the merger between Hewlett Packard Enterprise and Juniper Networks, providers of Wi-Fi and networking services to stadiums and hospitals, which he says was settled with the DOJ "behind closed doors, literally over cocktails" — raising, in his account, serious legal questions.<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="08:46" />

When Slater's team objected to the settlement, one of her deputies was fired, and Slater herself was fired soon after.<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="09:18" /> Davis describes the episode as an example of campaign-trail populism that "cashed out as kind of crony capitalism" under [Pam Bondi](/wiki/pam-bondi), and says he does not believe Bondi "did right by her."<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="09:18" />
