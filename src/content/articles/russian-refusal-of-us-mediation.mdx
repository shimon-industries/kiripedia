**Russian refusal of US mediation** refers to Moscow's announcement, during the [2026 Iran war](/wiki/2026-iran-war), that it would no longer engage in [Ukraine](/wiki/ukraine-proxy-war) settlement talks headed by the United States.

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) summarised the Russian position as a straightforward inference from American conduct: Washington had been *"engaged supposedly in talks with Iran, pretending to go through talks with Iran, and then they attacked Iran,"* so *"why should we trust the Americans to broker talks with Ukraine when they could just as soon turn around and attack us in the middle of the talks."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-kiriakou-washington-has-no-say-in-this-war" t="05:39" /> His own verdict was one sentence: *"I think that's exactly right."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-kiriakou-washington-has-no-say-in-this-war" t="05:39" />

He strengthened the point by noting that it had happened twice — *"the two wars with Iran happened in the middle of the so-called discussions that America was having with Iran on its nuclear program"* — and concluded that the United States *"can't be trusted anymore."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-kiriakou-washington-has-no-say-in-this-war" t="06:11" />

The same passage records that [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) had reached out twice within ten days for a ceasefire with Iran, and that the Iranians had played games with the approaches.<Cite s="2026-04-03-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-kiriakou-washington-has-no-say-in-this-war" t="06:11" />
