**The 189 undeclared officers** is a figure [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) traces to the briefing his intake class received from the CIA's director of security on his first day at the agency — a talk he says has stuck in his mind for thirty-one years.<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="20:38" /><Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="21:09" />

The briefing was about counterintelligence, and its point was that the threat was not confined to *"the usual cast of characters at the time — the Soviets, the Chinese, the Cubans."* The example chosen was Israel. The Israeli embassy in Washington had two **declared** intelligence officers, one from [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) and one from [Shin Bet](/wiki/shin-bet) — declared meaning *"they came here and they said, hi, I'm a Mossad officer, I'm a Shin Bet officer, and I'm here to cooperate with you."*<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="21:41" />

Against that, according to the FBI, the Israelis had *"at least 189 undeclared intelligence officers at loose across the country, at universities and defense contractors and governmental organizations, stealing our secrets to take back to Israel."*<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="22:14" />

## The headquarters ban

Kiriakou gives the operational consequence as the measure of how bad the relationship was: *"the Israelis were not allowed in CIA headquarters for liaison meetings. We had to meet them offsite somewhere, because they simply could not be trusted to not plant a bug or to try to recruit a CIA officer."*<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="22:47" />

He draws a broader institutional attitude from it — *"there was this real loathing of the Israelis at the CIA, but respect for the Palestinians"* — and reports the same sympathy at the State Department, adding that it stops mattering when a decision moves to the White House, *"because the White House is weighing political issues rather than just issues of what's right and what's wrong."*<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="22:47" /><Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="23:17" />

## A second telling

Kiriakou gave the same briefing again during the [2026 Iran war](/wiki/2026-iran-war), anchoring it to his first day at the agency — the day *"you put your hand up in the air and you swear to uphold and protect the Constitution against all enemies foreign and domestic"* — and to a talk by the CIA's director of security.<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="02:05" />

The structure is unchanged: two intelligence representatives at the Israeli embassy, one from [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) and one from [Shin Bet](/wiki/shin-bet), against a far larger undeclared presence identified by the [FBI](/wiki/fbi) — here given as *"187 other undeclared Israeli intelligence officers spread out all across America working to steal secrets from American defense contractors."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="02:05" /> The two-count difference from his usual figure of 189 is a variation in the retelling; the attribution and the substance are identical.

What he adds in this version is the reason nothing follows from the count: *"politically the relationship is so close that you don't want to rock the boat and start arresting people spying for Israel."* His illustration is [Jonathan Pollard](/wiki/jonathan-pollard).<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="02:36" />
