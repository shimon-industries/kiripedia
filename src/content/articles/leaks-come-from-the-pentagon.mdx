A viewer asked three questions at once about a New York Times report on a SEAL Team 6 mission in North Korea: how such a story leaks, whether the exposure is a national security risk, and whether Kiriakou would have approved the operation. He called them three great questions and took them in order.<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:52" /><Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:24" />

## Who leaks, and why

On the first, Kiriakou did not hesitate: "Somebody who opposed it and was overruled leaked that story."<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:24" /> [Ted Rall](/wiki/ted-rall) agreed at once — "100%" — and Kiriakou moved to the institutional question of where such a person sits.<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:24" />

## The rule

"I can tell you from my own experience at the [CIA](/wiki/cia)," he said, "99 times out of 100, the leak came from the Pentagon."<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:24" />

He gave two structural reasons. The first is volume — in his phrase, "they leak like sieves over there." The second is scale and process: the department is "so gigantic" and "they don't polygraph anybody, [so] it's just impossible to know where the leaks came from."<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:54" />

The absence of a polygraph program is the operative detail. Kiriakou's argument is not that Pentagon officials are less loyal than agency officers but that the two organisations have different exposure to detection — and that a leaker who cannot be identified is a leaker who can afford to act on a disagreement.

## The damage

On the second question Kiriakou did not defend the disclosure. Asked whether exposing the mission was a national security risk, he answered: "You bet it is. Of course. It exposes methodology."<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:54" /> When Rall observed that Kim Jong-un now knows something he did not know before, Kiriakou confirmed it: "That's right."<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:54" />

The position is worth noting because it is not the one his critics attribute to him. Kiriakou, who went to prison in a leak case and argues the [Espionage Act](/wiki/espionage-act) is unconstitutional, nonetheless says without qualification that this particular disclosure damaged security and revealed methods.

## The operators

The conversation carried on to the unit itself, which Rall asked about in blunt terms. Kiriakou's answer was that they do make mistakes, and often — "a lot" — and that "a lot of them get killed in the course of the operation."<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="23:26" /> Rall's summary, that you want people who are brave and willing but you also want them to come home and not be exposed, drew Kiriakou's agreement: "Yep. That is right."<Cite s="2025-09-11-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="23:26" />
