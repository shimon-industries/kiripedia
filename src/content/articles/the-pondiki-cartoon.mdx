**The Pondiki cartoon** is an episode from John Kiriakou's Athens posting, told on the DeProgram programme in September 2025 as an aside during a discussion of American ambassadors to Greece.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:22" />

## The paper

Kiriakou introduces it by name and translation: *"there used to be, there may still be, a newspaper called Pondiki, which means little mouse, and it was a left-wing satirical newspaper."* Offered the French comparison, he agreed the two might be *"sister papers."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:22" />

## The image

The subject was the sitting American ambassador. *"They had a photoshopped picture once of a nude Ambassador Burns on his hands and knees with a dog collar on."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:56" />

The second figure was a Greek minister Kiriakou makes clear he disliked, *"the minister of education, Vaso Papandreou, who I never liked. I never could stand her"*, depicted with the leash in one hand: *"She has a whip and she's beating him on his naked butt with a whip while she's got him by the dog collar."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:56" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:28" />

## The protest, and the desks

The ambassador took it up formally. *"He was so angry he lodged an official protest."* Kiriakou's view of that is unsympathetic: *"It's like, dude, it's a satirical newspaper. What, you never heard of freedom of speech and freedom of the press?"*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:28" />

The reaction inside the mission ran the other way. *"So we all went out and bought these newspapers. It was the back cover of the paper that day, and all of us taped them up in front of our desks."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:28" />

The display was safe because of an access restriction Kiriakou states and then immediately qualifies. *"He didn't have access to the station because he was the ambassador. He's not a station employee. So he never saw it."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:28" />

The qualification is the standard one he applies to any such reference: *"and I'm not confirming or denying the existence of a station in Athens. I'm just saying, of course..."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="21:58" />

## Burns

Kiriakou's dim view of the ambassador is not confined to the cartoon. Listing the succession of American envoys to Athens, he says Greece has had *"a long string of popular ambassadors going back to, and not including, Nicholas Burns,"* and adds that the dislike was not only local: *"even those of us who worked for Nick Burns hated Nick Burns."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="18:21" />

He returned to him a second time when assessing a later appointment, saying of the incoming ambassador that *"we could have done worse. We could have had Nick Burns come back, God forbid."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:22" />
