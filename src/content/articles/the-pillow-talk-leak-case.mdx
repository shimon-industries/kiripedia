[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) uses this case as his benchmark for proportionate treatment of a classified disclosure, and he watched it happen from the adjacent desk.

*"At the CIA in the 1990s I sat next to a woman who was having an affair with a man who used to be a senior CIA officer. He had retired and he had gone to CNN as a terrorism consultant — and in the course of pillow talk she relayed classified information to him. He went on CNN and repeated the classified information."*<Cite s="2024-02-19-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure" t="16:00" /><Cite s="2024-02-19-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure" t="16:34" />

The investigation was quick and the outcome was administrative. *"The CIA began an investigation. They immediately tied it right back to her. She was not charged with espionage. What they did was they wrote a strongly worded letter and put it in her personnel file. They suspended her without pay for two weeks, and she was ineligible for a promotion for a period of one year."*<Cite s="2024-02-19-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure" t="17:05" />

His conclusion is stated as a rule: *"that''s how you handle the misuse of classified information."* He applies it in both directions — to the boxes in [Joe Biden](/wiki/joe-biden)''s garage and to the documents at Mar-a-Lago alike: *"shame on you, you should have a strongly worded letter put in your personnel file. But espionage? Sorry."*<Cite s="2024-02-19-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure" t="17:05" /><Cite s="2024-02-19-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure" t="17:36" /><Cite s="2024-02-19-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure-kevin-gosztola-unauthorized-disclosure" t="18:09" />
