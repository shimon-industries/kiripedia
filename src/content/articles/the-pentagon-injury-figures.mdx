**The Pentagon injury figures** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s explanation of a July 2026 *New York Times* report that the United States had withheld information on dozens of military injuries sustained in the war with Iran.

## What was withheld

In the week before the strike that killed two American soldiers and left a third missing in Jordan, Iran had carried out three other attacks on American forces in the country. Those attacks injured dozens of service members and damaged several helicopters. The Pentagon disclosed neither the earlier strikes nor the casualties and damage they caused.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="05:11" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="05:43" />

## Why they could

Kiriakou's answer, offered with an apology for sounding like a broken record, is that no rule was broken. *"They are not compelled. The Pentagon is not compelled to report these figures. There's no law that says they have to report these figures."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="05:43" />

His explanation for the absence is institutional rather than conspiratorial. There is no such law because *"Congress is asleep at the switch,"* and never passed one.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="06:13" />

The rest follows on its own. The Pentagon's reasoning, as he renders it, is simply: why would we inform Capitol Hill, why would we inform the media that our helicopters are being destroyed or that dozens of people are injured, when we do not have to?<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="06:13" />
