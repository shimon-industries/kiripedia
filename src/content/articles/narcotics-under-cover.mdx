**Narcotics under cover** is the subject of a question put to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) by a viewer of his Q&A programme: while operating under cover, if a valuable asset invites the officer to take drugs, is he permitted to do so in order to establish that he is not the police?

Kiriakou's answer was immediate and unqualified. *"The answer is yes. You may partake of the drugs."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="22:51" />

## The rule's origin

What follows is a history rather than a policy citation. *"This all started way back in the day in Yemen,"* Kiriakou says, and the substance concerned was [qat](/wiki/qat-in-yemen) — a shrub whose leaves are chewed green every afternoon by, in his account, effectively every man in the country. Because a source could not be developed by an officer sitting out the national ritual, the CIA issued *"special dispensation to every officer working in Yemen or in other qat-chewing countries like Somalia, Djibouti, Ethiopia, Eritrea."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="22:51" /><Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="23:55" />

The rationale Kiriakou gives for the original dispensation is social rather than pharmacological: *"you've got to do it to fit in."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="23:55" />

## The harder case

Kiriakou then extends the rule to substances that are unambiguously narcotics and to postings where the stakes are higher. *"Now, if you're deep undercover in Colombia or Mexico or Peru or Ecuador and you need to snort a line, they're going to let you snort a line."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="24:25" />

The justification changes with the setting. Where qat was a matter of fitting in, cocaine in a deep-cover setting is a matter of survival: *"It could be a matter of life and death. So, you're going to snort the line."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="24:25" />

## Kiriakou's own reaction to the question

Kiriakou marks the exchange as unusual. *"Nobody's ever asked me that question before. Good question,"* he says — a note that places the subject among the parts of operational life that are routine inside the organisation and almost never discussed outside it.<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="24:25" />

The answer sits alongside the general position he takes in the same session on the relative morality of intelligence services: that the CIA's conduct is not constrained in the ways outsiders assume, and that [most other countries' services behave better](/wiki/more-moral-than-the-cia).<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="24:57" />
