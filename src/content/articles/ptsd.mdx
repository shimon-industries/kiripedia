**Post-traumatic stress disorder** (**PTSD**) is a recurring thread in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of his own life — from Athens to Pakistan to prison — and in his indictment of the institutions he writes about.

## Greece and Pakistan

Kiriakou says he has had PTSD *"a couple of times."* The first came out of Greece, where [Revolutionary Organization 17 November](/wiki/revolutionary-organization-17-november) nearly got him — the attack fell instead on his next-door neighbor, British defense attaché [Stephen Saunders](/wiki/stephen-saunders).<Cite s="2019-03-14-salem-access-tv-public" t="41:39" /> He also came back from Pakistan with PTSD — a fact he kept to himself at the time — shaped partly by his surprise at how willing his colleagues were to kill people.<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="42:12" /> Yet he is struck by what didn't cause it: not the two assassination attempts, not breaking down doors in Pakistan, not even a human head on the ground at a bombing rescue.<Cite s="2019-04-20-david-gornoski-on-torture-prison-ptsd" t="40:45" /><Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="16:46" />

Put to him in 2026 that commenters had concluded from the way he tells his stories that what he has is not PTSD but nostalgia, he agreed — *"Yeah, I do"* — and explained it as having thoroughly enjoyed his career, which he separates sharply from what the career became: *"9/11 turned everybody into stone-cold murderers."*<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="32:59" /> He measures the change against the ordinary world by inverting a friend's remark about having met a murderer, a brother-in-law just out of prison after twenty years: *"Try working at the CIA for 15 years when you're the only person who's not a murderer."*<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="33:30" />

## The prison dream

Nobody prepared him for what came after release: depression, anger, PTSD. For six months he had the same dream every night — he was out of prison, but it was a mistake; it was almost midnight, count time was coming, and he had to figure out how to get back inside before they sent him to solitary.<Cite s="2017-05-17-strand-book-store" t="28:08" /> The dreams only stopped six months into writing his prison book — getting it out of his mind, he says, is what ended them.<Cite s="2017-06-06-disruption-network-lab" t="1:23:21" /> Years later he still described variations of the dream,<Cite s="2019-03-14-salem-access-tv-public" t="42:09" /> and still took the medication that controls it: a failed blood-pressure drug with the odd side effect of making patients forget what they dreamt — now used, quite effectively, for PTSD.<Cite s="2019-08-08-david-gornoski-archives-david-gornoski-archives" t="14:48" /><Cite s="2022-04-07-david-gornoski-explores-life-as-a-spy" t="15:10" />

The delayed reaction had a cost. Through the arrest, prosecution, and imprisonment his wife was fully supportive — but when he came home and the PTSD set in, the pressure became too much, and the marriage ended.<Cite s="2018-06-20-rdap-dan-facing-40-years-espionage" t="09:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-20-rdap-dan-facing-40-years-espionage" t="11:30" />

## PTSD and the torture program

Kiriakou notes that the CIA's legal architecture for [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) was explicitly built around PTSD: psychological torture was permitted so long as it stopped short of permanent damage, which is why one CIA document showed a psychologist present at every instance of [waterboarding](/wiki/waterboarding) — essentially to say *stop* before the harm became permanent.<Cite s="2015-11-14-robin-hensel" t="19:12" /> The harm ran both directions: he remembers colleagues coming back from the secret sites crying, suffering from PTSD, taking time off, going on anti-anxiety medication.<Cite s="2023-07-22-david-pakman-show-rudy-trump-offered-me-a-pardon-for-2m" t="11:28" /> Washington, he adds, even has psychiatrists who specialize in *"screwed up former CIA officer PTSD"* — an actual cottage industry.<Cite s="2019-10-02-nicole-sandler" t="36:05" />

He initially scoffed at drone operators claiming PTSD *"like they're the infantry on the ground"* — then corrected himself: people act like tough guys and they're really just human beings who spend all day killing people like a video game. The [Daniel Hale](/wiki/daniel-hale) case, he says, is shot through with PTSD and what psychiatrists now call [moral injury](/wiki/moral-injury).<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="15:44" /><Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="15:12" />

## Prisoners

Kiriakou extends the same lens to ordinary prisoners: released straight back to the neighborhood they came from, no better off, *"but now you have PTSD and you're pissed off"* — and the only thing you know how to do is sell drugs again.<Cite s="2023-04-26-katie-halper-kiriakou-gerald-horne-on-the-indictment-of-ru" t="54:50" /> More broadly, he argues nobody responsible for many deaths escapes without PTSD or some psychological malady — *"unless you're a true sociopath."*<Cite s="2018-06-04-suzi-3d-unity4j-vigil" t="37:59" />
