**WLDragnet** is a free search tool that documents monitoring of social-media users by U.S. defense contractors on the basis of political affiliation. A user enters a Twitter handle and is returned a list of the places where the account was picked up.<Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="31:14" /><Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="33:20" />

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he checked it while it was still in beta, two or three years before this March 2025 interview, and was *"shocked"* by what came back: **Northrop Grumman Corporation** had searched his name hundreds of times, and *"it was clear they were building something."* He notes that he would never otherwise have known he was of interest there, and declines to protect the company by leaving it unnamed, *"it's not my job to protect them, so I'll just say it."*<Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="31:45" />

The activity was not confined to a single firm. Kiriakou says it extended to companies doing business with Northrop Grumman and to some of its competitors: after he wrote an article four or five years earlier criticising McDonnell Douglas, the record showed *"this flurry of activity at McDonnell Douglas, who is this guy, what's he doing."*<Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="32:17" />

## Use in litigation

Kiriakou was in litigation against Northrop Grumman on a separate matter at the time, and says his attorneys were able to use the WLDragnet material as part of the lawsuit.<Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="32:47" /> He describes the existence of such a tool as something that would never have occurred to him, *"but there it is, and it's free."*<Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="32:47" /><Cite s="2025-03-01-truthwire-where-the-hell-is-panquake-surveillance-the-t" t="33:20" />
