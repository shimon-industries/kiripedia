**Larry Devlin** was a [CIA](/wiki/cia) officer and chief of station in the Congo. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), who describes him as *"our former colleague"* and says he died several years before this 2026 recording, raises him as a standard he has not met: *"I wish I had the guts that our former colleague Larry Devlin had."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-dead-drop-s1e26-did-john-le-carre-invent-the-modern-spy" t="1:11:21" />

Devlin wrote a memoir of his entire career in agency operations, titled *Chief of Station, Congo* — a title Kiriakou notes is *"even the title is a security violation."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-dead-drop-s1e26-did-john-le-carre-invent-the-modern-spy" t="1:11:21" /><Cite s="2026-05-04-dead-drop-s1e26-did-john-le-carre-invent-the-modern-spy" t="1:11:55" />

The manoeuvre was in the timing. *"What he did is he told his daughter: publish this the day after I die. And that's what she did. And they never went to the [Publications Review Board](/wiki/publications-review-board)."* Kiriakou's verdict — *"well played"* — comes with the observation that the secrecy agreements have presumably been tightened since, *"at least if the lawyers are worth anything."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-dead-drop-s1e26-did-john-le-carre-invent-the-modern-spy" t="1:11:55" />
