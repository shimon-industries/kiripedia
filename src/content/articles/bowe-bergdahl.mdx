**Bowe Bergdahl** is the American soldier [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) offers as his second illustration of what a country does to the people it gets back in a trade. Kiriakou raises him unprompted, alongside [Francis Gary Powers](/wiki/francis-gary-powers), in answer to a viewer's question about the fate of [spies traded home](/wiki/spies-traded-home).

## The capture

In Kiriakou's account, Bergdahl *"was uh a military guy in Afghanistan who just walked off base one day, and was immediately captured by the Haqqani network, who turned him over to the Taliban."* The captivity that followed lasted years, and ended in an exchange: *"He was held for years, finally repatriated to the United States in a prisoner trade."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="08:56" /><Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="09:27" />

## The reception

Kiriakou is precise about the distinction he wants to draw, and states the correction inside the sentence: Bergdahl *"was not just treated as a traitor, he wasn't a traitor, but he was treated as such."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="09:27" />

He cites the political treatment as evidence of how far that went. *"Donald Trump during the 2016 election campaign said that he should be executed."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="09:27" />

## Afterwards

The outcome Kiriakou describes is neither the execution demanded nor an exoneration. *"He was in the military for another year. He spent a little bit of time in the brig and um was finally released. He lives with his mom and dad in Idaho."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="09:27" />

That ending is what Kiriakou uses the case to prove. It closes his answer on the fate of the exchanged with the flat generalisation that follows from both his examples: *"but um yeah, good things don't happen to people who get caught."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="09:27" />

The pairing is deliberate. Powers was a professional who did the survivable thing and was resented for it; Bergdahl was not an intelligence officer at all and was punished as though he had been one. In Kiriakou's framing, neither man's actual conduct determined how he was received — what determined it was that he had been in enemy hands, and had come home.<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="07:53" /><Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="09:27" />
