The **RDAP program** — the [Bureau of Prisons](/wiki/bureau-of-prisons)' Residential Drug and Alcohol Program — is a sentence-reduction scheme [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) repeatedly recommends to fellow whistleblowers facing federal time.<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="05:18" /> An inmate with any history of drug or alcohol problems, he says, can *"sit there every week for a year"* watching *"a DVD of the A&E network show Intervention,"* and *"they take 12 to 18 months off your sentence."*<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="05:18" /> Combined with good behavior and a halfway house, Kiriakou told [Daniel Hale](/wiki/daniel-hale), a five-year sentence could shrink to *"two and a half years"* — *"next thing you know you're out."*<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="05:50" /> He drew on the same math to reassure Hale that the fear of a decade behind bars was worse than the likely reality.<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="29:47" /><Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="31:53" />

In a separate interview, Kiriakou describes RDAP in the same terms — sitting through a course that is *"basically just sitting and watching episodes of Intervention"* — noting the judge in Hale's case had strongly recommended he serve at the low-security federal prison at Butner, North Carolina, in part because of a substance-abuse problem.<Cite s="2024-02-04-fortress-on-a-hill-henri" t="27:50" /> He also recounts advising Hale directly, when the U.S. Marshals Service conducted its pre-sentencing investigation, to disclose a drug or alcohol problem: *"Lead with it — that'll make you eligible to be enrolled in the Bureau of Prisons RDAP program."*<Cite s="2021-08-01-scott-horton" t="12:05" />
