**WikiLeaks**, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account, is best understood not as the work of one man but as a *system* — a durable piece of infrastructure for whistleblower protection that outlasted any single leak, and one substantial enough to merit its own history separate from [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange) personally. Kiriakou made the point explicitly: whatever comes next from WikiLeaks, *"maybe... the next iteration of WikiLeaks that comes across, there's not a person. It's, you know, it's a system."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="48:15" />

## Origins and the 2010 turning point

WikiLeaks began publishing material in 2007, starting with U.S. military documents from Afghanistan and Iraq, but its defining moment came in 2010 with the release of military and diplomatic cables sourced from [Chelsea Manning](/wiki/chelsea-manning).<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="01:07" />

## A whistleblower-protection system

Kiriakou describes WikiLeaks, in practice, as a protective mechanism for sources: *"We used to tell people all the time, if you really want to leak national security information for the purpose of blowing the whistle on waste, fraud, abuse, illegality, or threats to the public health or public safety, you got to do it through WikiLeaks because they're the only ones who are going to protect your identity."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="36:13" /> He ties the need for that protection directly to the [Whistleblower Protection Act](/wiki/whistleblower-protection-act)'s exemption for national-security employees, which leaves the press — and outlets like WikiLeaks — as the only remaining option.

The recommendation has been constant across the years. Would-be whistleblowers contact him constantly asking who in the media to approach, and his answer never changes. Outlets such as the New York Times, the Washington Post and [the Intercept](/wiki/the-intercept) publish encryption keys on their websites — *"I don't trust any of them, frankly"* — and *"the only place to go is WikiLeaks, because WikiLeaks is the only organisation that has never blown a source. It's the only organisation you can trust to handle this kind of information, and that was true years ago with [Chelsea Manning](/wiki/chelsea-manning), and it's true today."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="02:05" /><Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="02:37" /><Cite s="2020-05-15-choo-radio-network" t="01:35" />

He distinguishes two separate protections. Beyond never having exposed a national-security source, WikiLeaks *"protected the sources in the documents that they've released — and that's why this process takes time, because they're going through these documents to make sure that nobody gets in trouble, that nobody's identity is compromised. No journalism outlet does anything like that."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="06:50" />

The stake, on his account, is not the organisation but the channel: without it *"there's literally nowhere for a whistleblower to go, and so the information just doesn't get out,"* and *"then we don't know what our government is doing in our name."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="03:08" /><Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="04:13" />

## Cultural and political effect

Kiriakou credits WikiLeaks with a broader cultural effect: before 2010, he says, the word "whistleblower" was barely part of his vocabulary — *"I may have heard it once in my lifetime before WikiLeaks"* — but afterward it entered everyday conversation, including inside the secret spaces where he worked.<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="22:09" /> In the same discussion, WikiLeaks is attributed as a contributing source for the Arab Spring, its revelations feeding into the wave of protests and uprisings that followed.<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="22:09" />

## Why the agency cannot tolerate it

Kiriakou sets the two institutions against each other by their power source. *"The CIA derives its power from lies, from their false flags and their fake colour revolutions … Julian and WikiLeaks have derived power from truth-telling — and that's why they're such a threat, because if there's one thing that the [CIA](/wiki/cia) cannot abide, it is transparency."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="44:04" /><Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="44:35" /> Presented with a counterinsurgency doctrine in which information is the foundation beneath the financial, security and governmental pillars of a society, he endorsed it and inverted the application: *"the CIA would use that doctrine to destabilise a country … WikiLeaks is using information to destabilise those who would destabilise the society."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="46:39" /> Hence, he argues, the absence of a substitute: *"the Washington Post isn't going to take down the CIA, nor the New York Times or the Guardian or the Intercept. Only WikiLeaks has that kind of authority and leverage."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="47:10" />

## Other voices at the 2026 Berlin panel

The following three points were made by other participants at the March 2026 Disruption Network Lab panel in Berlin at which Kiriakou spoke, and are recorded here as panel material rather than as his own account.

### The NGO option Assange refused

A fellow panelist argued that after 2010, WikiLeaks faced a fork in its institutional path: it could have become an NGO accepting American government financing, which would have made it *"a lame operation."* That panelist credited [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange) personally with steering WikiLeaks away from that outcome and keeping it an organization willing to openly violate the conventions safer, government-funded operations would not.<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="01:40:06" /><Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="01:40:39" />

### Public opinion and the 2016 election

An audience member — neither Kiriakou nor any panelist — raised the concern that WikiLeaks' revelations may not move public opinion even when they succeed as journalism, noting of the 2010 cable releases: *"the effect... is that then Trump got elected. I think the fact that it didn't really change the mind of people that when we are whistleblowing, coming out with... scoops, we see that that doesn't affect the public opinion."*<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="01:32:44" />

### Years of confinement before Belmarsh

Another speaker noted that the persecution of Assange was not limited to his time in [Belmarsh Prison](/wiki/belmarsh-prison) — much of his life had already been spent in some form of confinement before Belmarsh and before his years in the Ecuadorian embassy.<Cite s="2026-03-20-disruption-network-lab-war-military-exposing-crimes-is-not-a-crime-jo" t="01:23:49" />
