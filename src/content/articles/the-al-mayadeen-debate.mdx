**The Al Mayadeen debate** took place two days before the American and Israeli attack on Iran, and [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described it on air on 28 February 2026 while the first strikes were still being counted.

He is a regular on the Lebanese network and volunteers his regard for it: *"I was on a Lebanese network called Al Mayadeen. I love this station. It is such an in-depth, I mean, it's in Arabic, of course, but it's such an in-depth news analysis station. I always say yes when they ask me to go on."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="17:44" /><Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="18:16" />

## The opponent he was not expecting

The format is normally an argument with an academic. *"Usually they have me sort of uh you know, as a point-counterpoint with a professor. And they have fantastic uh professors, especially on American politics."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="18:16" />

This time it was not. *"Day before yesterday, they had me debating the lead nego- the lead nuclear negotiator from Iran. I was shocked when he came on the screen and that's who I was supposed to debate."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="18:16" /><Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="18:46" />

Kiriakou noted that the negotiator made the same case he himself makes about Iranian compliance: that Iran is a signatory to the non-proliferation treaty and permits inspections, while *"it's the Israelis who have nukes, who won't sign the NPT, and who have forbidden any and all inspections."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="18:46" />

## The point that was rejected and then conceded

Where they disagreed was over what was actually on the table. *"I made a point that at first he he rejected outright and then finally came around and was like, well, okay. I said, look, what what the two sides are negotiating right now is [the JCPOA](/wiki/jcpoa)."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="19:16" />

Kiriakou's evidence was the content of the terms: *"It's all the same tenets that were in the JCPOA"*, the agreement Donald Trump had scrapped unilaterally. He listed them: that Iran could not enrich uranium to weapons grade; that the enriched uranium and Iran's nuclear sites had to be open to IAEA inspection; and that any enriched uranium had to be stored in a third country, the three under consideration being Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Turkey.<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="19:16" /><Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="19:47" />

His account is that Iran had accepted all of it, *"because they had agreed to it 15 years ago with Barack Obama."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="19:47" />

## The single refusal

One demand Iran would not meet, and Kiriakou does not treat the refusal as unreasonable. *"The only thing the Iranians did not agree to was scrapping their uh medium-range standard conventional missiles. And no country in its right mind would ever agree to something"* like that.<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="19:47" /><Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:19" />

He added that even a successful deal would only have postponed the attack, *"all that would be doing is putting what happened uh last night and this morning uh off uh for another couple of years. That's all it would do, maybe."*<Cite s="2026-02-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="20:19" />
