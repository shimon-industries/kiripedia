The **Vietnam War** predates [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s career — he was born in 1964, and his parents marched against the war<Cite s="2015-04-16-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="07:45" /><Cite s="2017-06-06-disruption-network-lab" t="1:14:28" /> — but it supplies the precedents for nearly every argument he makes.

## The war of lies

Kiriakou files Vietnam first in his catalogue of wars launched on falsehoods: the push for U.S. involvement came from the Gulf of Tonkin incident — *"a lie"* — just as Iraq came from lies later.<Cite s="2020-12-24-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-kiriakou-rebecca-vincent-assange-countdown-to" t="00:01" /> The [Pentagon Papers](/wiki/daniel-ellsberg) showed not only that the war was being lost but that the generals had known it for almost a decade while lying to the president — which is why Kiriakou calls [Daniel Ellsberg](/wiki/daniel-ellsberg) *"the godfather of all modern national security whistleblowers."*<Cite s="2019-03-14-salem-access-tv-public" t="27:05" /> He repeats the consensus tribute without irony: Ellsberg is *"the guy that ended the Vietnam War."*<Cite s="2021-06-01-scott-horton-daniel-hale-persecution" t="38:04" /> He even quotes the biographer's account of Lyndon Johnson explaining what the war was about by exposing himself.<Cite s="2015-04-23-the-real-news-network-the-iraqi-pissing-match-john-kiriakou-on-rai" t="02:20" />

## The torture precedent

Vietnam anchors Kiriakou's core case against torture: American POWs have described being tortured for names and answering with the starting lineup of the Green Bay Packers, or inventing ships and positions in the South China Sea — *"literally anything that they think you want to hear just to get you to stop."*<Cite s="2020-11-23-revolutionary-change" t="12:58" /><Cite s="2020-11-24-revolutionary-change-why-torture-is-both-immoral-and-ineffective-j" t="04:09" /> Waterboarding was used as recently as Vietnam, part of a lineage running back through the Belgians in the Congo to medieval China.<Cite s="2019-04-20-david-gornoski-on-torture-prison-ptsd" t="17:45" /> And the era's own villain knew better: even Robert McNamara — *"the one responsible for the Vietnam War"* — understood the technique was too clearly illegal.<Cite s="2019-03-14-salem-access-tv-public" t="04:15" />

## The institutional reckoning

In Kiriakou's history of the CIA, Vietnam marks the turn: the agency *"ran wild"* unchecked from 1947 through the war's end, until 1975 — the war lost, the assassination programs exposed — when things finally tipped toward the [Church Committee](/wiki/church-committee) era of oversight.<Cite s="2019-05-01-potkaars-podcast-potkaars-podcast" t="28:01" /> The [Cold War](/wiki/cold-war)'s worst nightmare, he says flatly, was the U.S. invasion of Vietnam.<Cite s="2026-07-01-the-jk-report-the-jk-report" t="32:49" />
