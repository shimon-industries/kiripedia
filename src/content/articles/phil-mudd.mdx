**Phil Mudd** is a former [CIA](/wiki/cia) and [FBI](/wiki/fbi) official turned television commentator. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) brings him up not for anything he did in government but as the second data point in a pattern, introduced with the words *"there are others like John."*<Cite s="2018-08-24-the-real-news-network-cia-whistleblower-john-brennan-is-out-for-him" t="07:49" />

## The pattern

The observation is that the escalating on-air denunciations of [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) by senior intelligence alumni are, in Kiriakou's reading, career moves rather than assessments. He quotes Mudd's contribution — Trump is *"a petulant child"*; Trump *"needs a pacifier and a new rattle"* — and then supplies what he takes to be the motive: *"Phil's trying to get his own show on CNN, and Phil wants to be the deputy director of the FBI or the deputy director of the CIA."*<Cite s="2018-08-24-the-real-news-network-cia-whistleblower-john-brennan-is-out-for-him" t="07:49" />

He extends the same reading to [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan), whose ambition Kiriakou places on the Pentagon, and treats the two together as evidence of a market rather than of a conspiracy. His summary is a shrug rather than an accusation: *"I mean, that's Washington. That's just the way it is."*<Cite s="2018-08-24-the-real-news-network-cia-whistleblower-john-brennan-is-out-for-him" t="07:49" />

The conclusion Kiriakou draws from the pattern is not about either man but about the audience for them — that a viewer who dislikes Trump is not thereby obliged to believe the people attacking him. That argument is set out at [no good guy](/wiki/no-good-guy).<Cite s="2018-08-24-the-real-news-network-cia-whistleblower-john-brennan-is-out-for-him" t="08:22" />
