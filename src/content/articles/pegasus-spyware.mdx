**Pegasus** is phone-interception software developed in Israel. In [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)''s description its defining property is that the target does nothing: *"You don''t have to take any action to activate it on your phone. You don''t have to click a link. You don''t have to respond to a text message or anything like that. It just works, just like that. And you have no idea."*<Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="29:15" />

## Where it has surfaced

Kiriakou points to *"the scandals that have already come to light in places like Greece or the United Arab Emirates, where Israeli software — Pegasus software specifically — has been used, first through an American contractor and then through the Israelis directly, where it''s being used against Americans."* The Greek case he recalls as an interception of a national sports team, and he says its consequence was constitutional rather than merely criminal: *"it literally brought down the Greek government."*<Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="27:13" />

His assessment of the present spread is deliberately unbounded: *"I think it''s more sophisticated and more broadly spread than what we know right now."* The comparison he draws is to signals collection inside the United States — *"we wouldn''t have known that similar software programs were being used by NSA against American citizens"* but for [Edward Snowden](/wiki/edward-snowden) — with the difference that Pegasus and its analogues are available on the commercial market. *"I mean, how do you protect yourself?"*<Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="29:15" /><Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="29:47" />

## Whether anything is secure

Asked whether any messaging application was genuinely private, Kiriakou says he wanted to give an informed answer and so telephoned [Tom Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake), the [National Security Agency](/wiki/national-security-agency) whistleblower. *"He said no. The easy answer is no. Nothing''s secure."* The reasoning Drake gave him concerns the moment of encryption rather than its strength: *"even if it''s not true that the US intelligence community has a backdoor into Signal, Signal is only encrypted when you hit the send button. That''s when it encrypts. They can intercept your message as you''re typing it, before you hit send."* Kiriakou draws the conclusion out: *"it doesn''t matter if they can''t break the encryption."*<Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="29:47" /><Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="30:20" />

Asked flatly whether privacy still exists, his answer was *"there''s no such thing."*<Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="32:24" />
