Breaking news reached the broadcast in its closing minutes: a car bombing in New Delhi. John Kiriakou's response is a compact example of how he handles attribution before any evidence is available.

## The report

The explosion occurred outside the Red Fort, a tourist attraction in New Delhi.<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="55:35" /><Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:06" /> Kiriakou relayed the early figures — "more than two dozen injured so far. I haven't seen any number on deaths" — and gave his read of the event itself: "it's clearly a terrorist attack."<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:06" /> The pictures and video from the scene that morning, he said, were grisly.<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:06" />

## The attribution

Invited by [Ted Rall](/wiki/ted-rall) to speculate, Kiriakou did — and prefaced it with the cost. "I wouldn't be at all surprised if it was Kashmiri separatist groups," he said, and immediately added: "Again, I'm going to get death threats from Pakistanis now, like I did two or three weeks ago, when I say that these groups carry out terrorist attacks."<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:06" />

He did not soften it. "Yeah, they carry out terrorist attacks because they're terrorist groups."<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:06" />

## The definition

What followed is the reasoning behind the label, stated as a rule rather than a judgement about Kashmir. "Whatever your political point is, is irrelevant," Kiriakou said. "If you're out there murdering civilians, you're a terrorist."<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:06" />

Rall completed the definition — that terrorism is violence against civilians for political purposes — and Kiriakou confirmed it: "Political. That's the definition of terrorism."<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:38" /> He then restated his guess in the form of a wager: "So yeah, I would bet this is probably Kashmiris."<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:38" />

## Note on epistemic status

Kiriakou marks this as speculation throughout — "I wouldn't be at all surprised," "I would bet," "probably" — offered minutes after the event and with no claim to information. What he states without hedging is the definitional point: that the merits of a cause do not bear on whether an organisation attacking civilians is a terrorist organisation.

The segment closed the broadcast; the hosts said they would follow the story the next day.<Cite s="2025-11-10-deprogram-ted-rall-deprogram-ted-rall" t="56:38" />
