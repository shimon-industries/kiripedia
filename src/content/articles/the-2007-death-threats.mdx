**The 2007 death threats** were the first personal consequence of [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s December 2007 decision to [speak publicly about the CIA's torture programme](/wiki/cia-torture-program-whistleblowing), five years before his prosecution.

## The mail

*"When I went public on torture in December of 2007 I got hundreds — literally hundreds — of pieces of hate mail and hate email."*<Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="17:49" />

## The three the FBI took seriously

The FBI telephoned him one day to say they had what they described as three credible threats on his life. *"One of them actually mentioned my children and my address,"* and the Bureau urged him to leave town.<Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="17:49" /> He took his family to Cancún for a week. The FBI then called to say they had taken care of everything: the senders had been interviewed, they were *"just local lunatics,"* and there was no real threat. The family came home.<Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="18:21" />

## As a measure of the climate

Kiriakou raised the episode in 2019 as a baseline, not as a grievance. Asked what would happen if [the Ukraine call whistleblower](/wiki/ukraine-call-whistleblower) were named, he answered by comparison: *"we're in a far, far more politically charged environment now than we were in 2007,"* with rally-goers assaulting journalists and protesters and the president encouraging it.<Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="18:21" /><Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="18:53" /> The implication he leaves is that a 2019 outing would draw worse than three letters the Bureau could dismiss in a week.
