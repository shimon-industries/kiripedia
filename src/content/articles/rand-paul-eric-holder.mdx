**Rand Paul**, a Republican senator from Kentucky, and **Eric Holder**, Barack Obama's attorney general, are bound together in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account by a single exchange before a Senate committee, in which Paul asked whether the president may order the killing of an American citizen and Holder conceded that he may.<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-trump-fires-bondi-cia-s-mk-ultra-history-and-two-weeks-sp" t="01:04:17" /> Kiriakou returns to it as his standard illustration of how far American civil liberties have eroded: *"When Eric Holder tells Rand Paul with a totally straight face that the president has the authority to kill an American citizen on American soil without benefit of trial, you know, we have a problem. That is a direct and dangerous threat to our civil liberties."*<Cite s="2018-07-28-discussions-of-truth-former-cia-agent-john-kiriakou" t="32:24" />

## The exchange

Kiriakou dates the hearing to the very end of the Obama administration and places it before the Senate Judiciary Committee.<Cite s="2020-12-23-codepink" t="45:49" /><Cite s="2015-08-17-a-public-affair-wort-89-9-fm-a-public-affair-monday-august-17th" t="14:08" /> The questioning came in two stages. Paul asked first about killing American citizens abroad — in one telling specifically *"with drones"* — and Holder said yes.<Cite s="2015-08-17-a-public-affair-wort-89-9-fm-a-public-affair-monday-august-17th" t="14:40" /> Elsewhere Kiriakou renders the first answer as conditional: *"Are you saying that the president has the right to kill anybody he wants?"* — and Holder replied, *"If that person presents a clear and present danger."*<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:24:41" />

The second stage — the same authority exercised on American soil — is where his tellings diverge. In the fullest version, Paul asked whether the president may kill an American citizen on U.S. soil *"if he believes that that citizen is a threat to public safety or to national security,"* and *"Eric Holder said yes, that the president has the right to kill American citizens not just overseas but on American soil."*<Cite s="2020-12-23-codepink" t="46:20" /> In other tellings Holder declined to answer that half of the question on the record, offering only to address it in closed session — *"and the Attorney General wouldn't respond to that question."*<Cite s="2015-08-17-a-public-affair-wort-89-9-fm-a-public-affair-monday-august-17th" t="14:40" /><Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:25:43" /> Years later Kiriakou still frames it as an open question with a silence where the answer should be: whether the government may kill an American citizen *"on his ranch in Idaho, we don't know,"* because Holder *"wouldn't even answer that question."*<Cite s="2024-09-18-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-reluctant-spy" t="45:27" /> In another retelling he attaches the confirmation to the National Defense Authorization Act rather than the hearing's drone framing.<Cite s="2025-05-05-austin-and-matt" t="34:38" />

What does not vary is the consequence he draws. The claimed authority rests, he says, on the Patriot Act — *"it's not specifically laid out like that in the Patriot Act, but that's the excuse they're using"* — and it *"has never been challenged in the courts."*<Cite s="2020-12-23-codepink" t="46:20" /> He treats the doctrine as of a piece with the effect of torture on a domestic population: a citizenry frightened into following its leadership *"like lemmings right off the edge of the cliff."*<Cite s="2015-08-17-a-public-affair-wort-89-9-fm-a-public-affair-monday-august-17th" t="14:40" /> *"What kind of country do we have?"*<Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="42:29" /><Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="43:02" />

## Paul and the Haspel nomination

The longest-running Rand Paul story in the corpus is the fight over [Gina Haspel](/wiki/gina-haspel)'s nomination as CIA director. The *Washington Post* asked Kiriakou for an op-ed opposing her, held it for the Sunday edition — five times the weekday circulation — and gave him an unusually large and in one account unlimited word count, running it across the paper's Outlook section.<Cite s="2024-10-30-bulwarg" t="17:19" /><Cite s="2022-05-19-scheerpost" t="14:03" /> The morning it appeared, at about ten o'clock, Paul telephoned and asked him to come to Capitol Hill and help strategize on how to block the nomination. *"I said, I'll be there in twenty minutes."*<Cite s="2025-03-24-sarah-westall-business-game-changers-cia-coups-coverups-and-torture-cia-whistleblo" t="25:21" /><Cite s="2021-09-08-primary-sources-jailing-the-messenger-the-cia-s-torture-whist" t="1:03:03" />

The scene he describes is the senator, four or five senior staffers and a whiteboard, counting votes — *"who's yes, who's no, who's maybe, who can we lobby"* — with lines and arrows and the names of senators who might be persuaded to vote against.<Cite s="2021-09-08-primary-sources-jailing-the-messenger-the-cia-s-torture-whist" t="1:03:39" /><Cite s="2025-03-24-sarah-westall-business-game-changers-cia-coups-coverups-and-torture-cia-whistleblo" t="25:52" /><Cite s="2024-10-30-bulwarg" t="17:49" /> Paul had already announced he would vote no and would filibuster, and Kiriakou at the time treated him as *"the only hope"* — though he noted that Paul was not on the intelligence committee, that Haspel's confirmation hearing would be held in secret anyway, and that the previous time Paul filibustered a nominee *"he only lasted a day or two."*<Cite s="2018-04-06-scheer-intelligence-john-kiriakou-the-wrong-direction-for-the-cia" t="23:44" /><Cite s="2018-03-21-scott-horton-ep-4629-gina-haspel-s-nomination" t="27:44" /><Cite s="2018-03-21-scott-horton-ep-4629-gina-haspel-s-nomination" t="28:50" />

A week later Paul voted to confirm her. *"It was shocking to me."*<Cite s="2021-09-08-primary-sources-jailing-the-messenger-the-cia-s-torture-whist" t="1:03:39" /><Cite s="2022-12-27-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l" t="1:25:24" /> Kiriakou offers no explanation he is confident in — *"why? Wow, because the CIA is…"* — and settles for the observation that *"that's Washington": "the CIA is in charge here. Donald Trump's not in charge. Rand Paul is not going to throw a wrench into the works."*<Cite s="2024-10-30-bulwarg" t="17:49" /><Cite s="2022-12-27-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l" t="1:25:24" /> In his telling of the wider defeat he assigns the decisive failure to the Democrats — *"the likes of [Mark Warner](/wiki/mark-warner) and Joe Manchin, they went soft"* — and records that after the vote *"the story just went away, it just went away quietly."*<Cite s="2019-02-18-unity4j-vigil-17-confirm-not-panel" t="17:45" /> Another of his attorneys had approached [Ron Wyden](/wiki/ron-wyden) about joining Paul's filibuster; Kiriakou's judgement was that Wyden lacked *"the wherewithal"* to bottle the nomination up in committee.<Cite s="2018-04-06-scheer-intelligence-john-kiriakou-the-wrong-direction-for-the-cia" t="27:04" /><Cite s="2018-03-21-scott-horton-ep-4629-gina-haspel-s-nomination" t="28:50" />

## A rare ally in Congress

Kiriakou's complaint about congressional oversight is that the intelligence committees behave as *"cheerleaders"* or *"lemmings"* rather than overseers, and that nobody on Capitol Hill has *"the guts to stand up to the CIA."*<Cite s="2016-04-26-fort-collins-community-action-network-john-kiriakou-cia-whistle-blower-part-2" t="08:29" /><Cite s="2018-04-06-scheer-intelligence-john-kiriakou-the-wrong-direction-for-the-cia" t="27:04" /> Against that background Paul is one of the few exceptions he will name, alongside Wyden.<Cite s="2024-07-30-spartan-leadership-podcast" t="32:46" />

Asked directly which members of Congress have actually helped him, he narrows the list to two: *"Chuck Grassley is the champion of whistleblowers on Capitol Hill… and the other is Rand Paul."* He has met with both, and says they *"put their own reputations on the line"* for whistleblowers including himself, Bradley Birkenfeld, [Thomas Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake) and [Bill Binney](/wiki/bill-binney) — *"no other 536 members of Congress have ever said anything supportive."*<Cite s="2020-04-20-freedom-hub-working-group-freedom-hub-working-group" t="57:51" /><Cite s="2020-04-20-freedom-hub-working-group-freedom-hub-working-group" t="58:21" /> The practical form that took was legislative: a Republican congressman *"with strong libertarian leanings"* volunteered to write a bill in the House, to be co-introduced in the Senate by Paul, that would vacate Kiriakou's conviction and reinstate his pension.<Cite s="2018-06-05-stack-the-legal-odds-in-yo-the-legal-power-hour-with-second-time-guest-j.sponsors" t="10:13" /> On civil asset forfeiture — which Kiriakou calls a due-process violation, *"a constitutional violation… appalling to me"* — his objection to a critical article was that it failed to mention Paul's bill on the subject, which he thought should have been its foundation.<Cite s="2017-09-05-stack-the-legal-odds-in-yo-the-legal-power-hour-with-guest-john-kiriakou" t="35:51" /><Cite s="2017-09-05-stack-the-legal-odds-in-yo-the-legal-power-hour-with-guest-john-kiriakou" t="36:23" />

Politically Kiriakou places Paul in a libertarian-Republican tradition opposed to the Iraq war and to *"many of these foreign adventures"* — a movement inside the party that *"just abhors overseas entanglement."*<Cite s="2018-04-06-scheer-intelligence-john-kiriakou-the-wrong-direction-for-the-cia" t="23:44" /><Cite s="2016-11-07-this-is-hell-we-should-all-be-afraid-a-whistleblowers-repo" t="27:33" /> In 2016 he named Paul, *"as crazy as it sounds,"* alongside [Bernie Sanders](/wiki/bernie-sanders) as a serious candidate he would trust, while conceding Paul was polling at two per cent and could not win a Republican primary.<Cite s="2016-04-26-fort-collins-community-action-network-john-kiriakou-cia-whistle-blower-part-2" t="09:01" /> His recurring strategic argument is that civil-liberties progress runs through such Republicans rather than around them — Paul, Mike Lee of Utah, Mark Meadows of the House tea party caucus — because *"they're the ones who can really influence the president much more effectively than we can,"* including on a pardon for [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange).<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="42:16" /><Cite s="2021-03-27-revolutionary-change-revolutionary-change" t="22:59" /> He has also warned against depending on so short a bench: Wyden *"is not gonna be around forever, and we can't put all of our eggs in the baskets of Ron Wyden and Mike Lee and Rand Paul."*<Cite s="2020-05-20-action-4-assange-action-4-assange" t="16:45" /><Cite s="2020-05-20-slow-news-day-freeassangevigil-week-46-1-w-john-kiriakou-fi" t="17:04" />

His estimate of Paul's reliability has cooled with the Haspel vote behind him. Asked in 2025 to name legislators who vote on principle, he gives [Thomas Massie](/wiki/thomas-massie) as the genuine article — *"picked right out of the 1980s and put down in today's Congress"* — cannot name a Democrat at all, and allows only that *"Rand Paul will do it a little bit… on the big issues."*<Cite s="2025-03-24-sarah-westall-business-game-changers-cia-coups-coverups-and-torture-cia-whistleblo" t="24:48" /> Asked who will say so publicly rather than privately, he answers that *"Rand Paul and Ron Paul are the only ones that come to my mind."*<Cite s="2021-11-05-live-on-the-fly-with-randy-live-on-the-fly-with-randy" t="31:27" />
