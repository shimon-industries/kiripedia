The **Bojinka plot** was a 1996 operation, planned in the Philippines by [Khalid Sheikh Mohammed](/wiki/khalid-sheikh-mohammed) — then known to U.S. intelligence only by the alias *"Mukhtar"* — to hijack as many as fourteen Boeing 747s out of Manila and crash them, simultaneously, into buildings along the U.S. West Coast.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:15:48" /><Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="44:22" /><Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="11:00" /> Kiriakou says that starting in 1996, the CIA knew al-Qaeda intended to use airplanes as weapons, citing the Bojinka operation as the evidence.<Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="10:26" />

## Disruption by accident

Per Kiriakou, the plot was undone by pure chance. Mukhtar had left his Manila apartment for lunch, leaving planning materials — papers, maps, notes — laid out across the workspace. The apartment's cleaning lady let herself in to clean, saw the materials, and recognized them for what they were: *"What the heck is all this stuff? I better call the police."* She called the Philippine police, who arrived, agreed with her assessment, and in turn alerted the Philippine intelligence service, which contacted the [CIA](/wiki/cia). By the time anyone acted, Mukhtar had fled Manila; the CIA was left with nothing but the alias.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:16:52" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="25:03" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="25:34" /><Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="44:52" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="59:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:00:00" />

Kiriakou has said the foiling of Bojinka confirmed, as early as 1996, that al-Qaeda intended to use hijacked aircraft as weapons — but that even with the plot disrupted, the CIA remained unable to determine when or where a future attack would actually occur, a gap that persisted until September 11, 2001.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="45:55" />

## The six-year identification gap: "Who is Mukhtar?"

The plot's architect remained an unidentified figure in CIA files for six years; the name "Khalid Sheikh Mohammed" appeared in no CIA document. The gap closed only in 2002, during [Ali Soufan](/wiki/ali-soufan)'s FBI interrogation of [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) at a black site. Soufan asked Abu Zubaydah directly, *"Who is Mukhtar?"* Abu Zubaydah laughed: *"You don't know who Mukhtar is?"* — and gave up the name: Khalid Sheikh Mohammed.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="25:34" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="26:05" /><Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="43:51" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:00:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:01:00" /> Kiriakou, who was in the office the day the identification came back, has said plainly: *"We had never heard that name before."* Once identified, KSM was targeted and captured roughly a year later in Rawalpindi, Pakistan.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:01:00" /><Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="45:22" />

## The al-Qaeda wiring diagram

Before Abu Zubaydah's capture, per Kiriakou, the CIA had no picture of al-Qaeda's organizational structure beyond its top two figures: *"We knew Osama bin Laden was at the top, Ayman al-Zawahiri was the number two — and then we didn't have any idea how the rest of it was set up."* Soufan's rapport-based questioning of Abu Zubaydah changed that: posing hypothetical operational scenarios — what would you do if ordered to carry out an attack in Düsseldorf? — Soufan drew out a full mapping of contacts, phone numbers, weapons access, and explosives access, intelligence the CIA could hand directly to German services. It was the first complete picture of how al-Qaeda was compartmentalized and stovepiped.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="58:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="59:00" />

## The bin Laden courier dispute

[CIA](/wiki/cia) officer [Jose Rodriguez](/wiki/jose-rodriguez), Kiriakou's direct supervisor at the Counterterrorism Center, has maintained publicly that the name of Osama bin Laden's courier — which ultimately led to bin Laden's location — came from waterboarding Khalid Sheikh Mohammed. Kiriakou disputes this, saying the CIA had already been tracing the al-Qaeda courier network backward from Al Jazeera since the 1990s — asking where Al Jazeera received bin Laden's tapes, then following the chain back four or five couriers before losing the trail. His assessment: the courier network was already known analytically, and identifying the specific courier did not require coercion. Rodriguez's own former chief of operations has also disputed Rodriguez's account.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:01:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:02:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:02:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:03:00" />

## The cleaning lady

The CIA had known since 1996 that a man operating under the name Mukhtar was working on a plan to use aircraft as missiles. The plan was Bojinka: hijack fourteen 747s out of Manila airport, fly them across the Pacific and fly them into buildings up and down the west coast of the United States.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="19:46" />

It came apart because he left the plans laid out on a table and went out to lunch. The cleaning lady let herself in, looked at them, decided it was a terrorist attack and called the police; the Manila police looked at them, reached the same conclusion and called the Philippine intelligence service; who looked at them, reached the same conclusion again and called the CIA — who confirmed it was an attack being planned against the western United States. By then the man was in the wind, and never came back from lunch.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="20:16" /><Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="20:48" /> It was [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah), years later and under [Ali Soufan](/wiki/ali-soufan)'s questioning, who supplied the name behind Mukhtar.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="21:19" />
