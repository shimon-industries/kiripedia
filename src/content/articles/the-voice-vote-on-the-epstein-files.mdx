**The voice vote on the Epstein files** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of the procedural manoeuvre by which the Senate passed the release of the [Jeffrey Epstein](/wiki/jeffrey-epstein) files in November 2025, and of why he treats it as a demonstration of bipartisan self-protection rather than of accountability.

## Being wrong first

A viewer opened by pointing out that Kiriakou and his co-host on another programme had predicted the Senate would block the release, and that the bill had just passed. He conceded without qualification. *"Yes, I do have some thoughts. So we were wrong about that, for a reason that developed very quickly."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="15:46" />

The reason was presidential. *"John Thune, the Senate majority leader, had said several times over the past week that he was not going to allow this thing to come to a vote. But night before last, [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) urged Republicans to vote yes. They voted yes."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="15:46" />

The House margin made the position untenable. *"The final vote in the House was 417 to one"*, the one being, in Kiriakou's characterisation, *"just a piece of trash from, I think it was, Texas or Louisiana."* Thune, he says, *"realised that the train had left the station and it was barrelling right toward him, and so he decided to get on the right side of this."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="15:46" /><Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="16:18" />

## The rescue

What interests Kiriakou is not the outcome but the method, which he introduces as *"another indication of the insider corruption that are both parties."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="16:18" />

*"To save Thune, Chuck Schumer asked for a voice vote. All those in favour say aye. Aye. All those opposed. The ayes have it. Just like that."* Asked whether that was really the length of time it took, he confirmed it, and confirmed the consequence: *"They didn't do a roll call. No, there was no roll call vote. And so there is no written record of how anybody voted."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="16:18" /><Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="16:53" />

## What the record now permits

The absence of a record is, in his reading, the entire product of the exercise. *"And now literally everybody but this clown from Louisiana can say: I stood up against the pedophiles. I stood up against Epstein."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="16:53" />

On whether the president would then sign it, Kiriakou reported only that he had said he would, and passed on the newspaper's caveat, *"sure, he's going to sign it, and then who's going to ensure that he actually does it?"*, alongside the standing escape route of an ongoing investigation.<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="16:53" /><Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="17:23" />

The passage is one of the sharper statements of a theme Kiriakou returns to constantly: that the two parties compete in public and cooperate in procedure, and that the procedural cooperation is where the real alignment shows.
