In the six to eight months before the September 11 attacks, per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the Bush administration had negotiated a deal with the Mexican government to change the status of undocumented Mexican workers in the United States: they would be given nine-month U.S. work visas, during which they would have to pay federal income taxes, then return to Mexico for three months before reapplying for the following year — allowing them to work nine months a year in the U.S. and three in Mexico.<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="2:22:09" /><Cite s="2025-08-21-eric-hunley-john-kiriakou-cia-whistleblower-exposes-the-deep-state" t="59:43" /> Kiriakou has repeated the account elsewhere in nearly identical terms, calling the plan one that *"would have legalized everybody"* and *"would have worked,"* and calling it a mistake that Trump did not revive something like it.<Cite s="2025-06-15-joe-dirosa" t="22:36" /> In every telling, the plan was never implemented because the September 11 attacks intervened before it could be ratified.<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="2:22:09" /><Cite s="2025-06-15-joe-dirosa" t="22:36" />
