The **Directorate of Science and Technology**, or DS&T, is one of the CIA's four directorates, alongside Operations, Intelligence (analysis) and Administration.<Cite s="2024-10-30-bulwarg" t="41:49" /> Many thousands of people work in it, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s estimate.<Cite s="2025-05-05-austin-and-matt" t="10:34" />

## The gadget shop

When Kiriakou was at the agency the directorate was *"almost an afterthought"*: it was responsible for disguises, for briefcases with secret pockets in them, and generally for supporting operations.<Cite s="2025-09-08-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="01:33" /><Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="05:43" />

His own operational encounter with it is the best illustration. Asking headquarters for technical help in narrowing down [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah)'s location in Pakistan, Kiriakou was sent two DS&T officers who both looked about sixteen years old; what they produced, after two days in the station's tech shack, was the [magic box](/wiki/magic-box) — a cigar-box-sized device assembled from wires, capacitors and LEDs bought at the Pakistani equivalent of Radio Shack, which beeped and swung a compass needle toward the direction of a mobile telephone call.<Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="14:12" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="11:13" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-dead-drop-s1e16-captured" t="11:44" />

## What it became

Kiriakou's account of the modern directorate is the reverse. It is now, he says, so sophisticated and so far advanced — working with the likes of [Palantir](/wiki/palantir), Elon Musk's various companies, Amazon Web Services and Nvidia — that it has handed the silly concealment devices and disguises off to outside individuals or makeup companies and does *"the big ticket items"* instead. What the DS&T is doing with things like virtual reality, he says, is closer to DARPA's futuristic work than to anything the CIA ever did in the past.<Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="05:43" /><Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="06:45" /> Asked for an example of a big-ticket item, he declines — not out of discretion but ignorance: *"I couldn't even venture to say. It's become so sophisticated."* He was never cleared even to know what DARPA was doing.<Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="06:45" />

## The analyst who became chief technical officer

Kiriakou illustrates the directorate's rise with the career of a man he sat next to in his twenties — a terrific analyst with a thing for technology, whom the office joked about. On three days off, as a favour, the man wrote a program that let Middle East analysts build family trees of the royal families they covered; no such software existed, because this was before the public internet. When the internet arrived he *"jumped right in"*. He is now the CIA's chief technical officer at SIS-4, and Kiriakou says he could be the director if the president wanted him to be. A friend's verdict on the promotion has stayed with him: *"It was the tech."*<Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="07:16" /><Cite s="2026-05-24-john-kiriakou-podcast-the-most-secret-cia-unit-ever" t="08:17" />

## In the abolition argument

The directorate features in Kiriakou's case for [abolishing the CIA](/wiki/abolish-the-cia): its functions, like the agency's analytic and collection work, are already duplicated across a dozen government organisations and could be spread among them.<Cite s="2015-08-18-politics-done-right-politics-done-right" t="30:16" /> He is careful, in the same breath, about the people: most of those in the directorate, like most of those at the agency, are there because they are patriotic and want to do something in public service.<Cite s="2019-04-25-david-hartful-john-kiriakou-speaks-at-venezuelan-embassy-04" t="46:33" />
