**Kiriakou's Cypriot ancestry** came to light through a DNA test taken around the time he was invited to speak at the Cypriots of the Diaspora conference in Limassol. The result: 24 per cent Cypriot — the largest single component of his ancestry, the remainder divided between the Greek islands and the Black Sea.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="29:38" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="30:09" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="30:42" />

The finding corrected a family belief. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) had grown up being told by his grandfather that the family was originally from Crete. He raised it with his cousin Mark Kiriakou, who told him: "Not Crete, Cyprus." Kiriakou pushed back — "No, he didn't say Cyprus. He said Crete" — and was wrong.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="30:09" /> His Nicosia host offered the corroborating point that the surname is uncommon in Greece and very common in Cyprus.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="30:09" />

Kiriakou accepted the conclusion directly: "I'm a Cypriot."<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="30:42" /> He had visited the island only once before, thirty-four years earlier, and said he was embarrassed that so many years had passed.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="32:47" />

An interview given on the island in 2026 catches him well into a stay: ten days in, he said, he had eaten nothing but Cypriot food, had had sheftalia earlier that week, and had worked through twenty different Cypriot cheeses — the basis for [the comparison he then drew with Greek cooking](/wiki/kiriakou-on-greek-and-cypriot-food).<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="23:38" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="24:40" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="26:14" />

His Greek citizenship came separately and earlier, granted before he went to prison after the Greek ambassador telephoned to ask how he could be helpful; Kiriakou asked for citizenship, and it was extended to all five of his children.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="29:38" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="31:44" />
