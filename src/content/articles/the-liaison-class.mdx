**The liaison class** was a course [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) ran at the CIA for officers of friendly foreign intelligence services — the sort of partner-training that underpins the agency's [liaison relationships](/wiki/general-intelligence-directorate), and which he describes with a certain modesty as *"John's little liaison class."*<Cite s="2026-02-02-dead-drop-s1e13-hostages" t="19:30" />

Its place in his account is fixed by a single visit. Weeks before 11 September 2001, [Cofer Black](/wiki/cofer-black) — the head of the [Counterterrorism Center](/wiki/counterterrorism-center), then meeting the president every day or every other day — made time to come and speak to the class. What he told them was that al-Qaeda members were saying goodbye to their families and that the agency was hearing code words for a massive attack: a honey salesman coming with vast quantities of honey, a great football match, an enormous wedding. He then asked them directly for any sources they had inside al-Qaeda.<Cite s="2026-02-02-dead-drop-s1e13-hostages" t="18:57" />

The class, in Kiriakou's account, *"just sat there and looked at him."* Black shook their hands and left.<Cite s="2026-02-02-dead-drop-s1e13-hostages" t="19:30" />

Kiriakou sent the visiting officers back to their hotel with an arrangement to collect them at seven for a steakhouse dinner — and used the gap to go to Black's office, ostensibly to thank him for coming and in fact to ask whether he had meant it. The answer he got was *"I was deadly serious. Something terrible is going to happen."*<Cite s="2026-02-02-dead-drop-s1e13-hostages" t="19:30" /><Cite s="2026-02-02-dead-drop-s1e13-hostages" t="20:00" />

The episode is why the [warning](/wiki/cofer-black-pre-9-11-warning) is first-hand rather than institutional in Kiriakou's telling: he did not work on al-Qaeda, and heard it because he happened to be the officer running the room.<Cite s="2026-02-02-dead-drop-s1e13-hostages" t="19:30" />
