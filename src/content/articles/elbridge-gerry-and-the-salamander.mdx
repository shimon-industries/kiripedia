The etymology came up in the middle of a discussion of the 2025 redistricting battles, after Kiriakou's co-host described the famous early American political cartoon of a voting district drawn as a dragon-like monster, and attributed it to Benjamin Franklin's paper. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="23:26" />

## The name

Kiriakou supplied the background. The man was Elbridge Gerry — he spells it, "G-E-R-R-Y" — and notes that the name was pronounced *Gary*. Gerry was the fourth vice president of the United States, and before that the governor of Massachusetts; Kiriakou places him as Madison's vice president. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="23:58" />

Gerry was, in his description, "intensely partisan," and in order to get his friends elected to Congress "he just took out a map of Massachusetts and he drew the district boundaries." <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="24:31" />

When Benjamin Franklin saw the resulting shapes, Kiriakou says, he remarked that they looked like salamanders and called them "Gerry's salamanders." That became *Gerrymander*, and over the years *gerrymander*. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="24:31" />

## The cemetery footnote

Kiriakou then adds what he calls "a little bit of trivia from John's someday-to-be-published cemeteries book": Elbridge Gerry is the only vice president of the United States buried inside Washington, DC. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="24:31" />

Asked where, he answers precisely — Congressional Cemetery, up on Capitol Hill. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="25:02" /> The aside is one of several references Kiriakou makes to a manuscript on cemeteries that had not, at the time of the broadcast, found its way into print.

## The context

The history was offered as perspective on the redistricting fights then under way. Kiriakou had just laid out the sequence — the Texas House preparing to redraw boundaries in a way likely to cost five Democratic seats, California's countermeasure requiring a referendum and a special election, the Republican National Committee committing $15 million in advertising to defeat it, and New York and Illinois considering their own redraws against Missouri and Florida. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="23:26" />

His verdict on the whole episode was that "we have never seen anything like this in the history of the republic" — bad form, sour grapes, and "the worst of gerrymandering." He noted the same afternoon's federal ruling that Mississippi's boundaries were illegally gerrymandered and would have to be redrawn to add a majority-Black district. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="23:26" /> Neither man claims the cartoon was truly the first of its kind. <Cite s="2025-08-21-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="25:02" />
