**Saudi influence in Egypt** is, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s description, both financial and old.<Cite s="2015-09-23-mintpress-news-former-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-takes-us-insid" t="14:10" />

## Bankrolling, not supporting

Asked about Egypt after the removal of the Muslim Brotherhood government, Kiriakou agrees that the Saudis back its successor and then sharpens the verb: they are not just supporting that government but bankrolling it and ensuring its survival. The automatic transcript renders the government's name as *"the CC government"*; Kiriakou is speaking of the administration that followed the Brotherhood's removal.<Cite s="2015-09-23-mintpress-news-former-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-takes-us-insid" t="14:10" />

## The longer history

He declines to treat this as a response to the Arab Spring. Saudi Arabia, he says, has a very long history of meddling in Egyptian internal affairs going back to 1958 and to the nationalist revolution in Egypt that year.<Cite s="2015-09-23-mintpress-news-former-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-takes-us-insid" t="14:10" />

## What it means for an uprising

The consequence he draws is the one that governs his wider pessimism about [change in the region](/wiki/democratic-change-in-the-middle-east): anybody participating in a popular uprising is going to have to fight against Saudi money and Saudi weapons, which is why he expects the process to take decades.<Cite s="2015-09-23-mintpress-news-former-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-takes-us-insid" t="14:40" />
