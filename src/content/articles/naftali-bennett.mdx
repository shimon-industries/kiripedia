**Naftali Bennett** is a former prime minister of Israel whom [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites for the proposition that the [2026 Iran war](/wiki/2026-iran-war) was not intended to be the last one.

Discussing which states obstruct [the Greater Israel project](/wiki/greater-israel), Kiriakou named two: *"Iran and Turkey, which is why Naftali Bennett, the former Israeli prime minister, is also now saying after Iran, it's Turkey. Turkey's next."*<Cite s="2026-03-31-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-john-kiriakou-israel-s-samson-option-is-real" t="03:58" /><Cite s="2026-03-11-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-john-kiriakou-what-s-really-happening-in-the-ir" t="48:47" /> The remark was an answer to the framing that Iran was the last thing standing in the way of the project — Kiriakou's correction being that there were two things, not one.<Cite s="2026-03-11-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-john-kiriakou-what-s-really-happening-in-the-ir" t="48:47" />

Kiriakou's own forecast is that neither objective is achievable — *"I don't see them succeeding. There are too many Arabs. There are too many Muslims. There are too many people who oppose this naked Israeli aggression."*<Cite s="2026-03-31-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-john-kiriakou-israel-s-samson-option-is-real" t="03:58" /><Cite s="2026-03-31-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-john-kiriakou-israel-s-samson-option-is-real" t="04:29" />

## From citation to assertion

By May 2026 the Bennett threat had stopped being something Kiriakou attributed and become something he asserted. Asked about Israel's repeated threats against Turkey, made *"primarily through its former prime minister Naftali Bennett,"* he answered flatly: *"They do. They're actively seeking war with Turkey. Turkey is their last impediment to them realizing their greater Israel dream."*<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="47:59" />

The corroboration he offers is a conversation rather than an analysis. He says he had recently had dinner in Washington with a Turkish deputy minister — a man he had never met, who reached out to him — and put exactly that question to him. The answer was not defensive: *"We welcome an Israeli attack on Turkey."*<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="47:59" /><Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="48:34" /> The obstacle Kiriakou then had to account for was the alliance structure — Turkey's [NATO membership and Article 5](/wiki/israel-turkey-nato-article-5), which would mean Israel could not realistically fight Turkey alone.<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="47:23" />
