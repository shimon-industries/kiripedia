**Tony Blinken** served as United States secretary of state under [Joe Biden](/wiki/joe-biden). [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) knew him from the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate-foreign-relations-committee), where Blinken was staff director and where Kiriakou inherited his desk, and offers him as an illustration both of how senior American foreign-policy appointments are made and of what happens when the appointment does not fit the man.<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" /><Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="09:35" /><Cite s="2026-01-01-fair-observer" t="14:06" />

The two men crossed at the committee rather than overlapping on it. Kiriakou's tellings vary on the exact handover: in one he took the desk when Blinken *"finally went to the White House"*, in another Blinken *"moved to State when I moved to the Senate Foreign Relations Committee"*, and in a third Blinken left the committee the week Kiriakou arrived.<Cite s="2021-02-01-the-bitter-truth-with-abe-the-bitter-truth-with-abe" t="08:21" /><Cite s="2026-01-01-fair-observer" t="14:06" /><Cite s="2021-02-05-actvism-munich-bezahlung-f-r-begnadigungen-cia-whistleblower" t="19:04" />

## A career spent working for one man

Kiriakou's central claim about Blinken is a career ladder with a single employer at every rung. Out of Harvard he became a junior legislative aide to Senator Joe Biden; after some years he became Biden's legislative director; he moved to the Senate Foreign Relations Committee as staff director when Biden took the chairmanship; when Biden was elected vice president Blinken became his national security adviser; in Obama's second term he became deputy secretary of state; and when Biden was elected president he became secretary of state.<Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="09:35" /><Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="10:11" /> The earlier telling of the same ladder runs through years in Biden's personal Senate office and puts the vice-presidential job as national security adviser and then deputy national security adviser.<Cite s="2021-02-01-the-bitter-truth-with-abe-the-bitter-truth-with-abe" t="08:21" /><Cite s="2021-02-01-the-bitter-truth-with-abe-the-bitter-truth-with-abe" t="08:52" />

*"He owes his entire adult life to Joe Biden. Every job he's ever had in his life has been working for Joe Biden."*<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" /> On Kiriakou's reading, Blinken was not appointed *"because he's the smartest, most forward-thinking strategist that the United States could identify"* but because he had been Biden's friend and employee for thirty years. *"That's it."*<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="27:03" />

## Kiriakou's assessment

Kiriakou is careful to separate the man from the appointment: *"I know Tony Blinken. I worked with Tony Blinken on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. He's a very nice man."*<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" /> The fuller judgement is warmer and harsher at once, *"Tony is a lovely guy, he's very smart, and he is in completely over his head. He's a great intellect, and he belongs at a think tank, not on the president's cabinet as the secretary of state."*<Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="10:47" /><Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="11:52" /> He returns to the same formula years apart: Blinken is *"a great guy and a smart guy, but he was not equipped to be Secretary of State."*<Cite s="2026-01-01-fair-observer" t="14:36" />

## The de facto secretary of state

The evidence Kiriakou offers for the mismatch is not an opinion but a pattern of assignments. [Bill Burns](/wiki/bill-burns) had also been deputy secretary of state, in Obama's first term, and held the rank of career ambassador, the highest a diplomat can attain, with postings in some seven places including Russia. Putting him at the CIA meant that if Blinken *"screws things up, you have an adult in the room."*<Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="10:47" /><Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="11:19" />

So when war broke out in the Middle East, the ceasefire negotiations between the warring sides went not to the secretary of state but to the director of central intelligence. *"Why do you do that? You do it because he's a better diplomat than the secretary of state is."*<Cite s="2024-12-19-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-the-cia-torture-and-more-the" t="11:19" /> Kiriakou states the conclusion flatly: the one anomaly of Burns's tenure was that he was *"the de facto Secretary of State"*, flying all over the world whenever there was sensitive or difficult diplomacy to be carried out.<Cite s="2026-01-01-fair-observer" t="14:06" /><Cite s="2026-01-01-fair-observer" t="14:36" />

## Foreign policy and the confirmation that never happened

Kiriakou places Blinken in a continuity rather than a break. Blinken *"parrots the Biden neoliberal foreign policy"*, and the reason Republicans did not challenge him at his confirmation hearing is, in Kiriakou's account, that *"there is no difference between a neo-liberal foreign policy and a neoconservative foreign policy. It's interventionist no matter who's in the White House."*<Cite s="2021-02-01-the-bitter-truth-with-abe-the-bitter-truth-with-abe" t="08:21" /><Cite s="2021-02-01-the-bitter-truth-with-abe-the-bitter-truth-with-abe" t="08:52" />

On Gaza he describes Blinken as the top of a divided department: an upper echelon that would give the Israelis everything they wanted, and lower-ranking State Department officers who were angry and wanted the administration to cut off arms, or at least threaten to.<Cite s="2025-08-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="39:21" />

## The context of the criticism

Kiriakou raises Blinken as the third element of a three-part diagnosis of American decline abroad. The first is the reliance on the military; the second is the domestic hostility to foreign aid that leaves the United States with nothing to offer countries whose votes and cooperation it wants. The third is *"in many cases poor leadership."*<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" />

He immediately pairs the point with his admiration for [Sergey Lavrov](/wiki/sergey-lavrov), whom he describes as having outperformed far better-resourced Western ambassadors at the United Nations, the comparison that gives the criticism its force.<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="27:33" /><Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="28:37" />
