Asked whether a Russian–Ukrainian settlement would need to be permanent, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) agreed that it would and set out the shape he expected it to take.<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="26:46" />

## Territory

*"It''s going to have to be that the Russians keep Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson, Crimea, the Russian-speaking areas that they control de facto now. I think they''ll end up controlling formally once this is over."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="27:18" />

## The membership question

The second Russian condition, he says, is that *"Ukraine cannot ever be a member of NATO."* His response is that the condition costs less than it appears to: *"But that''s a misnomer, because Ukraine doesn''t have to be a member of NATO. Australia is not a member of NATO. Japan, South Korea, Saudi Arabia, UAE, they''re not members of NATO. They''re what''s called major non-NATO allies. That''s an official status."* He expects the United States to concede the point in form and recover it in substance: *"I wouldn''t be surprised if the United States agrees with the Russians that no, Ukraine should not be a member of NATO, it will never be a member of NATO. But it could be granted major non-NATO ally status, thus giving it essentially the same rights as a NATO country."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="27:49" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="28:21" />

## The Russian concession

*"And I think what the Russians will concede is that Ukraine will get fast-track membership in the European Union. And then the EU begins the rebuilding of Ukraine, and that''s going to be the end of it."* His summary is that the war is close to its end and that the end will disappoint the side he is on: *"the end is not going to be what the United States and Ukraine have hoped for, but it''s going to be enough to put this thing to rest."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="28:21" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="28:51" />

## What the status actually buys

Kiriakou has described the designation as a real instrument rather than a courtesy, and defined it by what it unlocks. Japan, he noted in April 2024, had been given *"major non-NATO ally status, that''s an actual status, with capital letters. Saudi Arabia is a major non-NATO ally. Bahrain is a major non-NATO ally. My guess is the Philippines is going to get it soon."*<Cite s="2024-04-18-dr-david-oualaalou-iran-israel-possible-open-war" t="30:05" />

Asked what it means in practice: *"they have access to some weapons and weapon systems that they otherwise wouldn''t have access to. For example the UAE, Australia, Saudi Arabia, they have access to the [F-35](/wiki/f-35-israel-avionics). So we''ll give the F-35 to the Japanese as well. And we don''t for other countries, in fact [Turkey is a member of NATO and we won''t give them the F-35](/wiki/f-35-sale-to-turkey)."*<Cite s="2024-04-18-dr-david-oualaalou-iran-israel-possible-open-war" t="30:37" />

He attaches to it a separate objection about the alliance''s spending rule: the two per cent of GDP guideline is met by only *"three or four NATO countries"*, and yet *"we''re demanding it, and we''re forcing them to spend more money. It''s very dangerous."*<Cite s="2024-04-18-dr-david-oualaalou-iran-israel-possible-open-war" t="31:09" />
