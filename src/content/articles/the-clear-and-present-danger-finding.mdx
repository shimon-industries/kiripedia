**The clear and present danger finding** is the authorisation [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes as standing between the CIA's paramilitary capability and its use, set out in answer to a viewer who asked whether CIA paramilitary officers were already on the ground in Iran in early 2026.

## The answer

Kiriakou's estimate was that they were not, hedged with a caveat about his own currency. *"I would say probably not. Now, granted, my my information is dated."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="18:18" />

## The requirement

What he offers in place of current knowledge is the procedure. To have CIA paramilitary officers on the ground carrying out targeted killings, *"the president would have to um assert that a person is uh what's called a clear and present danger to the United States, to an American installation, um and to or to an American person."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="18:18" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="18:49" />

The finding is therefore person-specific rather than theatre-wide: it attaches to a named individual and to a category of American interest that the individual is said to endanger. A war does not supply it automatically.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="18:49" />

## The inference

Kiriakou reasons from the absence of the finding to the absence of the officers: *"And I I don't think we're there."* The claim is not that the capability is unavailable but that the legal predicate has not been laid.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="18:49" />

## Why the standard matters to him

The threshold is the same one that underlies Kiriakou's broader moral objection to the agency. Asked in a separate session whether the CIA was the world's most moral intelligence service, he answered that [most other countries' services are more moral](/wiki/more-moral-than-the-cia), and located the disqualifying conduct precisely in killing *"people whose politics you don't don't like, but who pose no threat to the United States."*<Cite s="2026-03-03-the-deep-focus-iran-escalation-captured-spies-what-really-happens" t="24:57" />

The clear and present danger finding is the formal expression of the distinction he is making: a requirement that lethal action be tied to a demonstrated threat rather than to a disliked politics. His criticism of the CIA is not that the requirement exists but that, in his account, it has not reliably constrained what the organisation does.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-john-kiriakou-ted-rall-s-live-iran-war-q-a-de" t="18:49" />
