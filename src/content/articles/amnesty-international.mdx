**Amnesty International** plays two opposite roles in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s story: witness for his defense, and bystander at his prosecution.

## The torture report

Amnesty's report that the CIA was torturing prisoners — following [Human Rights Watch](/wiki/human-rights-watch)'s and preceding or accompanying the International Committee of the Red Cross's — is a fixed point in Kiriakou's account of his 2007 [ABC interview](/wiki/brian-ross): the torture was already public knowledge, so his confirmation *"added nothing, technically, to the debate."* What he added was being the first current or former CIA officer to say it.<Cite s="2015-05-06-the-real-news-network-why-i-was-targeted-by-the-cia-rai-9-10" t="09:25" /><Cite s="2017-05-31-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="36:46" /><Cite s="2015-07-25-splendour-in-the-grass" t="13:04" />

The ordering shifts between tellings and is worth recording rather than smoothing. The commonest version puts Human Rights Watch in 2005, Amnesty in 2006 and the Red Cross in 2007.<Cite s="2017-05-31-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="36:46" /><Cite s="2024-10-06-joecat" t="30:31" /> Another puts Human Rights Watch in 2006 and both Amnesty and the Red Cross in 2007.<Cite s="2016-07-11-adventures-in-the-free-sta" t="24:49" /> A third has the Red Cross first, two years before he went public.<Cite s="2023-02-18-savage-joy-marie-mann-savage-joy-and-john-kiriakou" t="42:38" /> In the version he gives most often now, the point is not the sequence but the window: all three reports were out in the twelve months before he spoke, so *"I didn't think I was saying anything new."*<Cite s="2026-05-27-the-information-rights-pro" t="09:52" /><Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="05:46" /><Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="33:51" />

That argument was not only his. The FBI investigated the ABC interview, watched the tape, and concluded the information was already common knowledge and no crime had been committed.<Cite s="2015-05-06-the-real-news-network-why-i-was-targeted-by-the-cia-rai-9-10" t="09:25" /><Cite s="2016-07-11-adventures-in-the-free-sta" t="24:49" /> The declination letter sent to his attorney named the three organisations explicitly — Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International and the Red Cross had all published papers saying the CIA was torturing prisoners — before the case was reopened.<Cite s="2023-07-22-david-pakman-show-rudy-trump-offered-me-a-pardon-for-2m" t="03:07" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="33:21" /><Cite s="2025-04-27-the-dr-phil-podcast-short-clip-of-the-full-dr-phil-ep" t="13:03" /><Cite s="2025-11-03-hispaunidad-hispaunidad" t="28:31" />

Kiriakou also cites Amnesty, alongside Human Rights Watch and the Red Cross, on the drone war: reports that some 90% of those killed were innocent civilians who happened to be near the strike site. The value he assigns them there is the same — independent voices against an agency asking the public to take its word that the dead were bad people.<Cite s="2016-04-26-adelya-f-hako-beyond-the-horizon-s1-e9-john-kiriakou" t="10:14" /><Cite s="2017-06-30-ryot-lies-and-torture-of-the-cia-with-john-kiriako" t="10:06" />

## The silence

Asked at a Berlin event why he never mentions Amnesty International, Kiriakou's answer was unsparing: *"I begged Amnesty International to release even a one-sentence statement about my case."* He wrote to headquarters in London, and to Zeke Johnson, director of Amnesty International USA — *"who was kind enough to send me a subscription to The New Yorker magazine, along with a letter saying I'm sorry, but they don't want to have anything to do with your case."* After about a year, he got tired of begging. *"I got zero help from Amnesty."*<Cite s="2017-06-06-disruption-network-lab-locked-up-for-exposing-crimes-kiriakou-annie" t="1:30:03" /><Cite s="2017-06-06-disruption-network-lab-locked-up-for-exposing-crimes-kiriakou-annie" t="1:30:35" />

The refusal was not Amnesty's alone. *"When I was arrested they ran screaming from the room. I got literally no help from Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch or the ACLU — nothing."*<Cite s="2018-08-07-unity4j-full-interview-probably-solo-confirm" t="52:53" /> He extends the diagnosis: the organisations are *"very in favour of whistleblowers elsewhere in the world… not with American whistleblowers,"* and their statements tend to appear only once public support has already built, which he calls reactionary rather than leading.<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="38:27" /><Cite s="2018-08-07-unity4j-full-interview-probably-solo-confirm" t="52:22" /> In the same account of who stood where, the ACLU's national office would have supported him but the organisation decides regionally, and a member of its board resigned in protest at its refusal to make a statement during his trial.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="49:33" />

Kiriakou's account of Johnson himself has softened across tellings. The 2017 version has the New Yorker subscription arriving with a letter of refusal. In 2021 and again in 2025 he describes Johnson as *"an awesome guy"* who wrote to him in prison apologising for his bosses in London — *"hey, we think we made a mistake, we're sorry, just wanted you to know, we're pulling for you"* — and then bought the subscription *"to kind of dull the pain."*<Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="38:27" /><Cite s="2021-05-21-scheer-intelligence-a-former-cia-mideast-expert-s-view-of-the-isr" t="38:58" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:05" /> The blame in the later versions sits with London, not with the American office.

## Ai Weiwei and the reversal

What eventually moved Amnesty was not Kiriakou. [Ai Weiwei](/wiki/ai-weiwei) was making Lego portraits of political prisoners — 178 of them, in Kiriakou's count — and wanted the names cleared with Amnesty International first. Told that Amnesty did not believe Kiriakou was a political prisoner, the artist answered *"I know Kiriakou. I'm telling you, he is a political prisoner."* Amnesty agreed in the week Kiriakou was released, having been *"pulled kicking and screaming"* to it.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:36" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="51:06" /><Cite s="2018-08-07-unity4j-full-interview-probably-solo-confirm" t="53:24" /> Human Rights Watch, he says, came round before they did.<Cite s="2018-08-07-unity4j-full-interview-probably-solo-confirm" t="52:53" /> He has placed the portrait exhibition at the Smithsonian Institution in one telling and at Alcatraz in another.<Cite s="2018-08-07-unity4j-full-interview-probably-solo-confirm" t="53:24" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="50:36" />

## The name a prisoner heard

The organisation's standing among prisoners surfaces in an anecdote Kiriakou tells about a Pakistani safe house. He had brought [Cofer Black](/wiki/cofer-black) in to see a detainee and introduced him as *"Mr Cofer Black of the Central Intelligence Agency"*; Black asked whether the man was getting enough food, sleeping well, being treated properly. Returning alone afterwards, Kiriakou was thanked: *"Sir, I have to thank you so much for bringing Amnesty International to come and check on me."* He did not correct him — *"I didn't say he was from Amnesty International, I said he was from the Central Intelligence Agency"* — and answered instead that *"we're not animals, we're treating you with respect."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="13:05" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="13:35" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="14:08" />

The same expectation defines by its absence what Guantánamo withholds. Among the rights Kiriakou lists as denied there — no access to the Supreme Court, no due process — is the right to be visited by the Red Cross and by Amnesty International.<Cite s="2023-12-07-truthovercomfort" t="24:40" />
