**In-Q-Tel** is the [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia)'s openly acknowledged venture capital arm. Its function is the financing of private-sector development of technologies the agency intends to deploy, a practice that, post-September 11, came to dominate the agency's technical-acquisition strategy. *"It is far cheaper and far easier to just buy something that's already been invented than it is to develop it from scratch. … Post-9/11, money is not an issue at all. And so DARPA is doing it, NSA's doing it, CIA's doing it, and then within In-Q-Tel they're financing it in the private sector. So the sky's the limit."*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="42:18" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="42:51" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="43:53" />

## Palantir

The single most prominent In-Q-Tel investment to date is [Palantir Technologies](/wiki/palantir): *"They gave Palantir their first, it was like a million and a half, to get off the ground. Very first investment. And now look at Palantir: just this year for the first time they have more than a billion dollars in revenue. So it's a success story."* Kiriakou notes that within the CIA itself Palantir was initially unpopular but is now widely used for intelligence applications including targeting.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="43:53" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="44:24" />

By late 2025 Palantir's market valuation had crossed **$1 trillion**, with the majority of its revenue coming from CIA contracts.<Cite s="2025-11-19-julian-dorey-vault-7" t="43:46" />

## Etymology and legality

The agency's venture-capital arm was created in the immediate aftermath of September 11, 2001, when the CIA obtained congressional approval, made possible by a legal waiver under the Patriot Act, to invest in private companies through a venture-capital vehicle.<Cite s="2026-01-26-tin-foil-hat-w-sam-tripoli" t="14:34" /><Cite s="2026-01-13-covertaction-magazine" t="1:15:12" /> Kiriakou notes the name as a deliberate construction, *"intel"* + *"Q"* (a wink at Q, the quartermaster of the [James Bond](/wiki/james-bond) films), and characterizes the existence of a for-profit CIA venture business as itself a category of conduct that would otherwise be illegal: *"the CIA now is in the venture capital business for a profit. Which is illegal. … Not if you're writing the laws."*<Cite s="2025-02-19-katie-halper" t="1:02:10" /><Cite s="2025-11-19-julian-dorey-vault-7" t="42:41" /><Cite s="2025-11-19-julian-dorey-vault-7" t="43:13" /> In-Q-Tel is, in Kiriakou's description, wholly owned by the CIA.<Cite s="2026-01-01-fair-observer" t="39:09" /> He draws a parallel between In-Q-Tel and the broader shift toward [U.S. military venture capital](/wiki/us-military-venture-capital) in national-security technology acquisition.

## The intelligence-tech pipeline

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites [Will Hurd](/wiki/will-hurd), a CIA case officer he mentored who went on to Congress and the OpenAI board, as a further example of the same intelligence-and-technology convergence that In-Q-Tel embodies.<Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="52:43" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="53:46" />

## The waiver and the first bet on Palantir

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says that after 9/11 the CIA got a congressional waiver to open a venture-capital arm, [In-Q-Tel](/wiki/in-q-tel), and that its very first investment went to [Palantir](/wiki/palantir), now a multibillion-dollar company. He marvels that this is even legal.<Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="54:17" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="54:50" /> Kiriakou has given differing figures for that first check across interviews, 1.5 million dollars in one account and 15 million dollars, which he called *"a fortune in 2002,"* in another.<Cite s="2026-01-26-tin-foil-hat-w-sam-tripoli" t="14:34" />
