The **Greek neo-Nazi seats** are the result [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) singled out from the June 2023 Greek parliamentary election as the sharpest instance of a continent-wide rightward shift.

## The count

Kiriakou raised Greece as a correction to the coverage. Much had been made in the press that week about the ascendance of the far right in France and Italy, he said — noting that Italy had a fascist prime minister and that the most popular party in France, if polls were to be believed, was the extreme right party.<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="58:51" />

But *"Greece had its elections two weeks ago, and eight parties won seats in parliament. Six of them are right-wing parties, and four of them are right-wing extremist parties. There are two neo-Nazi parties that won a total of 13 seats in the Greek parliament."*<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="58:51" />

## The wider trend

He read the Greek figures as part of a pattern rather than an outlier: *"This is a very disturbing trend that we're seeing all over Western Europe. And we already are seeing entrenched right-wing parties in Poland, in Hungary, in the UK, let's be honest."*<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="59:25" />

On the British case he named a specific policy as evidence — see [the Rwanda deportations](/wiki/the-rwanda-deportations).<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="59:25" />

Earlier in the same exchange, Kiriakou had summarised the electoral choice now on offer across much of Western Europe as *"a right-wing government, a far right-wing government, or an extreme right-wing government. Those are their choices."*<Cite s="2023-07-06-am-wakeup-false-flags-real-terrorists-w-john-kiriakou" t="57:03" />
