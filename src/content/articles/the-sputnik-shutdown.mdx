**The Sputnik shutdown** is John Kiriakou's account of losing his broadcasting job in 2020, given on the DeProgram programme in August 2025 while discussing a former colleague's later success.<Cite s="2025-08-12-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="44:06" />

## Before it

The programme in question is the one on which Kiriakou first met his eventual DeProgram co-host. Asked where they had met, he answered that it was *"when you came on the show that Brian Becker and I were doing in 2017"*, a date that places the collaboration with [Ted Rall](/wiki/ted-rall) eight years before the conversation.<Cite s="2025-08-12-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="43:36" />

## The closure

Kiriakou describes the ending with a correction about his own position. *"When we lost our jobs at, actually, I had left Sputnik briefly. I ended up going back, but when our show was shut down at Sputnik back in 2020..."*<Cite s="2025-08-12-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="44:06" />

He gives no reason for the closure and does not describe any dispute over it.

## What followed

What interests him is the recovery, and how small the capital requirement turned out to be. His co-host *"did a GoFundMe to raise five grand just for some sound equipment, a decent camera, and a microphone. And that's how he started"* his own programme.<Cite s="2025-08-12-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="44:06" />

Kiriakou's assessment of the outcome is admiring and blunt: *"And now he's a monster. He's making more money than he's ever made in his life."*<Cite s="2025-08-12-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="44:06" />

He adds that he holds the example up deliberately: *"I point to Brian as an example of what I hope we can accomplish, all the time."* He had last seen him in February, at a counter-inauguration event.<Cite s="2025-08-12-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="43:36" />

## Its place in his career

The episode marks the transition in Kiriakou's post-prison working life from institutional broadcasting to independent, audience-funded programmes, the model under which the DeProgram show itself operates.
