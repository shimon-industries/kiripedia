**Medea Benjamin's broken arm** is an incident [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) volunteers as a concrete illustration of Egyptian conduct at the Gaza border, offered in response to a general account of surveillance and repression met by activists travelling through Egypt.

## The account

His interlocutor had described what participants in a march to Gaza the previous summer reported of Egyptian policing. Kiriakou answered with a case rather than an assessment: *"it was just a couple of years ago that media Benjamin went with a group from Code Pink. And the Egyptians literally broke her arm."*<Cite s="2025-11-14-the-deep-focus-show-taxpayers-against-genocide-w-seth-donnelly" t="20:56" />

He underlines the disproportion by size. *"I mean, Medadia is a little tiny. I mean, maybe maybe she's 90 or 95 pounds"* — *"but they they physically broke her arm."*<Cite s="2025-11-14-the-deep-focus-show-taxpayers-against-genocide-w-seth-donnelly" t="20:56" />

The circumstances of the arrest matter to the point he is making: it happened *"um arresting her just because she was trying to get to Rafa to uh escort a shipment of of food"* into Gaza.<Cite s="2025-11-14-the-deep-focus-show-taxpayers-against-genocide-w-seth-donnelly" t="21:26" />

## What it is offered to show

Kiriakou raises the episode inside a longer argument about Egypt's double position on the Rafah crossing — a country whose people, on his reading, want the border open and whose government does not. His judgement of the government is unambiguous: *"I think that the Egyptian government wants it closed. And in that respect, they're they're partnered with the Israelis."*<Cite s="2025-11-14-the-deep-focus-show-taxpayers-against-genocide-w-seth-donnelly" t="17:14" />

Benjamin and [CODEPINK](/wiki/code-pink) appear elsewhere in Kiriakou's account as allies in his own case and in the campaign for [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange); here the organisation appears in its other capacity, as a group whose members are physically stopped from reaching Gaza.
