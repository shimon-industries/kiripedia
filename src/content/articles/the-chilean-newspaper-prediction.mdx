[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has recorded one prediction he made publicly and got wrong, and returns to it as a caution rather than a defence.

## The question

In the week [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) was elected president in 2016, he says, he gave an interview to a Chilean newspaper. The paper put the same question to three people: to him, to [Thomas Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake) of the National Security Agency, and to [Edward Snowden](/wiki/edward-snowden). All were asked whether they thought things would be better or worse under Trump for national security whistleblowers.<Cite s="2020-11-24-revolutionary-change-revolutionary-change-IozNdH" t="10:27" />

## His answer

*"I foolishly said that I was cautiously optimistic,"* he recalls, *"because they couldn't possibly get any worse than they were under [Barack Obama](/wiki/barack-obama)."* His own verdict on the reasoning, delivered four years later: *"Never say that. It was a stupid mistake, because of course things got worse almost instantaneously."*<Cite s="2020-11-24-revolutionary-change-revolutionary-change-IozNdH" t="10:27" />

## What he counted afterwards

He supports the correction with cases. Reality Winner, the National Security Agency whistleblower, was serving five and a half years. Terry Albury, the FBI whistleblower, was due out within a week of the conversation. And, he says, the country knew from Jeff Sessions — *"the former attorney general and his big mouth"* — that a large number of national security leak investigations were then open.<Cite s="2020-11-24-revolutionary-change-revolutionary-change-IozNdH" t="10:59" />

His summary was blunt: *"it's far worse than Barack Obama,"* and *"the sentences are longer than they were under Barack Obama."*<Cite s="2020-11-24-revolutionary-change-revolutionary-change-IozNdH" t="10:59" />

## Why he keeps the error on the record

Kiriakou's public position is that the prosecution of whistleblowers is a bipartisan practice, and he has argued the point with statistics on both sides — noting in the same conversation that George W. Bush killed a handful of people by drone in his final month in office while Obama killed several hundred in his. The Chilean prediction is the counter-example he supplies against himself: an occasion when the bipartisan analysis he now applies would have saved him from an optimistic forecast, and did not.
