The **halfway house** is the residential re-entry stage of a federal sentence. Federal prisoners are entitled by law to as much as twelve months of it; in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s description of how it works in practice, what that really means is about two weeks in the facility before the remainder converts to house arrest — six months of halfway house plus three of home confinement being a realistic expectation for a well-behaved prisoner.<Cite s="2016-02-23-awakeradio1-awakeradio1" t="15:41" />

## Denied

Kiriakou received none of it. He is explicit about why: he was writing blogs from prison about problems in prison — the [Letters from Loretto](/wiki/letter-from-loretto) — and so he served every single day of his sentence with no relief at all.<Cite s="2016-02-23-awakeradio1-awakeradio1" t="15:09" /><Cite s="2017-09-05-stack-the-legal-odds-in-yo-the-legal-power-hour-with-guest-john-kiriakou" t="28:30" /> He nonetheless reported to one on release, and treats the entitlement as a right rather than a reward: a prisoner gets halfway house time regardless of what he does, because it is his right to it.<Cite s="2016-02-23-awakeradio1-awakeradio1" t="15:09" />

## The job board

The central condition of halfway-house release is employment: forty hours a week, and not from home. Kiriakou describes the arithmetic that follows. The house he reported to held a hundred and ten men and had one job listed on its board — dishwasher at a Fuddruckers — and failure to find work risks return to prison.<Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="1:17:04" /> His own position was worse than most, since his résumé was, as an interviewer put it, unorthodox: a trained CIA officer, a member of the [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) capture team, and two years in federal prison. He advertised for work on Twitter — *"must be 40 hours and not from home; I don't want the halfway house to send me back to prison just for unemployment."*<Cite s="2015-04-02-vice-news-the-government-turned-me-into-a-dissident" t="03:10" /> The fears were not borne out: the halfway house relented and allowed him to work for a medical technology company from home, and he shortly afterwards took a post as an associate fellow at the Institute for Policy Studies covering torture, intelligence policy, the Middle East and prison reform.<Cite s="2015-04-02-vice-news-the-government-turned-me-into-a-dissident" t="15:56" />

## Designed to fail

Coming home, Kiriakou says, was rougher than he expected; you cannot simply step back into your life. Probation restrictions, halfway-house restrictions and travel restrictions apply at once; a financial report is due every month; permission must be requested every time you leave the house and a telephone call made the minute you return. His conclusion is structural rather than personal: *"it's no wonder so many people get violated and go back to prison — the system is set up to make you fail, it's not set up to help you succeed."*<Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="1:16:33" /><Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="1:16:02" />

## Alongside home confinement

The halfway-house obligation does not end when a prisoner is released to [home confinement](/wiki/home-confinement). Kiriakou's three months of house arrest ran concurrently with a weekly check-in at the halfway house and drug testing twice a week — the latter, he notes, imposed on a man who has never taken a drug in his life.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="1:02:51" /><Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="1:03:23" />
