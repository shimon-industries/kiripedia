[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes [Gust Avrakotos](/wiki/gust-avrakotos) — the officer made famous by *Charlie Wilson's War*, and portrayed in the film by Philip Seymour Hoffman — as a father figure and one of the most controversial men in the [CIA](/wiki/cia): a man who "killed a lot of Russians, a lot of medals, promotions, couldn't get along with anybody. Everybody hated Gust and Gust hated everybody. But he was a sweetheart deep down. You just had to know how to deal with him."<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="01:01" />

The story he offers as evidence of what "knowing how to deal with him" meant is an argument about a dead policeman.

## The remark

The two were working the [17 November](/wiki/revolutionary-organization-17-november) task force together. Kiriakou mentioned offhandedly that the group's second victim — the former chief of the Hellenic National Police, Mallios — had been a torturer during the junta.<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="01:31" />

## The reaction

Avrakotos grabbed him by the lapels and slammed him against the wall. "Mallios was my friend."<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="01:31" />

Kiriakou's answer, as he gives it: "you better get your hands off of me, old man, or I'm going to make you sorry."<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="02:02" />

When Avrakotos let go, Kiriakou did not retract the substance. "What's wrong with you? Mallios was a torturer. I'm sorry if he was your friend, I'm sorry he got shot in the head, but he was a torturer. And keep your hands to yourself next time."<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="02:02" />

## The aftermath

The outcome was not an apology but an assessment. Afterwards, Kiriakou says, Avrakotos told him: "I really respect you for the way you reacted."<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="02:02" />

"So anyway, we were friends."<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="02:33" />

The friendship mattered operationally. It is the reason Kiriakou telephoned Avrakotos when he could see no way to reach the incoming intelligence representative of a hostile state, and it is the reason he was given the manoeuvre that became [the broken side-view mirror](/wiki/the-broken-side-view-mirror) — a trick Avrakotos said he had used himself in the 1950s.<Cite s="2023-11-16-dalton-fischer-podcast-dalton-fischer-podcast" t="02:33" />

Kiriakou does not resolve the disagreement about Mallios in the retelling. He states his own view of the man as a fact and reports Avrakotos's as an affection, and lets the two stand alongside each other.
