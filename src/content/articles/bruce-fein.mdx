**Bruce Fein** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s longtime attorney and, by Kiriakou's description, *"not just one of my closest friends, but my attorney as well."*<Cite s="2024-12-10-jon-leon-guerrero-john-kiriakou-enhanced-techniques-whistleblowing" t="00:32" /> A Harvard Law-trained constitutional attorney and Berkeley graduate, he served as Deputy Attorney General under President Reagan and as General Counsel of the Federal Communications Commission.<Cite s="2026-02-17-the-deep-focus-will-the-u-s-attack-iran-going" t="01:37" /><Cite s="2024-12-10-jon-leon-guerrero-john-kiriakou-enhanced-techniques-whistleblowing" t="00:32" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="1:56:36" /> Kiriakou introduces him variously as *"a renowned constitutional scholar"* and, more briskly, as a man who *"has done everything — he's an awesome guy."*<Cite s="2026-02-17-the-deep-focus-will-the-u-s-attack-iran-going" t="01:37" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="1:56:36" /> Kiriakou has referred to him as *"one of my attorneys"*, indicating Fein was one of several counsel rather than the sole one.<Cite s="2018-06-04-suzi-3d-unity4j-vigil" t="34:25" />

## The hundred books

Fein maintains a standing list of one hundred books he believes every American should read. Kiriakou raises it as the answer to the question of what an intelligence professional ought to be reading — *"I'm like, all of them. Read all of the books"* — and stresses that the list is not a conversational flourish: *"He's really serious about this. Every American needs to read these 100 books. And I think he's absolutely right."*<Cite s="2024-12-10-jon-leon-guerrero-john-kiriakou-enhanced-techniques-whistleblowing" t="00:32" /><Cite s="2024-12-10-pete-a-turner" t="00:32" />

## The CIA-failures book

Fein and Kiriakou have spent roughly a decade circling a joint book on the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s record of institutional failure. The earliest version on the record is framed around [groupthink](/wiki/cia-groupthink): in 2018 Kiriakou said the two of them *"have been talking about writing a book exactly about that,"* on the grounds that across the major international developments of the agency's first seventy years *"the CIA has been wrong about just about every one of them."*<Cite s="2018-06-04-suzi-3d-unity4j-vigil" t="34:25" /><Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="39:53" />

By February 2026 the project had a history and a restart date. The two had produced an outline about ten years earlier, then *"both got busy and we didn't write the book."* Kiriakou began writing again a couple of weeks before the interview and had reached the first 5,000 words of a manuscript he estimates needs to run between 80,000 and 100,000. Fein, whom he calls *"a prolific writer,"* has taken a divided share: *"we've divided his part of the book from my part of the book, and it's going to be hard work, but we're going to do that book."*<Cite s="2026-02-17-the-deep-focus-will-the-u-s-attack-iran-going" t="02:08" /><Cite s="2026-02-17-the-deep-focus-will-the-u-s-attack-iran-going" t="02:39" />

The thesis Kiriakou gives for it is a claim about consent: *"I think the American people deserve to know what the CIA has done in their name, and how the CIA has failed time after time after time."*<Cite s="2026-02-17-the-deep-focus-will-the-u-s-attack-iran-going" t="02:39" />

## The Kushner approach

Fein was the point of contact for the first serious opening in Kiriakou's [pardon](/wiki/kiriakou-pardon-request) campaign. After [Tucker Carlson](/wiki/tucker-carlson) put Kiriakou on air repeatedly with instructions to speak directly into the camera to the president, [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) saw one of the segments and sent the clip to [Jared Kushner](/wiki/jared-kushner). The next day Kushner's attorney, Abbe Lowell, telephoned Fein and said: *"My client wants to see your client."*<Cite s="2026-05-20-the-devory-darkins-intervi" t="46:45" />

Kiriakou's reaction was undisguised — *"I was so excited, I wanted to jump up and down"* — and the two of them went to Lowell's office together and met with Lowell and Kushner. Kushner closed the meeting by asking for a one-page paper: three-quarters of the page telling Kiriakou's story, the last quarter explaining how a pardon would help Trump get re-elected. They wrote it, submitted it, and *"then we never heard back again."*<Cite s="2026-05-20-the-devory-darkins-intervi" t="46:45" /><Cite s="2026-05-20-the-devory-darkins-intervi" t="47:15" />

## 2018 Giuliani meeting

Fein accompanied Kiriakou to the 2018 meeting at the Trump International Hotel at which [Rudy Giuliani](/wiki/rudy-giuliani) — via an unnamed aide — solicited $2 million from Kiriakou in exchange for arranging a presidential pardon. On the way out of the meeting Fein's only comment, per Kiriakou: *"Criminal. Criminal."*<Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="1:56:36" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="1:58:42" />

Days later, when *New York Times* reporter Mike Schmidt contacted Kiriakou about the incident, Kiriakou was sitting in Fein's office. Asked whether to go on the record, Fein: *"Hell yeah, go on the record. Giuliani needs to take responsibility for what he did."* The *Times* ran the story on its Sunday front page.<Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="2:02:54" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="2:03:58" />

## 2022 FBI introduction

In 2022, when Kiriakou had assembled 15,000 pages of evidence of bank fraud and money laundering at a firm that had hired him as COO, Fein connected him to a mutual contact — a former deputy director of the FBI — who arranged an appointment for Kiriakou at the FBI's Washington field office. The FBI declined interest in the case.<Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="2:11:22" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="2:11:53" />
