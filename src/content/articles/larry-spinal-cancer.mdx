**Larry** is the prisoner [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) offers as the end point of the [medical system](/wiki/prison-medical-care) at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto). He did not know him well — Larry lived near the chapel where Kiriakou worked as an orderly, so theirs was a *"hey Larry, how you doing"* relationship.<Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="21:22" />

Passing him on the way to work one day, Kiriakou saw him visibly hunched over and asked whether he was all right. Larry's back had been killing him for over a year; he had been to sick call that morning, where it was shrugged off and he was given the usual Tylenol.<Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="21:22" /><Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="21:52" />

A few weeks later he was walking with a cane. Kiriakou told him he needed heavyweight help and to have his family call their congressman — but, he notes, actually finding that kind of help is beyond most people, and it was beyond Larry.<Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="21:52" /><Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="22:24" />

## The chaplain

At a chapel staff meeting the chaplain mentioned that he intended to look in on Larry, who by then had gone from a cane to a walker to a wheelchair. Kiriakou told him about the year of unanswered complaints and asked him to intervene. To his great credit, Kiriakou says, the chaplain did: he went to the medical unit and demanded Larry be taken to an outside hospital for testing.<Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="22:24" /><Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="22:54" />

Another two weeks passed before the regional medical office agreed to pay for it. Larry was taken to a hospital, given an MRI, and diagnosed with stage four cancer of the spine.<Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="22:54" />

He was sent back to Loretto and died in his bunk two weeks later, having received no treatment of any kind except Tylenol, and having never been considered for compassionate release. *"In a lot of ways Larry was ignored to death."*<Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="23:26" />

Kiriakou's conclusion is put as a floor rather than an argument: there are genuinely bad people at Loretto, but none of them had been sentenced to death — *"not literally anyway"* — and they deserve at least basic medical care, which the Bureau of Prisons is legally obliged to provide.<Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="23:26" /><Cite s="2026-08-03-dead-drop-s2e12-dangerous" t="23:56" />
