**Economic roots of terrorism** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s argument, drawn from interrogating young al-Qaeda fighters in Pakistan, that jihadi recruitment is driven by poverty rather than religion. The fighters told essentially the same story: illiterate, jobless village men who could not marry because no family would accept them, told by the local imam to go make jihad in Afghanistan for $300 a month, with a $500 martyrdom bonus for their parents.<Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="33:41" /><Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="34:14" /> They *"couldn't find the United States on a map"* and had never read the Quran, doing it purely out of desperation.<Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="34:46" /> Kiriakou concludes that economic development and education are *"the two greatest weapons against terrorism"*, undercut by the U.S. aversion to foreign aid.<Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="35:18" />

Kiriakou traces the same pattern directly to U.S. bombing campaigns: he says he interrogated many al-Qaeda fighters who told him they had no problem with the United States until it bombed their village and killed their parents, cousins, and friends, only then did they take up arms.<Cite s="2023-12-07-truthovercomfort" t="33:00" /> He has repeated the underlying prescription across interviews in near-identical terms: the fix to Middle East terrorism is not *"sexy"* or quick, but education and public-works projects that give people a job skill and functioning infrastructure, removing the incentive to turn to extremism.<Cite s="2017-05-31-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="27:26" /> He separately says the recruits he personally captured knew nothing about the United States beyond that it was bombing their countries, were not religiously motivated, and had never read the Quran, concluding that terrorism is fought through education, public works, and genuine foreign aid, explicitly distinguishing this from USAID-branded programs and citing Danish-built sewer systems in Sana'a and Italian-built electrical grids in Aden as the kind of aid that actually works.<Cite s="2020-12-23-codepink" t="50:30" />

Kiriakou separately argues that political leaders' stated desire for a stable Middle East is not humanitarian but commercial: *"people really do want a stable Middle East because it's better for business"*, a stable region generates far more trade revenue than the alternative of selling arms into a war zone.<Cite s="2017-05-31-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="27:58" />

## The marriage problem

Retelling the foot-soldier profile in 2025, Kiriakou puts the motive in domestic rather than economic terms first. Every fighter at the grunt level *"told exactly the same story"*: an isolated village, illiterate, no job skills, no training, *"and they wanted to get married. But what man would want his wife to marry a kid who can't read, can't write, and can't work? Nobody wants their daughter to marry a guy like that."*<Cite s="2025-06-01-stephen-gardner-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-presence" t="12:27" /><Cite s="2025-06-01-stephen-gardner-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-presence" t="12:57" />

The recruiter's pitch, as he renders it, is addressed to that gap before it is addressed to any grievance: *"the local imam went to these kids and said, why are you here in this village? You should make something of yourself. You should go to Afghanistan and make jihad against the Americans. And if you do, we'll give you $300 a month. And if you're martyred, we'll give your parents a $500 [martyrdom bonus](/wiki/the-martyrdom-bonus)."* The answer he reports is *"okay, I got nothing else going on."*<Cite s="2025-06-01-stephen-gardner-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-presence" t="12:57" /><Cite s="2025-06-01-stephen-gardner-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-presence" t="13:27" />

What follows is his standing policy conclusion, here applied to [Syria](/wiki/syria-sanctions-relief-2025): *"they couldn't find the United States on a map. They had no beef with the United States, it's that they were poor. So I've always said, if we were serious, not just the United States but the West, about addressing the root causes of terrorism, we would be involved in widescale economic development and education."*<Cite s="2025-06-01-stephen-gardner-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-presence" t="13:27" /><Cite s="2025-06-01-stephen-gardner-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-presence" t="13:59" />
