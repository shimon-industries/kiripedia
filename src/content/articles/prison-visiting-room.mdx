The **visiting room** is where a federal prisoner sees the outside world, and in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto) it is both the emotional centre of a sentence and a piece of machinery. Getting to it means going through a door that leads into a small strip-search room and activating a flashing light.<Cite s="2026-07-27-dead-drop-s2e11-unfinished-business" t="30:58" />

## The children

Kiriakou describes telling his children where he was going and then watching them adjust in the room itself: they were sad, but they accepted it, and after three or four visits the visits became ordinary.<Cite s="2018-06-20-rdap-dan-facing-40-years-espionage" t="10:30" />

## The visitor he did not expect

About a month into the sentence, one of his former CIA bosses appeared in the visiting room while his wife and children were there.<Cite s="2024-11-16-the-team-house" t="1:43:46" />

## When the room is closed

The visiting room is also where Kiriakou marks the difference between ordinary imprisonment and the special administrative measures imposed on national-security prisoners. A SAMs prisoner cannot use the visiting room while any other prisoner is present, which means every visit has to be separately coordinated — one of a set of restrictions he cites when describing what awaited [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange) in the American system.<Cite s="2021-01-05-talks-with-kiriakou-about-the-assange-extradi" t="13:59" />
