**The Maxwell Justice Department interview** was the wide-ranging July 2025 questioning of [Ghislaine Maxwell](/wiki/ghislaine-maxwell) by the Department of Justice, which according to her counsel she had requested and in which she invoked the fifth amendment on nothing and answered every question.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="43:42" />

## Why the risk matters

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s reading turns on exposure rather than content. A prisoner who answers everything can be prosecuted for perjury if any of it is false, and a perjury conviction could extend her sentence: *"it was very risky to answer these questions."* From that he draws his inference, *"to me, that indicates someone who's probably telling the truth"*, and compares it to the position of a plaintiff who sues for defamation and thereby submits to discovery.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="44:13" />

On motive he is explicit that he is guessing: *"we, this has to be speculation because we really don't know."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="44:43" /> What he thinks is being sought is *"some sort of a commutation, even a pardon"*, which he did not consider a stretch given the reporting, and which he thought [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump), *"being Trump"*, would not rule out.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="45:46" />

The obvious objection was put to him, that a pardon would free her to talk, when silence is what Trump would want. Kiriakou could not resolve it either, allowing only the possibility that a pardon might be given *"for being quiet"*.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="45:46" /><Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="46:17" />

## The names are not the question

Kiriakou uses the interview to restate an objection he had made the previous week to [Pam Bondi](/wiki/pam-bondi)'s statement that the videos in the government's possession were child pornography that would never be released. His answer was that nobody had asked for them: *"We don't want to see the child porn. We want to know the names of the men committing crimes against children. That's what we want to know."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="44:43" />

That distinction governs how he treats the names that surfaced afterwards. Bill Clinton, John Kerry and *"a handful of other people who are well known to every single American"* were reported among them, and Kiriakou's response was to hold the question open rather than close it: *"the question remains, who were the guys committing crimes against children?"*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="46:17" />

## What he thinks it was

Asked what the underlying arrangement actually amounted to, Kiriakou declined the intelligence framing. It was, he thought, *"not about necessarily a honeypot operation"* and *"not about Mossad necessarily or probably"*, but *"probably just like gross dudes drinking and doing drugs and having a good time"*, and doing things that were illegal and immoral, some of them, not all. His image for it is a party with something going on in another room: *"maybe you don't go into that other room and maybe you do go into that other room."*<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="48:21" />

On Maxwell herself he is unequivocal. Asked whether she had been railroaded, he answered *"oh, I don't think so. I think she was guilty"*, adding that others should have been prosecuted alongside her and [Epstein](/wiki/jeffrey-epstein) but that *"she got what she deserved."* She procured, and that is settled.<Cite s="2025-07-25-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="47:50" />
