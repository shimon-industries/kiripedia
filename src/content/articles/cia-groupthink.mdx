[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) identifies institutional conformity as the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s central analytical failure, and states the consequence in the broadest available terms: *"if you look at the major international developments over the last 70 years since the creation of the CIA, the CIA has been wrong about just about every one of them — and it's because of that groupthink."* He and one of his attorneys, Bruce Fein, discussed writing a book on the subject.<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="39:53" />

## The vocabulary and the reality

Kiriakou's objection is that the agency has adopted the language of contrarian analysis without the practice. *"They throw around these slogans and business buzzwords about thinking outside the box, and establishing a red team or a blue team — and it's all nonsense. They don't know what they're doing. In fact they end up sitting around a conference room table and just agreeing with one another."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="40:24" />

## Self-importance as the companion trait

Kiriakou pairs the analytical failure with an institutional self-image. He recounts a former senior CIA official, retired about a year and a half, telling him that he had gone on antidepressant medication *"because on Friday he was one of the most important people in America, and on Monday he was a nobody, because he had retired. And he can't handle being a nobody."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="38:48" />

*"That's a theme at the CIA,"* he says. *"They all think that they're the most important people in America, because they have sort of self-appointed jurisdiction over life and death for so many people — and then they trick themselves into thinking that they're irreplaceable. And in fact they're very replaceable."*<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="39:21" />

The hiring pattern he describes elsewhere — an agency that selects for [sociopathic tendencies](/wiki/sociopathic-tendencies) and then finds full sociopaths climbing to the top of it — is, on his account, the same problem seen from the personnel side.<Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="41:26" /><Cite s="2018-06-08-suzi-3d-unity4j-full-interview" t="41:57" />
