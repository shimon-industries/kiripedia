**Cameo** is the app on which customers pay public figures to record short personalised videos, and which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) joined in February 2026. He describes the premise without flattering himself about where he sits in it: *"Cameo is an app where D-list celebrities are represented"* — you like someone from a reality show, you hire them to wish your friend a happy birthday.<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="28:31" /><Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="29:02" />

## Signing up

The company called him on a date he still remembers — 28 February 2026 — and asked whether he had heard of it. He had: his brother-in-law had bought his sister one a few years earlier, and it had been fun. Asked whether he wanted to join, his answer was *"Sure, why not?"*<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="29:02" /><Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="29:32" />

Setup took under five minutes — a short biography and a photograph. He was told to price himself at $50 and let the algorithm adjust it every fifteen minutes according to demand.<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="29:32" />

## The volume

*"I did 900 cameos just in the month of March. I set this new record."* By late July he had done roughly 2,600, *"and they just keep going."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="29:32" /><Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="30:04" />

About half are birthday wishes. Most of the remainder are requests for a story from the CIA. Fewer people ask for a prison story, *"but I think frankly those are some of the better stories."* Around ten per cent are pep talks or requests for advice; roasts are also available.<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="30:04" /><Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="30:35" />

He is candid that the price is steep — *"I wouldn't spend $180 for John to say happy birthday, but a lot of people do"* — and notes that booking one opens a channel: the customer may then text him, and he can text back, free.<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="30:35" />

## The three

Of the 2,600, about two hundred have been requests for advice or a pep talk. Three of those people texted him afterwards to say he had talked them out of killing themselves.<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="30:35" /><Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="31:06" />

One he describes in detail: a fifteen-year-old girl with bulimia, very low self-esteem and a difficult relationship with her parents, whose request message *"just sounded to me to be so desperate."* He broke the platform's rules on the recording itself: *"Listen, we're not supposed to do this, but here's my phone number. I want you to call me right now."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="31:06" />

She called. They spoke for some time, and he talked her out of it. She has called twice since, each time for under a minute — *"she just needed to hear a friendly voice, you know, in the midst of a bad day."* His own verdict on it is understated: *"I'm glad that I could be helpful."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="31:36" />

## The criticism

The visibility has cost him something. Kiriakou left a daily podcast he had been doing with a close friend, simply because there was too much else on — *"I can't do a podcast every single day."* The reaction surprised him: *"I'm surprised at the viciousness of some of the comments… that I sold out, I abandoned my friends. It's like, what are you talking about?"*<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="32:07" />

His defence is plainly financial and unembarrassed: *"I've been poor for 19 years. I'm 62 years old in a couple of weeks. My parents both died in their 60s. I don't know how much time I'm going to have. I have five kids. Two of them are in college right now. I need to make a couple of dollars."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-to-my-sons-truth-betrayal-the-cost-of-speaking-out" t="32:42" />

## The request he always refuses

Asked for the most confidential thing he learned at the [CIA](/wiki/cia), Kiriakou answered that the question is not a novel one but his most routine piece of correspondence: *"people ask me on Cameo all the time to tell them the deepest, darkest secret. I can't."*<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="02:33" />

Pressed repeatedly over the course of the same interview — including with the suggestion that he could disclose one and then talk his way out of it — his position did not move: *"I could, but I won't."* The reason he gives is the standing one: the material is *"sources and methods"*, and he distinguishes that category from the crimes he considers himself obliged to expose.<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="02:33" /><Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="22:45" />
