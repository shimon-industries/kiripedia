The **Bab el-Mandeb** is the strait where the Arabian Peninsula and the Horn of Africa come almost to a close, joining the Red Sea to the Arabian Sea. All shipping coming down through the Suez Canal must pass through it — which is why, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account, the Somali piracy of previous decades was concentrated there.<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="09:48" />

He gives its width inconsistently across tellings — nine miles or sixteen in one, about four in another where he contrasts it with the Strait of Hormuz, and sixteen miles between [Somaliland](/wiki/somali-land) and Yemen in a third. His comparative point is stable even where the figure is not: the Bab el-Mandeb is much narrower than Hormuz, and therefore easier to close.<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="09:48" /><Cite s="2026-01-05-lee-camp-unredacted-toni" t="20:22" /><Cite s="2026-03-30-deprogram-show-with-going-to-ground-deprogram-with-ted-rall" t="02:41" /><Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="2:18:58" />

## The only interest

Kiriakou uses the strait to define the limits of legitimate American involvement in [Yemen](/wiki/yemen). Reflecting on a question he first asked in 1990, he says that even all these years later, *"except for the security of the Bab el-Mandeb, we don't have national interests in Yemen."* What the United States should be doing there instead, he argues, is digging water wells, helping build roads and hospitals and upgrading the electrical grid — *"but that's never going to happen."*<Cite s="2025-09-12-dialogue-works-john-kiriakou-israel-humiliates-itself-middle" t="52:43" />

## The Houthis and the chokepoint

The strait is what makes the Houthi movement a strategic actor rather than a local one. Kiriakou describes the Houthis — Shia, a minority in Yemen, close to and probably armed by Iran — as controlling not the country but the part of it that includes the capital, and as having expressed solidarity with the Palestinians of Gaza by harassing shipping through the Bab el-Mandeb and firing on vessels including American military ones, which he calls a really stupid thing to do.<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="13:05" />

## Somaliland

The Israeli response, in his account, surprised everyone: opening diplomatic relations with the breakaway republic of [Somaliland](/wiki/somali-land), the only country in the world to recognise it. The exchange he describes is recognition for a military base — a base to be used against the Houthis, and thereby to keep the Bab el-Mandeb open. *"That's the whole key. If you can beat up on the Houthis, then the Bab el-Mandeb remains open."* Asked whether the base had been built, he said not yet.<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="14:50" /> He has visited Somaliland himself, in 2009, and describes it as bordering Djibouti and Ethiopia, sitting just south of the Bab el-Mandeb and about twenty miles across the water from Yemen — *"right in the heart of pirate central."*<Cite s="2026-01-06-deep-focus-maduro-capture-cia-deadliest-day" t="25:30" />
