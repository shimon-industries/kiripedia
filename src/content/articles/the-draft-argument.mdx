**The draft argument** is the exchange [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) recalls when asked whether the reckoning over Vietnam had shaken his view of his own country. It had not — *"not at first"* — and the reason is contained in a dispute he had with a friend in the College Democrats and had not thought about in years.<Cite s="2015-04-16-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="13:36" />

His stated position on the war was unambiguous: *"I opposed the war. I thought it was immoral. I thought it was illegal."* His position on serving in it was the opposite, and he offers no reconciliation of the two.<Cite s="2015-04-16-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="13:36" />

> *"But if I had been drafted, I would have gone. And he said if he had been drafted, he would have gone to Canada. And we had a real argument about that. And he said to me, how can you oppose a war and then go fight in it? And I said, because my country asked me to. I wouldn't give that same answer today, but as an 18-year-old that's what I believed."*

He sets the contradiction against another from the same period. In September 1984, aged nineteen, he introduced [George McGovern](/wiki/george-mcgovern) at the announcement of his presidential candidacy, and *"in my speech of introduction, I said that he was one of the few senators to stand up to the illegal and immoral war in Vietnam."* Put to him that this sits badly with his support for [the invasion of Grenada](/wiki/grenada-invasion), his answer is simply that he was nineteen and *"still figuring it out."*<Cite s="2015-04-16-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="14:11" /><Cite s="2015-04-16-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="14:42" />

The same period produced the sharpest illustration of the split. He and his friends would walk up to the White House gate at night, *"when you could walk right up to the gate,"* and shout *"Fuck you, Reagan!"* at whatever they had seen on the news — *"and then I'd go back to the College Democrats and say, you know, guys, I've got to go with Reagan on this one."*<Cite s="2015-04-16-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="14:42" />

What held it all together was a proposition he states plainly and dates precisely. *"Sure, we made mistakes, certainly, but our hearts were in the right place. I really believed that we were that shining city on a hill. Back then and for many years later — really until September 11 — I believed that."*<Cite s="2015-04-16-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="15:17" />
