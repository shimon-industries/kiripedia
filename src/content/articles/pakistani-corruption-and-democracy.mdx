**Pakistani corruption and democracy** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s standing assessment of why Pakistani democratic government does not work, delivered on 19 November 2025 immediately after [he declined an Indian television interview on the same subject](/wiki/the-cnn-india-refusal) and warned that he would be threatened for what he was about to say.

## The claim

*"Pakistani corruption is so deep and so broad that it's almost impossible for the country to have a true functioning democracy."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="37:37" /><Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="38:08" />

The host's interjection — *"corruption's always high, it's Pakistan"* — came from the viewer's own question rather than from Kiriakou, who states the point as a structural finding rather than a national characteristic.<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="37:06" />

## The second factor

Alongside corruption he sets the record of the armed forces. *"Pakistan also has a long history of military coups and other interventions into its democracy."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="38:08" />

The two together produce the conclusion reached in the exchange that followed — that what exists is not really a democracy but a military dictatorship with a democratic veneer, a formulation the two men arrived at jointly rather than one Kiriakou states alone.<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="38:08" />

## Imran Khan

On the imprisoned former prime minister, Kiriakou is unambiguous and repeats himself. *"[Imran Khan](/wiki/imran-khan) should not be in prison."* And again: *"But Imran Khan shouldn't be in prison. He should be allowed to defend himself and have his day in [court]."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="38:08" />

The position is notable because Kiriakou has been publicly attacked by Khan's party for a separate assessment about Pakistani military capability, and because he does not soften the corruption finding in order to make the defence of Khan easier.

## The pattern

What makes Khan's imprisonment ordinary rather than exceptional, in Kiriakou's telling, is that it is the norm. The exchange runs through the names — Nawaz Sharif, Benazir Bhutto — and arrives at a grim summary of the job: that anyone standing for it should expect to end in a cell, and that the alternative ending is assassination.<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="38:08" />

His own conclusion, offered as a question rather than a judgement, was *"I don't know who would want the job."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="38:08" />
