**The OPCW Douma investigation** is the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons inquiry into an alleged chemical attack in Douma, Syria, in April 2018, and one of the cases [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) uses to argue that institutional findings can be manufactured rather than merely mistaken.

*"This is a far bigger deal,"* he said of the documents released from OPCW whistleblowers: *"fifteen separate investigators objecting to the fact that their analysis was either taken out of context or it was falsified entirely."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="08:46" />

What was suppressed, on his account, was a negative finding — *"that there was no evidence to indicate that such an attack took place. The information was falsified, and it was the false information that was used to come to a political conclusion."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="09:18" />

## The route it had to take

Kiriakou treats the disclosure as both admirable and damning. That the investigators came forward *"I think is wonderful. It was very brave."* That they had to go to [WikiLeaks](/wiki/julian-assange) to be heard is the part he objects to — and, he adds, the reason anyone knows at all: *"we wouldn't know any of the story without WikiLeaks."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="09:50" /><Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="10:22" />

## The narrative that survived it

His closing observation is about what the revelations did not change. *"It's funny — that's what the investigators said from the very beginning, and nobody paid any attention to them."* Even after the releases, he noted, programmes including *Morning Joe* on MSNBC and Ali Velshi were still repeating that a chlorine gas attack had taken place at Douma.<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="11:58" />
