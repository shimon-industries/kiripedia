**The contrition requirement** is the procedural obstacle [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) identifies in his own repeated failure to obtain a pardon, and the one he says he will not remove.

## The rule

Kiriakou states it as a formal condition of the application rather than an informal expectation. *"this is one of the things when you apply for a pardon, you have to show proof that you showed contrition. And I'm not contrite."*<Cite s="2025-02-25-katie-halper-why-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-almost-to" t="00:00" />

He then supplies the question and his answer to it: *"Are you sorry for the crime you committed? No, I'm not. I would do it again."*<Cite s="2025-02-25-katie-halper-why-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-almost-to" t="00:00" />

## The cost he sets against it

The refusal is not offered as costless. Kiriakou opens the same passage with an inventory of losses — *"My wife and I split up. I lost my pension. I lost my security clearance. I lost my job"* — and closes it with the same conclusion: *"I would do the whole doggone thing over again … Because it was the right thing to do. And torture is outlawed in this country now. And I'm proud to have played a role in that."*<Cite s="2025-02-25-katie-halper-why-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-almost-to" t="00:00" />

The reasoning he attaches is about the passage of time rather than vindication in the moment: *"You come to some issues where you know that you're right and they're wrong. And eventually everybody else is going to see that."*<Cite s="2025-02-25-katie-halper-why-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-almost-to" t="00:00" />

## The one period of doubt

Asked whether he ever regretted doing the principled thing, Kiriakou dated the doubt precisely to the plea negotiations — *"when the Justice Department offered me 45 years in prison and told me that was the best deal I was going to get"* — and to the night he intended not to survive, an episode recorded at [whistleblowers and suicide](/wiki/whistleblowers-and-suicide).<Cite s="2025-02-25-katie-halper-why-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-almost-to" t="00:30" />

What he says settled the question was not comfort but arithmetic about who else would act: *"the more I thought about it, the more I thought, 'No, somebody had to do it. We're led by a cabal of criminals. And somebody had to say something.'"*<Cite s="2025-02-25-katie-halper-why-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-almost-to" t="01:03" />
