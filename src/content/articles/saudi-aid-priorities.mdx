Asked about the role of Pakistan in the Saudi story, John Kiriakou first said he could not confirm the specific allegation put to him — he did not know whether it was true, but he had heard it for the last twenty or twenty-two years, and it rang true to him. <Cite s="2024-05-25-leonardo-salvaggio-personal-channel-saudi-9-11-probe" t="29:21" /> Then he added something he thought many people do not understand.

## A deep affection with a shape

There is a very deep love between Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, he says — to the point where the city where [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) was captured, Faisalabad, was named after King Faisal of Saudi Arabia, because he had given so much money to Pakistan to develop it. <Cite s="2024-05-25-leonardo-salvaggio-personal-channel-saudi-9-11-probe" t="29:21" /> <Cite s="2024-05-25-leonardo-salvaggio-personal-channel-saudi-9-11-probe" t="29:54" />

So Pakistanis have this deep love of Saudi Arabia, and the Saudis are very generous. But, Kiriakou says, they are generous many times in the wrong areas: instead of building schools they build mosques; instead of building hospitals they send planeloads of Qurans; and instead of building water treatment plants or power plants they send money for weapons and ammunition and training in various camps. <Cite s="2024-05-25-leonardo-salvaggio-personal-channel-saudi-9-11-probe" t="29:54" />

## The conclusion he draws

This, he says, is really a problem for the United States, which has allowed itself to believe over many years that the Saudis are its friends. They are not our friends, he says; they're our enemies — and it was always his view that they should be treated as enemies. <Cite s="2024-05-25-leonardo-salvaggio-personal-channel-saudi-9-11-probe" t="29:54" />

The misdirection has a mirror in his account of American programmes in Yemen, where he argues the reverse case: that the way to defeat militancy is patient infrastructure of the kind described in [the Sanaa sewer project](/wiki/sanaa-sewer-project), not projects like [the Yemen jazz radio plan](/wiki/yemen-jazz-radio-plan). His view of why the Saudi state behaved as it did before the attacks is set out in [the Saudi fear of an election](/wiki/saudi-democracy-fear).

## Money as a veto on change

The same spending pattern appears in Kiriakou's 2015 account as an obstacle to political change rather than merely as misdirected charity. Anybody participating in a popular uprising anywhere in the region, he said, is going to have to fight against Saudi money and Saudi weapons — a claim he illustrated with [Egypt](/wiki/saudi-influence-in-egypt), where he described Riyadh as bankrolling the government and ensuring its survival.<Cite s="2015-09-23-mintpress-news-former-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-takes-us-insid" t="14:10" /><Cite s="2015-09-23-mintpress-news-former-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-takes-us-insid" t="14:40" />
