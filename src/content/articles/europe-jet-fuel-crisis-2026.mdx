**Europe's 2026 jet fuel crisis** was the reported prospect, in April 2026, that the continent would exhaust its supply of jet fuel within six weeks. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) opened his programme with it on the day it broke, taping on 16 April: *"Breaking news this morning that Europe, listen to this, how crazy this is. Europe will run out of jet fuel in 6 weeks."*<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="00:39" /> His assessment of the consequence was immediate, *"That would lead to an economic calamity that will ripple across the entire planet"*, and the questions he put to his guest were how such a thing could be negotiated, and how anyone gets out of it.<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="01:10" /> He credited the report to the Associated Press, calling it *"this craziness from the Associated Press,"* and asked whether there was any precedent for it.<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="02:11" />

## Why the fuel stopped

The economist [Richard D. Wolff](/wiki/richard-wolff), answering on the programme, said he knew of no real precedent and traced the shortage to the [Strait of Hormuz](/wiki/strait-of-hormuz) rather than to any exhaustion of crude. Roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes through the strait, he said, along with a large share of the derivatives of oil, jet fuel among them, together with plastic and fertiliser.<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="07:21" /> Kiriakou interjected with the figure he returns to elsewhere: *"Fertilizer. 65% of the world's fertilizer."*<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="07:21" />

When Iran closed the strait to all but a trickle of shipping, the consequence Wolff identified was one step further down the chain than the headlines suggested: *"the oil that needs to be refined to produce jet fuel wasn't arriving at the factories that convert regular petroleum to jet fuel."*<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="10:29" /> The crude was not gone; it was not arriving where it had to be processed.

## Why Europe had no cushion

Two failures, in Wolff's account, left the continent without a buffer. The first was commercial: European airlines had not laid in stock, which he put down partly to *"cheapness on the part of the European airlines"*, jet fuel is expensive, and they did not want to pay to hold it for long periods.<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="11:01" /> The second was political. *"The Europeans had not been told by the United States about the attack. They had not been warned. They had not had time to prepare."* They had assumed, he said, that the United States would coordinate with them, and they were wrong.<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="10:29" /><Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="11:01" /> The consequences of that failure to warn are treated separately under [Europe and the 2026 Iran war](/wiki/europe-and-the-2026-iran-war).

The fuel shortage arrived alongside a price shock. Oil, Wolff said, had moved from around sixty to seventy dollars a barrel to *"well in excess of $100 a barrel"* and was still rising as they spoke, a rise he expected to produce a recession and, if it ran a few months longer, a global depression.<Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="15:47" /><Cite s="2026-04-17-the-deep-focus-show-europe-is-running-out-of-fuel-w-richard-wolff" t="16:20" />
