**The regime change approval process** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of the route a proposal to overthrow a foreign government takes through the United States government. He gave it in detail in August 2020, prefacing it by declining to name one link in the chain — *"I won't say the name of the office because they'll probably fall on my head like a ton of bricks"* — and this article follows him in leaving it unnamed.<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="28:10" />

## The route

It begins informally. An officer with an idea *"simply sits at your desk and you write a memo and you say, I would like to overthrow this government and these are my reasons for wanting to overthrow the government."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="28:10" /> That memo goes up the officer's own chain of command, which signs off — *"and they always do"* — after which it is carried to the unnamed office whose job is to walk it through the rest of government.<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="28:41" />

From there the sequence is legal rather than operational. The memo goes first to the Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel, *"the body in government that determines whether such an overthrow would be legal"* — and, in his account, *"of course, they always decide that such an overthrow would be legal."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="28:41" /><Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="29:16" /> It then goes to the [National Security Council](/wiki/national-security-council)'s team of attorneys, who repeat the exercise and reach the same conclusion, and then to the national security adviser, who decides whether it is *"a good enough idea that it should go to the President."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="29:16" /><Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="29:48" />

If the president judges that the overthrow fits his foreign policy, he orders it as an executive order, signed by himself and the national security adviser.<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="29:48" />

## Where the paper ends up

The signed order returns to the CIA and is *"physically put in a safe on the seventh floor in the CIA's operations center."* Kiriakou gives the reason for the safe as a collision of two rules: it is a government document *"so it can't be destroyed,"* but it is *"so highly classified that no one can see it besides the CIA's leaders and the CIA leader's attorneys."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="29:48" /><Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="30:23" /> The order to begin the overthrow itself goes to the Pentagon.<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="30:23" />

## The legal test he says officers are taught

Against that machinery he sets the rule he says is taught early: there are only three circumstances in which regime change is lawful — the foreign country has attacked the United States, its government has invited the United States in, or there is a mandate from the United Nations Security Council. *"You can't just decide one morning that you don't like another country's politics, and so you're going to overthrow that government. That's not the way that international law works."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="25:55" /><Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="26:27" />

He describes two generations inside the agency holding different views of this. Officers who had been there since the 1970s and 1980s were *"gung ho for regime change,"* treating it as part of the agency's reason for existing; his own generation, he says, believed *"we're supposed to be the good guys … and regime change is just simply illegal."* The choice he draws from that is stark: either renounce it, *"or we have to be willing to watch our own leadership be prosecuted for war crimes and crimes against humanity."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="25:21" /><Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="25:55" />

## Why he objects to generals running intelligence agencies

The same passage contains his reason for opposing the appointment of [military officers to lead intelligence agencies](/wiki/generals-in-national-security-posts). The question he asks of any such appointee is *"how did they get those four shiny stars on their shoulder?"* — and his answer is that they got them *"by saying, yes, sir, throughout a 30 or 35-year career."* What the post requires is the opposite: someone willing to say *"Mr. President, let me tell you why that's a terrible idea."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="26:58" /><Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="27:34" />

## The banality of it

Kiriakou's own gloss on the process is that its horror is procedural rather than dramatic. He reaches for a Banksy work — a cheap second-hand pastoral scene onto which the artist painted a park bench with Adolf Hitler sitting on it, admiring the view — and applies the title directly: *"to me, that's what this is. We all know this is wrong. We all know this is illegal. We all know that innocent civilians are going to die."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="30:58" /><Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="31:29" /> The cost falls on people with no say in it, he says, because *"this banal evil is what created the policy."*<Cite s="2020-08-24-clearing-the-fog-the-banal-evil-of-us-imperialism-a-view-from" t="31:29" />
