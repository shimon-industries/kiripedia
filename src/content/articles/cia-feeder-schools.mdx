**CIA feeder schools** are the universities that supply the agency with recruits. Asked to do an episode on Georgetown's applied intelligence programme and how the CIA recruits from it, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) accepted the premise but widened it: *"I'll go one better. It's way, way bigger than Georgetown's Applied Intelligence Program."*<Cite s="2026-07-08-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-cia-recruiting-and" t="04:07" /><Cite s="2026-07-08-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-cia-recruiting-and" t="04:37" />

## Not Georgetown first

He grants Georgetown its reputation — *"Yes, Georgetown is a feeder school for the CIA"* — and then displaces it: *"but so is George Washington University. And in fact, GW sends more people to the CIA than Georgetown does."*<Cite s="2026-07-08-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-cia-recruiting-and" t="04:37" />

## Mercyhurst

The institution he singles out is one he expects his audience not to know: *"there are schools like you've probably never heard of, like Mercyhurst College in Erie, Pennsylvania, that had the country's very first applied intelligence master's program."*<Cite s="2026-07-08-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-cia-recruiting-and" t="04:37" />

## Salamanca

Kiriakou also teaches into the pipeline himself. He runs a graduate programme in intelligence studies at the University of Salamanca in Spain, and notes the apparent paradox of its location: *"even though it's in Spain, they're learning intelligence. They're mostly Americans, and a lot of them end up at the CIA."*<Cite s="2026-07-08-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-cia-recruiting-and" t="05:08" />
