**Kiriakou's gastrectomy** was the operation in which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s stomach was surgically removed in September 2022, undertaken to reverse an uncontrolled [type 2 diabetes](/wiki/kiriakou-type-2-diabetes) that had defeated every available drug.

## The Lancet paper

The route to the operating table began with his doctor, who handed him a reading assignment rather than a prescription: an article from *The Lancet* written by a Scottish surgeon.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="06:53" /> The surgeon had been treating a woman with stomach cancer who also had uncontrolled diabetes. Her stomach was removed for the cancer, and within twenty-four hours her pancreas began producing insulin again. Other surgeons, comparing notes, reported the same result.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="07:26" />

## Three telephone calls

Kiriakou pursued it by telephone. He called the Scottish surgeon and made an appointment to speak with him, and was told: *"It works. We don't know why it works. We don't yet fully understand the human body, but it works. And there's almost no downside, no side effects."*<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="07:56" /> Asked whether anyone in the United States performed it, the surgeon named one in New York, who repeated the same formula — *"We don't understand why it works, but it works."*<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="07:56" />

Kiriakou asked whether the operation was a bypass, a sleeve or a duodenal switch, and was told those do not work: *"The stomach has to physically come out."* He was given the name of a single surgeon practising in Arlington, Virginia — where Kiriakou lives — Dr Jihad Salama, from Lebanon.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="08:27" />

## The money

Salama's first point was financial. Because the procedure is experimental, insurance would not cover it. The price was $17,000, including two nights in hospital. Kiriakou did not have it, so he went to a bank and took out a loan for the full amount.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="08:57" />

## The operation

The surgery was performed in September 2022 and was robotic: the surgeon worked the toggles while the machine made *"two little cuts"*, still visible, through which the stomach was taken out.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="09:28" /> Kiriakou had taken nothing but clear liquids for ten days beforehand, so that the organ was empty and had shrunk; it was cut, pulled out, and a pouch holding a quarter of a cup — *"just enough to fit in the palm of your hand"* — was constructed from his intestine.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="09:28" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="09:59" />

He took liquids for a further week, then moved on to pudding, purée and soups. Over the following nine months he lost 55 kilos and, he says, has kept all of it off.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="09:59" /> The result he places above the weight is the disease: *"I don't have diabetes anymore. My A1C is normal. Everything's normal."*<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="10:31" />

The operation left him unable to eat certain foods at all. Asked about Greek lamb — *"I'm Greek. I love arni, right? Who doesn't?"* — he said it is now too heavy, too dense and too fatty, and that he vomits as soon as he eats it.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="11:32" /> It also placed him in the minority of patients who suffer [serious complications](/wiki/kiriakou-post-gastrectomy-complications).<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="12:03" />
