**Kiriakou on Greek and Cypriot food** records the comparison [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) drew between the two cuisines while in [Cyprus](/wiki/kiriakou-cypriot-ancestry) in 2026, ten days into a stay on which he says he ate only Cypriot food.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="23:38" />

## The cheeses

His judgement, offered as something that would *"sound silly"*, is that the cheeses of Cyprus are *"so much more sophisticated"* and more subtle than those of Greece.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="24:09" /> The distinction he draws is structural rather than a matter of quality. In Greece there is feta at every meal, alongside kasseri, kefalotyri and kefalograviera, *"but most of them are variations on each other."* In Cyprus, for reasons he says he cannot explain, *"each cheese stands independently by itself"* — one nutty, one tasting of pine, one to be eaten with honey, and halloumi as *"the flagship of all the cheeses."* He put his own count that week at twenty different Cypriot cheeses.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="24:40" />

On halloumi he adds a note on availability in the United States: until about five years earlier it could be found only in a specialty cheese shop or at Whole Foods, and sometimes at a Greek grocery — the aside being that *"most of the Greek stores are not owned by Greeks. They're owned by Egyptians,"* who bring in Egyptian products alongside. It is now sold everywhere, and he buys it constantly. The Cypriot version is saltier than the American, and he prefers it.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="25:11" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="25:42" /> Asked to choose between feta and halloumi he took halloumi; between tzatziki and tirokafteri, tzatziki; between gyros and souvlaki, gyros. He had eaten sheftalia earlier the same week.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="26:14" />

## Coffee

Kiriakou takes freddo cappuccino over freddo espresso, on a volume argument rather than a flavour one: he drinks so much coffee that the added milk simply means more of it.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="26:14" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="26:47" /> He shares his interviewer's objection to the standard straw, which is too narrow to draw the drink up properly, and so does not use one.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="27:19" />

He was a frappé drinker — *"frappé metrio"*, and never at breakfast — until a visit to Rhodes with his son, where his cousin Christo asked him why he drank it and answered his own question: it is Nescafé, and therefore bad coffee. Kiriakou switched, and concedes the point: the freddo is higher quality because the beans are ground rather than poured from a tin. *"He was right."*<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="27:50" /><Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="28:21" />

He also claims a reading habit on the subject, saying he goes through the whole of any new coffee study that appears.<Cite s="2026-06-01-giorgos-omirou-clickbait-title-personal-channel-probe" t="28:51" />
