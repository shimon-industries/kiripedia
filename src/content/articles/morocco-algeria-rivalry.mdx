Morocco and Algeria *"hate each other. They've always hated each other,"* and are rivals for what Kiriakou calls primacy in North Africa.<Cite s="2026-08-04-john-kiriakou-who-is-really-behind-the-migrant-crisis-in-ce" t="08:47" /> His comparison of the two puts Algeria ahead on every axis but wealth: it is *"bigger, it's more important, it's more powerful. It's just as poor, but it's very powerful politically."*<Cite s="2026-08-04-john-kiriakou-who-is-really-behind-the-migrant-crisis-in-ce" t="08:47" />

That rivalry is his explanation for the [mass crossing into Ceuta](/wiki/ceuta-migrant-crossings-2026) in August 2026. Spanish prime minister Pedro Sánchez made an official visit to Algeria on 20 July; the Moroccan king was *"upset and angry and decided to sort of throw a wrench into the works."*<Cite s="2026-08-04-john-kiriakou-who-is-really-behind-the-migrant-crisis-in-ce" t="09:18" /> Kiriakou treats the manoeuvre as a demonstration of leverage rather than a policy — Morocco showing Spain *"that it can just mess with Spain whenever it wants to"* — and notes it is not the first time, Morocco having let the same thing happen in 2021.<Cite s="2026-08-04-john-kiriakou-who-is-really-behind-the-migrant-crisis-in-ce" t="14:28" /><Cite s="2026-08-04-john-kiriakou-who-is-really-behind-the-migrant-crisis-in-ce" t="14:59" /> His conclusion is that Spain erred structurally by allowing Morocco to control the flow of migrants into the [enclave](/wiki/spanish-enclaves-in-north-africa) at all.<Cite s="2026-08-04-john-kiriakou-who-is-really-behind-the-migrant-crisis-in-ce" t="14:59" />
