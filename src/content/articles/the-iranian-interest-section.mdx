**The Iranian interest section** is the diplomatic arrangement through which John Kiriakou says he obtained permission to travel to Iran, cited on the DeProgram programme in September 2025 as evidence for a general argument about recognition.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:07:45" />

## The principle

The discussion had turned to Russia's recognition of the Taliban government and whether the United States should follow. Kiriakou's position was that the question is badly framed. *"I don't think diplomatic recognition ... should be like one of those things that is a reward or something."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:07:15" />

The rule he proposes is universal: relations *"with every other country. Period."* His reasoning is functional rather than moral — *"let the diplomats do what they're paid to do, to carry out diplomacy. We should have relations with everybody. We really should. Everybody that wants relations with us."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:07:15" />

## The evidence

His argument is that the contacts happen regardless, and only become more awkward. *"The truth is, when we need to talk to people, we end up talking to them anyway."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:07:45" />

The illustration is his own travel. *"Right when I needed a visa to go to Iran — we don't have diplomatic relations, but Iran had an interest section in the Pakistani embassy in Washington, and that's what my fixer went through to get it."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:07:45" />

An interest section is the standard device by which a state without relations maintains a consular presence inside a third country's mission; Kiriakou treats it as proof that the severance is nominal.

## The conclusion

*"So in other words, the US has to talk to Iran sometimes. They have to. But the lack of diplomatic relations just makes it harder for them to do their jobs."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:07:45" />

## Applied to Afghanistan

Kiriakou extends the same reasoning to a country the United States left entirely. He says Washington *"should have recognised the Taliban immediately"*, and that at a minimum *"we should have an embassy open there, even if it's just two or three people. And we should be carrying out diplomacy constantly."*<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:06:13" /><Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:10:22" />

He grounds the practical case in Americans still in the country after the withdrawal, who in the absence of a mission have no consular representation at all.<Cite s="2025-09-04-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="1:10:22" />
