The **"Soylent Green is people" cable easter egg** is a long-running [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) case-officer in-joke in which the line *"Soylent Green is people"* is hidden inside operational cables as an arrangement of incongruously lowercase letters.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="17:35" />

## Mechanism

CIA cables are, by convention, written exclusively in capital letters — a typographic norm carried forward from the agency's 1950s teletype-era infrastructure that has never been updated. *"It's like some thing from the 50s. They just never changed it."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="17:35" />

This convention makes any lowercase letter visually anomalous. Case officers writing the obligatory blow-by-blow, minute-by-minute account of an operational meeting would scatter individual lowercase letters through the otherwise all-caps text such that the lowercase letters, in sequence, spelled *"Soylent Green is people."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="17:35" />

## Detection

The cable easter egg almost always made it through the CIA's review and distribution system without remark. *"It would always make its way through the system. Nobody would pay any attention, until like the one guy is reading it and he's like, 'Oh, they did it again.'"*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="18:05" />

## Cultural reference

The hidden line refers to the climactic revelation of *Soylent Green*, the 1973 American science-fiction film starring Charlton Heston. In the film, set in an overpopulated near-future where most of the public subsists on government-issued nutritional wafers, Heston's character discovers that the green-colored wafer is manufactured from the bodies of citizens scooped up at protests and at suicide centers. His final, screamed line — *"Soylent Green is people!"* — is the source quoted by the cable easter egg.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="17:03" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="17:35" />

## Why the film stays with him

Kiriakou returns to *Soylent Green* repeatedly as a portrait of a world on the brink rather than as a joke, and treats the single thing it got wrong as instructive about the limits of foresight: in a future imagined complete with floating cars, the policeman at the centre of the story still has to pull over and step into a phone booth to make a call. *"I guess there are just some things people don't see."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="2:33:18" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="2:33:49" />

He pairs the observation with an episode from his own service. On temporary duty at a Middle East station for a week or so, he watched a technical team from headquarters install the station's first email programme, gathering everyone around a single computer to demonstrate that one could type a colleague's name, hit refresh, and write to them. The station chief's reaction was to grasp the implication and reject it: *"Wait a minute. Are you saying that my people can have access to anybody they want? … I don't like it. I don't like it one bit"* — and he decreed there would be no email in his station.<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="2:34:19" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="2:34:49" /> Kiriakou offers it not as stupidity but as the same blindness the film displays: *"Sometimes you just don't see it. Your brain can't comprehend it."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="2:34:49" />
