Kiriakou's Athens house
=======================

Kiriakou's Athens house was the home John Kiriakou found for himself in 1998 after refusing the Nordeen house and giving up on the embassy housing office.

It stood in Kifissia, a northern suburb halfway up the mountain and therefore about ten degrees cooler than central Athens, a city whose weather he compares to Charlotte, North Carolina: mild winters, scorching summers, snow unusual and never much.

He liked the architecture: five bedrooms on five levels, each level small, the house very tall and modern, and no room square, all odd perpendicular shapes stacked on one another. Every room had a balcony, and every balcony faced a different direction.

The garden

The side yard was small but held five fruit trees, all bearing: an olive he estimates at two hundred years old, pears, plums, oranges and lemons.

A colleague explained the local arrangement for olives: an olive company will either harvest for a fee and press or brine the crop for you, or take the whole lot and give you back a quarter of what they press. Kiriakou took the second deal, and the family cooked with oil from their own tree for the whole two years. "I still think about that olive tree."

The plums, he says, were the best he has ever eaten, small as shooter marbles, with a tiny stone. Alone in the house one day at peak season he ate, by his own estimate and without exaggeration, two hundred of them. He shared them with an attic tortoise that lived in the garden and liked them as much as he did: "I'd have to like wrestle this guy for the plums."

What the house meant

The house also carried the marriage. Kiriakou's wife, who he says wanted to be nowhere except Ohio, was thrilled by it, walking distance from restaurants, the grocer's and the zaharoplastio pastry shop, and a ten-minute drive from two of her uncles. It sits in his telling as the last comfortable ground before the posting turned: "I can see how so many of the things I really liked about my job were the very same things that were slowly undermining my marriage."
