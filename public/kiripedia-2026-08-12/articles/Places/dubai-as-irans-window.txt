Dubai as Iran's window
======================

Dubai as Iran's window is John Kiriakou's answer to a viewer who asked whether hitting Dubai hard would benefit Iran, given how much American interest was concentrated there during the 2026 war.

Kiriakou's answer was negative and immediate. "I think no and I'll tell you why because that's where Iran does its business."

The function of the city

The reason he gives is economic dependence rather than sentiment. "Iran I mean Dubai is chock-full of Iranian businessmen and that's Iran's outlet to the western world. That's how Iran evades sanctions by having businesses in Dubai. So I think no. Hitting Dubai is not going to help Iran at all."

On this account Dubai is not a target that Iran is restrained from hitting by fear of retaliation, but an asset Iran holds inside the opposing coalition, the place through which a sanctioned economy reaches the markets that sanction it.

What was hit anyway

Kiriakou's own inventory of the damage Iran managed to inflict includes Dubai twice over. Iranian forces "fired rockets at the Jebel Ali the the the free port in Dubai," damaged the airports at Manama and Kuwait, fired a rocket at the Saudi oil fields that was intercepted, and damaged a couple of hotels in Dubai.

His general assessment of that campaign is that it was slight. Asked whether Iran was causing damage anywhere, his answer was "Not that I've seen", and "there's some damage but absolutely nothing like what's being inflicted on Iran."

The distinction he draws is between the hotels, struck to frighten tourists and empty the Gulf economies, and the city's commercial machinery, which Iran needs working. The strikes on the Gulf hotels are, in his reading, calibrated to damage a neighbour's economy without severing Iran's own lifeline.

The wider judgement

The restraint fits Kiriakou's overall reading of Iranian strategy in the war. Iran is not trying to win: "all they have to do is survive." An economy that has to outlast a bombing campaign cannot afford to destroy the one port through which it still trades.
