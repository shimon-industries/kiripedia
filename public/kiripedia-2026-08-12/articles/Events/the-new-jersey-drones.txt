The New Jersey drones
=====================

Asked what he made of the drone sightings over New Jersey, John Kiriakou gave an answer that moved through three positions and settled on none of them.

The first assumption

At first, he said, he simply assumed it was a Defense Department exercise of some sort.

The trooper's account

Then came a friend's report. Kiriakou has a friend who is a New Jersey state trooper, and who told him that the police were working under the impression that a Department of Energy or Department of Defense cargo had gone missing, "that perhaps it was nuclear, perhaps it was maybe a weapon of some sort or a missile," and that the convoy had lost the eighteen-wheeler carrying it.

Kiriakou is careful about the standing of this. "I don't know," he said. "He said that's what he thought it was. That's what he had been told it was."

The generals

The third account came from a 60 Minutes segment he had watched shortly before the interview, in which the current and former commanding generals of an Air Force base, Langley, he believed, were interviewed. Their explanation for the absence of any response was technical: people think the objects can simply be shot down, but "they're so small that you can't use a fighter jet to shoot something that is so small and is guided by hand."

Their conclusion, as he reports it, was that the drones were hostile and "likely Chinese." He notes that a Chinese ship was off the coast of New Jersey, at least for a while, during the period the objects were flying.

The gap in the defences

The line he quotes from the generals is the one that stayed with him: that the United States has to come up with a better system to protect itself. "We can protect ourselves against incoming ICBMs, for example," he said. "But we can't protect ourselves against drones."

His host's reply, that the same thing has been happening worldwide, and at the same time of year over several years, is left unanswered.
