The George Mason confrontation
==============================

The George Mason confrontation was a videotaped exchange between former CIA director John Brennan and a questioner at George Mason University, raised with John Kiriakou by a viewer on 5 November 2025. Kiriakou treated the clip less as a story about bad manners than as a diagnostic reading of a man he has known for most of his adult life.

What he was asked, and what he saw

The question came from the audience: "what's my reaction to John Brennan's interaction with Thomas Bial at George Mason University on the 30th?" Kiriakou answered that the video had reached him many times over. "I'm not exaggerating when I tell you at least a dozen friends of mine sent me the video, and I watched it at least a half a dozen times."

He described what was on it plainly, with a legal aside he immediately set down again: Brennan "physically poking him in the chest, which by the way is assault, but that's beside the point."

Thirty-five years of acquaintance

The point, for Kiriakou, was the expression rather than the gesture. "I've known John Brennan for 35 years. That's the first time I've ever seen him scared."

He read the fear as anticipatory rather than situational, not fear of the man in front of him, but of what was coming. "I think he's genuinely scared of what's going to happen to him. You know, the drain is open and I think the water is starting to circle." His summary was four words long: "I'm glad I'm not John Brennan today."

Why the reading carries weight

Kiriakou's assessments of Brennan are not neutral, and he does not present them as such; he has elsewhere described Brennan as central to the CIA torture program he himself went to prison over. What distinguishes this account is its narrowness. He does not claim to know what Brennan is afraid of, does not name a prosecution or an investigation, and offers no prediction beyond the metaphor of a draining sink. The claim is only that a man he has watched for thirty-five years looked, on this occasion, unlike himself.

The surname of the other party is given here as the auto-captioned transcript renders it; Kiriakou repeats it only once, in reading back the viewer's question.
