China's generals without combat experience
==========================================

China's generals without combat experience is a short assessment John Kiriakou gave on 26 January 2026, after the host raised President Xi's ousting of a number of senior Chinese commanders.

What was put to him

The observation was not Kiriakou's. The host said he had taken note of a detail in the coverage, that the Chinese top military leadership now has no combat experience at all, being too young to have fought in the revolution, and announced he intended to deploy it the next time someone described China as a military threat: "they haven't done a war in so long that they don't have any generals who have actual firsthand experience."

He also relayed a reading of the purge itself, drawn from a Chinese army newspaper editorial: that the officers on the outs were judged insufficiently deferential to the president, and that what was being selected for was personal rather than institutional loyalty.

Kiriakou's answer

Asked how important the story was, Kiriakou split the timeframe. "I think it's less important in the near term, especially here in the United States. I think it's much more important over the long term, for the reasons that you cite."

The long-term significance he identifies is operational. "When it comes to planning, military planning, we're going to have to take into consideration that there are no senior generals with combat experience. That's very important."

What the answer does and does not claim

Kiriakou does not say the deficiency makes China weak, and he does not endorse the host's intended use of the fact as a rebuttal to threat rhetoric. His point is narrower and cuts both ways: a factor that a planner must model, because an adversary whose commanders have never been shot at is an adversary whose behaviour under fire is unknown rather than known to be poor.

The passage is characteristic of how he handles claims he did not originate. He accepts the factual premise as reported, declines to inflate it, and converts it into the only currency he treats as reliable, what a planning staff would have to write down.
