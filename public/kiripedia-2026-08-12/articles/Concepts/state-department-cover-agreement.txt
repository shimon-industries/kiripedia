The CIA–State Department cover agreement
========================================

The cover agreement between the CIA and the State Department is the standing arrangement under which agency officers serve abroad in declared diplomatic positions. John Kiriakou has described an allegation that the agency used it, after 2001, for a purpose it was not written for.

The allegation

While serving as a senior investigator on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, Kiriakou was approached by a journalist who said he had evidence that the CIA "was misusing its cover agreement with the State Department to place people involved in the torture program under State Department cover so that their names could not be exposed in the press." The protective effect was legal rather than practical: if such a name were published, whoever supplied it would be exposed to the Intelligence Identities Protection Act, the same statute under which Kiriakou himself was later convicted.

The letter, and the answer he was not allowed to read

Kiriakou''s reading was that this amounted to a violation of the cover agreement itself. He sent the agency a request for clarification under the signature of the committee''s then chairman, Senator John Kerry. A response arrived roughly six weeks later, classified Top Secret, a level above the clearance Kiriakou held at the time, so that the answer to his own question was withheld from him. A colleague who could read it summarised it for him. In Kiriakou''s paraphrase the letter said, in very strongly worded language, "mind your own business."
