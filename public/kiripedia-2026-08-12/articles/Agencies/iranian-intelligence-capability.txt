Iranian intelligence capability
===============================

Iranian intelligence capability is assessed by John Kiriakou in blunt and unqualified terms. Asked how good Iranian intelligence was, his answer was that it "is not good. It's not at all good. It's never been good."

The explanation offered

Kiriakou attributes the weakness to training rather than to manpower, money or motivation. "It's because they don't get good training," he says. The two states that might supply that training do so only marginally: "The Russians will help them out a little bit. The Chinese will help them out a little bit. But essentially, they're on their own."

The assessment is offered in the context of the 2026 war, in answer to a question about whether Iranian strikes on hotels in the Gulf were precision attacks driven by actionable intelligence. Kiriakou's answer was that they were not: the poor quality of the collection is part of why the targeting is what it is.

The tension with capability elsewhere

Kiriakou's low estimate of Iranian tradecraft coexists in the same session with a high estimate of Iranian reach. He describes the IRGC as running pre-operational planning probes against American embassies worldwide, a practice he had encountered personally on his first day in operations, and he describes both the IRGC and the Ministry of Intelligence and Security as maintaining sleeper cells in Pakistan, Afghanistan, Thailand, Indonesia and Argentina.

The two judgements are not presented as inconsistent. In Kiriakou's framing, the Iranians are persistent and geographically extended but technically unimpressive, capable of casing a building and of keeping people in place for years, but not of the collection that would let them strike a named individual.

Consequence for targeting

The practical consequence Kiriakou draws is that Iranian strikes should be read as economic rather than personal. Because the intelligence is not there to do anything else, "these are not targeted attacks against individuals. They're random attacks just trying to do as much damage as possible", with the damage chosen to fall on the tourist and business economies of the Gulf.
