Why he believes Epstein killed himself
======================================

Interviewing an investigative journalist who has followed the Jeffrey Epstein case since its beginning and who suspects a criminal conspiracy behind Epstein's death, John Kiriakou volunteered the opposite conclusion, and prefaced it as such.

The statement

"I was going to say I believe he committed suicide," he said, "only because having done prison time myself, I can tell you that most of the prison guards have their heads up their asses."

He returned to it after laying out his account of how federal officers actually work a shift, the missed rounds, the guard booth, the cameras that are down half the time. "And so I do believe he committed suicide," he said. "I believe that it was unfortunate for a whole bunch of reasons, but I believe it was a suicide."

The structure of the argument

What is notable about the reasoning is what it does not rely on. Kiriakou offers no reading of the autopsy, no view on the cell assignment, and no assessment of who might have wanted Epstein dead. His argument is entirely negative: the anomalies that others treat as evidence of intervention are, in his experience, the normal condition of a federal jail.

He applies the same reasoning to the guards' own accounts. He does not accuse them of complicity; he describes them as people who were probably asleep, working a job with an entry requirement he puts at a high school diploma and no felony record.

Where it sits against his other views

The position is not a defence of the government's handling of the case. In the same conversation Kiriakou argued that the Trump administration was breaking the law in its partial release of the Epstein files, that the grand jury minutes had been blacked out in their entirety without justification, and that the transfer of Ghislaine Maxwell to a minimum-security work camp could only have been a personal favour requiring the attorney general's order.

His guest's counter-argument, that Epstein's usefulness as a blackmailer collapsed once everyone knew he was one, and that a comparable figure in an earlier scandal had been given an offer he could not refuse, is left standing beside Kiriakou's view rather than answered. Neither man persuades the other.
